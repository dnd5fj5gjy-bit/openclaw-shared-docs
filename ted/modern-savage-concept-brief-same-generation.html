Why the reference worked

03

Making it premium

The elevation isn't fancier graphics, and it isn't slowing down. Both halves stay swift and high-octane like the reference. The premium comes from the two halves speaking opposite visual languages, grade, framing and sound, so the contrast carries the message even at pace.

Same Generation — engineered

  • Cold, clinical grade. Oversaturated artificial colour.
  • Claustrophobic crops, screens, glare, crowds.
  • Frantic cutting, jump cuts, motion, noise.
  • Sound: layered, loud, synthetic, airless.

Bear — alive

  • Warm, natural, filmic grade. Real light.
  • His most gripping footage: stunts, feats, wild-eating.
  • Same swift ~1s pace, high drama, momentum carries through.
  • Sound: natural and clean, wind, water, breath.

Typography

Replace the reference's plain sans labels with restrained brand type (Archivo), small and confident, never meme-y. The label is a whisper, not a shout.

Music & sound design

Scored, not just a trending audio. Build the frenzy, drop it hard on the transition, open into the wild, climb to the payoff. Cleared for commercial use.

07

Guardrails

Please hold these.

  1. No identifiable competitor brands. No competitor logos, named packaging or real storefronts in the "Same Generation" half. In a paid brand ad that's legal and brand exposure. Use generic / unbranded crowds and packaging, or blur marks. Confirm with Olly before anything branded goes in.
  2. Calibrate the wild-eating. Primal, impressive and ancestral, tied to organs since that's the product bridge. Not gratuitous gross-out. This is a premium family brand, so it's strength, not shock value.
  3. BG voice. If we add any Bear voiceover, use the new improved BG AI voice, not the old one. Otherwise music and sound design carry it, no VO.
  4. No health claims. Strength and nourishment framing only. Nothing that says the product boosts, treats, fixes or prevents anything.
  5. Keep the payoff qualitative. "Grass-fed organs, real berries, whole-food vitamins." No calories, protein grams or macros for our formula, on screen or in the caption. Those aren't signed off yet.
