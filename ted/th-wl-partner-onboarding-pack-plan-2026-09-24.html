01 · In one read

Key recommendations

The August activation review found most live partners had gone live without telling their own patients the service exists. This pack gives them the material to do that, without letting unapproved clinical claims or medicine names into public view.

Build Blood Testing first, then TRT, then ED, and Weight Loss last. Every clinic already has the full blood test range and the Testosterone Blood Test and Wellman add-ons, TRT and ED are on by default, and weight loss is switched off for every real partner until its contract is signed (Master Reference s5, s12). Lowest risk and widest use go first.

Give partners finished files in their own brand, produced by Ku Creatives, not editable Canva templates. Only six fields change per partner (logo, colours, name, location, tracked link or QR, and their own live price). What Dr Andrews approves is exactly what gets published.

Get the approved phrase list signed off before any design work starts. Ted drafts a phrase bank of approved claims, disclosures and calls to action; Dr Andrews signs it off once. Every later asset is built only from it, so each approval round is a quick check against the bank rather than a fresh review.

Two layers. A Launch Kit handed over at go-live, for the pathways switched on for that partner only, then a monthly Always-On drop of new social posts and blog articles to keep partners posting.

Every asset carries a tracked link or QR per partner, per pathway, per asset. We measure patients entering each pathway, by partner. We do not measure prescriptions issued, so clinical decisions are never tied to a marketing target (clause 2.5).

Retire the 10/08/2026 Partner Promotion Pack now. It uses a generic "from £40" price, lists weight loss, which no real partner can offer yet, and includes a "30-day results guarantee". The Master Reference rules out the first two (s11 rule 4 and s12), and the brand guidelines rule out the third.

Patient-facing assets carry the partner's brand. 05 · Compliance

Rules, approval gate and enforcement

Advertising a prescription-only medicine (POM) to the public is prohibited under the Human Medicines Regulations 2012 (Part 14) and CAP Code rule 12.12, with MHRA guidance in the Blue Guide. The ASA has actively ruled against weight-loss POM adverts since late 2024, and TRT and ED adverts face the same scrutiny. Partners are bound by clauses 7.3 to 7.5, and "misleading advertising" and "repeated brand-guideline breaches" are grounds for immediate suspension (clause 17.1).
