The diagnosis

Onboarding worked. Activation didn't follow.

Every live partner is technically capable of sending a patient through today. Only Ojay Health (Ollie Matthews) has actually done it — one live TRT+ED patient, payment split confirmed working. The other six aren't stuck on the platform. They're stuck on the step nobody's chasing them on: telling their own audience it exists.

Onboarding is a project with a deadline (get the subdomain live, get the branding right, get the account verified). Getting the first patient has no deadline and no one chasing it — so for six of seven partners, the platform is sat there un-promoted. That's the entire strategy below: turn "go live" from the finish line into the actual starting gun, partner by partner.

The systemic fix

What actually stops this happening to partner eight, nine, ten

The partner-by-partner nudges fix today's backlog. These fix the pattern.

1. A standing 30/60/90-day check-in — currently doesn't exist

Every partner above went live and then the relationship went quiet until Olly happened to notice. Build a simple standing cadence: 30 days ("are you live and have you told anyone yet"), 60 days ("here's what worked for Ojay, want the same kit"), 90 days ("let's look at numbers"). Doesn't need to be fancy — a recurring reminder against each partner's go-live date is enough to stop the current drift.

2. A ready-made "announce it" kit, given to every partner at go-live, not left for them to invent

By Firdous already proved partners will self-promote if it occurs to them — most just never get asked or given anything to post. Ted can turn the go-live moment into a small fixed kit every time: one co-branded social post, one email-to-existing-patients template, one line for in-person mention. Handed over automatically at go-live rather than offered reactively.

3. Fix the Ryft/KYC bottleneck at the process level

Ian's utility-bill rejection has now sat unresolved for weeks with no clear owner chasing Ryft. Worth Thomas confirming what the actual resolution path is (resubmission format, whose job it is to escalate to Ryft) so this doesn't quietly repeat for the next payment-verification case.

4. Patient-count visibility, not just onboarding-stage visibility

The internal reporting Ted's seen tracks onboarding stage well but not patient activity per partner — that's exactly why six live partners can go unnoticed for weeks. A simple per-partner "patients to date" line, even manually updated, would make the nudge list obvious at a glance instead of needing a full mailbox read to reconstruct it.
