01

The idea

Keep the split-frame "then / now" device the editors built, it's strong, it just needs somewhere to land. Each comparison points at something that's actually in the scoop: the vegetables set up the real berries and whole-food vitamins, the grass-fed beef sets up the grass-fed organs.

Two comparison beats give the piece rhythm and get the organ story in, which is the thing we're really selling. Then the turn, then the answer.

03

Type & style

Match the existing series: white text over footage, serif italic on the emphasis words, split frame on the comparison slides (2 and 3), single frame on 1, 4 and 5.

Flag for Olly: the serif italic isn't in the Modern Savage web design system (which uses Archivo, uppercase, for display). Fine to keep for social consistency, but worth a decision at some point on whether social and web should share a type identity.
04

Caption

Your grandparents didn't take supplements. The food did the work.

It carried what the soil, the seed and the animal put into it. Somewhere along the way, we started growing for size and speed instead of what's inside. The vegetables look the same. They just carry less.

That's the whole reason we built Modern Savage. Grass-fed organs, real berries, whole-food vitamins. The plate our grandparents had, in one scoop.

Real food. Real strength. Nothing hidden.

05

Compliance

Please hold these.

  1. Keep the payoff qualitative. Slide 5 stays "grass-fed organs, real berries, whole-food vitamins". No calories, protein grams or any macro figures for our formula, on screen or in the caption. Those aren't signed off yet.
  2. No health claims. Strength and nourishment framing only. Nothing that says the product boosts, treats, fixes or prevents anything.
  3. The one hard stat is sourced. "16% less calcium, 15% less iron" comes from Davis et al (2004), Changes in USDA Food Composition Data for 43 Garden Crops, 1950 to 1999, J Am Coll Nutr. The study attributes the decline to crop varieties bred for yield and size, which is why slide 2's bottom image must show big / uniform produce rather than spraying. Keep image and claim aligned.
