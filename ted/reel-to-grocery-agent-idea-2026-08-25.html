Idea Doc

Reel-to-Cart: send a recipe video, get the shop delivered

Sourced from Olly's voice note, 24 Aug 2026. Written up so it's buildable, not just an idea.

What it is

A bot/app that turns a recipe reel into an actual grocery order

Core unlock

No new app to learn — you just forward a video

Status

Adjacent products exist; nobody owns this exact loop end-to-end

The core idea

Someone sees a recipe reel on Instagram or TikTok. Instead of screenshotting it, half-remembering it, and doing a normal shop, they forward the reel to a bot. The bot watches the video, works out the recipe and exact ingredients, and turns that straight into a shopping basket — with Tesco, M&S, or whichever retailer the person shops with — ready to check out.

The whole point is that there's no new app to download and no new habit to learn. The interface is "send a video to a chat," which is something people already do every day.

How it would actually work

1

Ingest. User forwards a reel (or a screenshot) to the bot via WhatsApp/Telegram/iMessage.

2

Recipe extraction. Video + audio + on-screen text is run through a vision-language model to pull out the dish and its ingredient list with quantities — this is the same class of problem Ted already solves when transcribing and structuring voice notes.

3

Basket build. Ingredients are mapped to real SKUs on the user's chosen retailer (Tesco, Ocado, M&S Food) via their existing grocery API/partner integration, at the right pack size.

4

Confirm and send. Bot replies: "Want the rest of this recipe? Reply yes and it's in your basket for delivery." One tap to confirm, no manual re-typing of a shopping list.

5

Memory layer. The bot remembers what a household has bought before and how fast they use it. "You got oregano last week — still got it, or need more?" Only asks about what's actually likely to have run out, so the basket gets leaner and more accurate every time it's used.

The second idea inside this — budget mode

Olly raised a related but distinct use case: sending a photo of a weekly shop or a meal-plan reel and having the bot find the cheapest way to build the same meals without overbuying ingredients. This is really a budgeting layer on top of the same core engine — same ingredient-extraction pipeline, but optimising for "cheapest valid substitution + minimum waste" instead of "match this exact recipe."

Worth building as a mode, not a separate product: same input (reel/photo), same extraction step, different basket-building logic (cost-minimise vs. recipe-match).

Why this could work

Zero-friction input

People already save and forward recipe reels constantly. This meets that existing behaviour instead of asking for a new one.

Retailer leverage, not competition

The product doesn't need to become a grocer — it becomes a front door to Tesco/Ocado/M&S. That's a partnership pitch, not a build-a-warehouse problem. Retailers may want to acquire or white-label it rather than compete with it once it has volume.

Compounding memory

Every order makes the next one better — pantry state, portion sizes, preferred brands. That's a real moat once a household has used it a few times; a new entrant starts from zero.

Competitive reality check

Pieces of this exist already, which is why Olly flagged "I think it's been done." Worth being precise about what's actually taken and what isn't:

  • Recipe → ingredient extraction from video/image: partially solved (Whisk/Samsung Food, SuperCook, various TikTok-recipe-scraper tools) but generally as a standalone app, not a forward-a-reel-to-a-bot flow.
  • Ingredient list → grocery basket: exists inside single ecosystems (Whisk plugs into some US retailers; some UK meal-kit apps do this for their own recipes) but not generalised across arbitrary social content.
  • Pantry memory / re-order prediction: exists in isolation (Amazon Dash-style subscribe-and-save, some grocery apps' "buy again") but not tied to recipe-driven ordering.

The whitespace isn't any single piece — it's stitching "arbitrary social video in" straight through to "confirmed basket at your usual retailer out," with memory compounding on top. Worth a proper competitor scan (Whisk, SuperCook, Mealime, ChefGPt-style apps, and any UK-specific reel-to-recipe tools) before committing build time, to confirm nobody's already closed this exact loop.

What it would take to validate

  • Confirm UK grocery retailers with an API or partner programme for basket-building (Tesco, Ocado, M&S) — this is the hard technical/commercial dependency, not the AI part.
  • Build a rough proof-of-concept: reel in, ingredient list out, no basket integration yet. Cheap to test whether extraction is reliable enough on real Instagram/TikTok recipe content.
  • Test with a small group manually completing the basket step, to validate people actually want this before building retailer integrations.
  • Decide the wedge: full recipe-to-cart first, or budget/waste-minimisation mode first — they share infrastructure but have different early adopters (time-poor cooks vs. budget-conscious shoppers).
