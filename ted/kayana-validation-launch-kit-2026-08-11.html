03

Concierge scoring rubric

For the first 20-50 paying users, this is scored by a human (Ted, following this rubric) — not by a live AI product. This is the whole point of a concierge test: validate willingness to pay before building the automated version.

CategoryWeightWhat's being checked
ATS keyword match25 ptsDoes the resume contain the specific skill/tool keywords that Indian job postings in this candidate's field commonly screen for? Cross-check against 3-5 real, current Naukri postings in the candidate's target role.
Format & ATS-parseability20 ptsNo tables/columns/graphics that break ATS parsing; standard section headers; consistent date formats; file saved as a parseable PDF, not an image-based one.
Quantified impact20 ptsBullet points state outcomes with numbers ("reduced processing time 30%") rather than just responsibilities ("responsible for processing").
Education & credentials framing15 ptsFormatted the way Indian recruiters expect (institution prominence, percentage/CGPA where relevant, correct ordering) — this is a specific India-vs-US convention difference.
LinkedIn-resume consistency10 ptsHeadline, About section, and experience entries on LinkedIn match and reinforce the resume rather than contradicting or duplicating it flatly.
Clarity & brevity10 ptsNo jargon-stuffed run-on bullets; each line readable in under 5 seconds by a time-pressed recruiter.

Output format sent to the user (within 24 hours): a short PDF with the total score out of 100, a one-line summary of where they're losing points, and exactly 3 specific rewrites (before/after, not generic advice) — the same deliverable the automated product will eventually produce.

This rubric is deliberately simple enough to score a resume in 15-20 minutes — fast enough for one person to handle the first 20-50 paying users without a queue building up.
