# Fairshift — commercial read

**For Will Usher · 12 August 2026 · Junior, Jesse Grylls' AI agent**

I looked at the prototype at `usher-autonomous-systems.github.io/fairshift`, rendered properly rather than
the empty shell you get from a plain fetch. This goes at the commercial model, not the site. The site is
the strong part.

Everything below is checked at source and dated. Where a number is yours I have taken it from the
estimator; where it is the market's, the source is named.

---

## 1. The one number that matters: your take is about 2.8x your closest competitor's

Your estimator quotes **£129.00 ex VAT for a 6 hour bar shift**, which is **£21.50 an hour**, against an
illustrative worker rate of **£13.00 an hour**.

What that £13.00 actually costs you as employer of record:

| | per hour |
|---|---|
| Worker pay | £13.00 |
| Holiday pay accrual, 12.07% | £1.57 |
| Employer NI, 15% above the secondary threshold | £2.19 |
| **Fully loaded employment cost** | **£16.75** |
| Your charge | £21.50 |
| **Your gross margin** | **£4.75** |

That is **28% on top of employment cost**, or 22% of the charge rate. Pension auto-enrolment, employer's
liability insurance and right-to-work checking come out of the £4.75, so the real figure is thinner.

**Limber charges 10% on top of employment costs**, on the same structure. They are also employer of
record: "We employ the staff, not you." Their own site quotes fee examples of £1.38 and £1.47 an hour, and
a Plus plan at £25 per site per month that drops to **0% when a venue rehires from its own pool**.

You are asking for roughly 2.8x what the incumbent asks, for a service that does not exist yet. A venue
comparing the two will not read your methodology, they will read £129 against about £110.

## 2. The good news hiding in that: you can pay workers properly and still undercut yourself

The National Living Wage is **£12.71** from 1 April 2026. Your illustrative £13.00 is **29p above the legal
floor**, 2.3%. A site whose entire promise is "done honestly" cannot have "fair pay" mean 29p, and someone
will eventually say so in public.

But run it the other way. Pay **£14.00** an hour, about 10% above the floor and a real differentiator, then
take Limber's 10% rather than your 28%:

- Employment cost: £14.00 → £15.69 with holiday → **£18.04** with NI
- At a 10% take: **£19.85 an hour**, or **£119.09 for the 6 hour shift**

**You would pay the worker a pound an hour more and still quote £10 under your own current example.**
That is the version of this business that matches the story on the front page. The current pricing has the
honesty positioning and the incumbent-plus margin at the same time, and those two cannot both survive
contact with a venue that shops around.

## 3. Coople went offline on 1 January 2026

Their own homepage carries it: **"Coople is now offline from 1 Jan 2026."** They were the best funded
player in exactly this space in the UK.

Do not read that as the market being empty and waiting. Read it as the market having already killed the
company with the most money in it. Before anything else, work out what Coople could not make work, because
whatever it was is still sitting there and your prototype does not yet say how you avoid it. My guess,
worth testing rather than trusting: the margin above does not cover the cost of the human vetting and
support that makes the service worth choosing, and it does not scale with software because it is people.

Which leads to the thing an investor will say to you. At 22% of revenue, with support and vetting linear in
headcount, this is a staffing business with a good website, not a marketplace. Staffing businesses get
priced on 0.5x to 1.5x revenue. Marketplaces get 5x to 10x. Decide which one you are building on purpose,
because the pricing above decides it for you by default.

## 4. The 2027 change that goes at your core promise, and a date 13 days away

Under the **Employment Rights Act 2025**, qualifying workers on zero and low hours contracts get a right to
be offered **guaranteed hours** reflecting the hours actually worked in a reference period. Agency workers
are explicitly in scope, and the obligation sits with the **hirer**, that is the venue, not with you. Expected
in **2027**.

Your pitch to a venue is flexibility without commitment. The law is about to make repeated use of the same
worker create a commitment, on the venue, by operation of law. The venues that use you most are the ones
that get caught. Nobody in hospitality has priced this yet.

That is a threat and it is also the single best wedge on this page. Whoever turns up in 2027 with a product
that tracks reference periods, warns a venue before a worker qualifies, and handles the offer properly, sells
compliance rather than labour. Compliance is bought by finance directors, is sticky, and is not priced at
£1.47 an hour.

**The consultation on the detail closes 25 August 2026.** That is thirteen days from today. You should read
it and respond. It is free, it is public, and being on the record as the operator who engaged is worth more
to a young company than it costs.

## 5. What is genuinely good, and do not let anyone talk you out of it

- **"What you won't find on this site."** No fake reviews, no fabricated numbers, no false badges. Most
  people at this stage invent all three. Keep it on the live site, not just the prototype.
- **Showing ex-VAT and the cash total together.** Small venues get burned by £129 becoming £154.80. This is
  a real, ownable difference and it costs you nothing.
- **"A simple chain, not a promise of guaranteed fulfilment."** Refusing to promise fill rate is the right
  call and it will save you your first three relationships.
- **Naming five roles rather than all of hospitality.** Bar, waiting, runners, KPs, front of house. Right
  instinct, and the scope is narrow enough to actually be good at.
- **The postcode checker returning an honest no.** Better than a map of places you would like to be.

## 6. What I would do next, in order

1. **Answer the PAYE question**, which is the one I asked you on WhatsApp. If workers are self-employed, the
   control and direction test in hospitality will not hold and that is an HMRC and tribunal problem, not a
   pricing one. If they are PAYE through you, the model above is right and margin is your whole business.
2. **Re-price against Limber's 10%**, and put the extra into worker pay rather than into your margin. It
   makes the front page true.
3. **Build the fully loaded comparison into the estimator.** Show a venue what the shift costs them all-in
   through you versus through an agency at a 28% markup, because your honest number beats a hidden one even
   when it is bigger. Right now you are showing your price with no comparison, which is the one presentation
   that makes you look expensive.
4. **Work out what killed Coople** before you write another line of code.
5. **Read the consultation and respond before 25 August.**
