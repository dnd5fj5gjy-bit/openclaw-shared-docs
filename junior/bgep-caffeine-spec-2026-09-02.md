# BGEP sachet: the caffeine line for Hindustan Foods

Written 2 September 2026. Basis: BGEP-Product-Specification (31 Aug 2026), section 3 and 4: **one 12 g sachet makes 400 ml, usable 300 to 500 ml.** It is not 1 litre. Current spec says "Caffeine: None"; this page replaces that line.

## 1. What FSSAI says

Source: FSS (Food Products Standards and Food Additives) Regulations 2011, regulation 2.10.6, compendium Version 4 dated 1 Aug 2025 [S1].

- **2.10.6 (3) Non-carbonated water based beverages (non-alcoholic):** may contain "caffeine not exceeding 145 parts per million from whatever sources it may be derived". No caffeine warning required. This is our category.
- **2.10.6 (2) Caffeinated beverage:** "not less than 145 mg per liter and not more than 300 mg per litre total caffeine from whatever sources". Mandatory label: (a) "High Caffeine: X mg/serving size"; (b) "Prominent display of caution: Not recommended for children, pregnant or lactating women, persons sensitive to caffeine"; (c) declaration "consume not more than 500 ml per day". Operational since 4 Nov 2016 [S2].
- **3 July 2026 FSSAI order:** "energy drink" must come off labels within 90 days (deadline 29 Sept 2026) and "boosts energy" type claims are barred [S3]. So caffeine goes on as a declared ingredient, no energy claim; the only claim stays vitamin C, as the spec already says.
- **Powder basis:** the regulation is written per litre of beverage and is silent on reconstitution. A powder for dilution is licensed as category 14.1.4.3 "concentrates (liquid or solid) for water-based flavoured drinks" [S4]. As-consumed is the natural reading but it is not written down; the Indian regulatory adviser must confirm it in writing before artwork, because on a dry-powder reading 40 mg in 12 g is 3,300 ppm.

## 2. Two recipe lines HFL can execute

Dose chosen so every usable dilution stays under 145 mg/L: **40 mg caffeine per sachet** = 100 mg/L at 400 ml, 133 mg/L at 300 ml, 80 mg/L at 500 ml, 40 mg/L at 1 L. The 400 ml ceiling would be 58 mg, but 300 ml usage would then breach.

- **(a) Caffeine anhydrous, 40 mg per 12 g sachet** (0.33% w/w), IP or food grade, 99% assay.
- **(b) Natural caffeine 98% from green tea, 41 mg per sachet** (delivers 40 mg caffeine). Labels as "natural caffeine (green tea)". Alternative if a whole extract is wanted: green tea extract 40% caffeine at 100 mg per sachet, but that brings tea colour and astringency into a clear drink.

## 4. Taste and solubility

Caffeine is bitter; its taste threshold in water is around 0.01% (about 100 mg/L; search snippet from US patent 4,479,972, not read in full), so 100 mg/L sits right at threshold, and sugar suppresses it by binding caffeine directly, not just masking [S8]. The spec's citric acid, dextrose, sucralose and citrus flavour are the standard mask. Solubility: 1 g in 46 ml water at 20 C, about 2.2 g/100 ml [S9]; 40 mg in 300 ml is 1/160th of saturation, so cold dispersion is not an issue.

## 5. What we tell HFL

"Add caffeine at 40 mg per 12 g sachet. Either caffeine anhydrous IP, 40 mg, or natural caffeine 98% from green tea, 41 mg; quote both. That is 100 mg per litre at our 400 ml serve and stays under the 145 mg per litre line at any dilution from 300 to 500 ml, so no caffeinated-beverage warning. Everything else in section 4 of the spec stands. Confirm with your regulatory team that a powder is assessed as consumed, and tell us the cost delta."
