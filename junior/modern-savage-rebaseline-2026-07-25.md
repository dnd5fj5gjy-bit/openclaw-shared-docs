## 1. The arithmetic

The 21 July chain, at its fast end, runs 46 days from artwork-at-Belmark to cart-open: prepress and print 15 working days, 3 days to Miami, 5 working days to fill and pack, freight 7 days, then 3PL receiving and a test order.

Hold that chain constant and slide only the artwork date:

| Artwork reaches Belmark | Cart opens | Against HOS, Sat 12 Sep |
|---|---|---|
| Fri 24 Jul (the plan) | Tue 8 Sep | 4 days clear. Missed. |
| **Mon 27 Jul** | **Fri 11 Sep** | **1 day clear. Survivable.** |
| Wed 29 Jul | Sun 13 Sep | 1 day late. Moment lost. |
| Fri 31 Jul | Tue 15 Sep | 3 days late. |
| Mon 3 Aug | Fri 18 Sep | 6 days late. |

Monday is the last date that puts product in hand before Bear is on stage. It also assumes every single lead time lands at its fast end with no slippage anywhere in a chain of six handoffs, which has not happened once on this project so far.

This matters more than the artwork date. **If the real number is 20 working days rather than 15, then even the perfect 24 July scenario lands the cart on 13 September and 12 September was already lost before last Friday.** We would have been running a plan whose central date was wrong for two weeks without knowing.

Confirming that number is one line to Sarah Salscheider, who answers everything, usually same day. It is a higher-value question than the artwork chase because it tells us whether the thing we are chasing is still worth chasing.

## 5. What to do if Monday fails

Do not let this decision arrive by default in late August. The 21 July plan set the pre-order contingency decision for 14 August. **Move it to Tuesday 28 July**, because by then we will know whether Monday's call landed, and every day after that spent pretending 12 September is alive costs marketing time that the pre-order route needs.

The pre-order version, in short: cart opens 8 September as planned, sells founding-member pre-orders, ships October. It protects the HOS moment, which is the actual irreplaceable asset here. Bear on that stage happens once.

Two constraints on it, both known: the FTC mail-order rule in the US and the 30-day default in the UK mean either charge on despatch or state a clear ship window on the page. Site copy for that needs roughly a week, so it must be commissioned the moment the decision is made, not after.

**The thing worth protecting is the stage, not the schedule.** A sold-out pre-order announced from that stage is a better outcome than a rushed physical launch that arrives late and misses it entirely.
