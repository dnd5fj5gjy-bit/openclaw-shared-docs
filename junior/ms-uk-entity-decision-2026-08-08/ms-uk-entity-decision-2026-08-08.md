## What the old position was

Until yesterday the UK plan sold through an overseas entity. That made us a **non-established taxable person**. Two consequences followed, and both have been shaping the launch plan:

First, the VAT registration threshold for an NETP is **nil**. Not £90,000. Registration is required before the first taxable supply of any value, triggered the moment stock lands at SCEND.

Second, SCEND is caught by the **Fulfilment House Due Diligence Scheme**, which obliges a UK warehouse to run checks on customers established outside the UK and to hold their VAT numbers. That is why Jack Crumpton wrote on 30 July that they cannot ship a single order without it. He was not being difficult. He was describing his own legal obligation.

So the VAT number became a hard gate in front of every UK order, and it has been sitting there for five weeks while the pre-order campaign collected UK money.

---

## What changes the moment the company is UK-established

Both of those consequences are attached to being **not established in the UK**. Neither survives a genuine UK company.

On the threshold, HMRC's own registration manual is explicit that the nil threshold applies to persons with no UK establishment. A UK-established business gets the ordinary **£90,000** rolling twelve-month threshold. A brand new company starts at zero turnover, so it is not required to be registered on day one at all.

On SCEND, HMRC's internal FHDDS manual puts it beyond argument. The scheme catches goods "owned by a person who is not established in the UK", and the manual's worked example is a business that owns its own stored goods: **"the business does not fall within the scheme."** If Modern Savage Limited owns the stock, SCEND's FHDDS obligation towards us disappears.

Put together: **a UK-established Modern Savage Limited can lawfully take delivery of stock and ship UK orders before it holds a VAT number.** The gate that has held the UK track since 30 July is not a legal gate once the entity is UK-established. It was a consequence of the structure, and you have just changed the structure.

Nobody has said this out loud, including in my own work, because the line was buried in a skincare negotiation.

---

## The catch that decides whether any of this is real

VAT Notice 700/1 is precise about what makes a company established here, and it contains the trap:

> "A company which is incorporated in the UK would normally be considered to have an establishment in the UK as long as it is also able to make or receive business supplies at its registered business address. **A registered, serviced or virtual office alone is not enough to create a business establishment.**"

Incorporation is not sufficient by itself. BGV's registered office is Ignition Law at 1 Sans Walk, which is a law firm providing a registered office service. If Modern Savage Limited is filed there by default, which is exactly what happens when a formation agent does it in an afternoon, the company is UK-incorporated and arguably still not UK-established. Every advantage above evaporates and we are back to a nil threshold and an FHDDS check, having spent a week.

**This is the whole thing. Give the company a real UK business address where it can make and receive supplies, with people and resources actually behind it, before it is filed.** It is a five-minute decision that has to happen first, and it is worth putting to Raemy in exactly those terms rather than as "where shall we register it".

There is a second reason to care: Beau is US-based and your directors sit outside the UK for large parts of the year. HMRC looks at where the business is actually run, not only where it was filed. The address needs to be defensible, not decorative.

## What this does not change

**You should still register for VAT, just voluntarily and without the launch hanging off it.** At the plan's own numbers, 800 subscriptions a month at £55 is roughly £44,000 a month, which crosses £90,000 inside the third month of trading. A UK production run with Protein Partners carries input VAT you will want to reclaim. So registration is coming either way. The change is that a slow application now delays a reclaim rather than the launch.

And it may well be slow. HMRC's target is 40 working days, but a **newly incorporated company with overseas directors** is precisely the profile that triggers extended verification, and practitioners report two to three months on that profile. That is a reason to file the application early, not a reason to wait for it before shipping.

**Three things still cannot start until the company exists**, so every day of delay is a real day:

- **FBO registration takes 28 days and is entity-specific.** SCEND's arrangement is that we register as food business operator at their address. That clock cannot start before Modern Savage Limited exists, and 28 days from mid-August lands mid-September.
- **A bank account.** New company account opening runs one to four weeks, and Stripe's merchant entity has to match it.
- **The EORI number**, which is fast but sits downstream of the company existing.
