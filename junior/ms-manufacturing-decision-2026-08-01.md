## 2. What I checked, and what it says

Three things, all confirmed at source rather than from my own notes.

**First, there is no small-quantity exemption for meat.** UK import rules treat our product as a composite product. A composite containing dairy or egg can escape veterinary checks at the border if it is shelf stable and under half animal product. **Meat has no such threshold.** Any amount of processed meat, explicitly including meat powders and meat extracts, triggers the full regime: an export health certificate, a border control post veterinary inspection, pre-notification on the government IPAFFS system, and an approved country plus a listed establishment.

The adult blend carries 2,500mg of freeze dried bovine liver, heart, spleen, kidney and lung per serving. That is a meat powder. It does not matter that it is ten percent of the scoop.

**Second, our manufacturer is not on the list, and nothing like it is.** Defra publishes the list of non-UK establishments approved to export animal products to Great Britain. I downloaded the version dated **29 July 2026**: 26,952 establishments worldwide, 3,763 of them American.

Of those, **146 US establishments are approved under the meat products section**, which is the section our blend would sit in. I read all 130 distinct names. Every one is a meat processor, a packing company or a cold store. **There is not one supplement, nutraceutical, pharmaceutical or laboratory facility among them.** ACF Pharma, Lightning Nutra and Lightning Labs do not appear anywhere in the American entries, in any category.

This is not a paperwork gap we can close in August. A third country establishment gets listed because its own government asks ours to list it and ours agrees. Our manufacturer cannot apply directly, and the published guidance gives no service standard for how long it takes. Every practitioner account of it is months.

**Third, and this is the part I would ask him about before anyone commits to a US-only formula.** USDA rules say livestock lungs must not be saved for use as human food, and may be exported only for non-human purposes. Our adult formula contains bovine lung. It sells legally in America because supplements sit under a different regulator than meat, but an export health certificate for Britain is signed off through the meat system, which is the one that excludes lung outright. **I would not assume that certificate can be issued for this formula at all.** That needs confirming with the manufacturer and with APHA rather than taken on my reading, but it is the kind of thing that surfaces in week six of a listing application, not week one.

## 3. The finding that actually changes the decision

I went looking for whether we should reverse it instead: make everything in the UK, ship to America. That has the same shape of problem in the other direction. US rules restrict imported ruminant offal, and British bovine material carries additional BSE conditions on top.

**So the organ content means neither direction is a simple import.** This is not an argument about which country is cheaper. Meat crossing a border is a regulated event in both directions, and our product is meat with vitamins in it.

Which means Bear's instinct is right and his mechanism is wrong. **You cannot simplify to one manufacturer serving two markets. You can simplify to one market.** And he has already told you which one he would pick: "I suspect US might be the best bet."

That is the version of his advice that survives contact with the rules, and it is genuinely simpler than the plan I sent him. One factory, one market, one set of labels, one fulfilment partner, one compliance regime. The 800 founding places become 800 American places. The UK follows once there are reorders to justify a UK production run, which is his six month test anyway.

Bear said margins do not matter much during the test phase as long as the cash is there. Agreed, so this compares the only currency that matters right now.

**My recommendation is A, with one modification.** Run the founding 800 in the US, where the product is already legal, the factory is already engaged, the packaging is already ordered and the price is 38% better per subscriber. Keep the UK on a separate, slower track rather than trying to feed it from America.

**The modification, because 12 September is a UK stage.** The Summit Stack contains no animal organs. It is creatine, electrolytes, NMN, PQQ, resveratrol, D3 and K2. **It is not meat, so none of the above applies to it.** It can be imported into the UK on ordinary food supplement rules. If you want something to sell in that room in September rather than a waiting list, that is the SKU that can be there, and nobody has looked at it that way.
