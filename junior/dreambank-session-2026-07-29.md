## The commercial case, in one paragraph

HelpBnk's filed SIC code is **73110, advertising agencies**, and every public account of the business
says the same thing: everything is free to the user, nothing behind a paywall, no courses, and the money
comes from advertising against content. So attention is not a nice-to-have for them, it is the entire
revenue line. Squibb's own channels are reported at around 200 million views a month, and Bear's
run at roughly 100 million impressions a month. Those two figures are not like-for-like and should not
be quoted as a comparison, but the direction is not in doubt: Bear is not a garnish on this platform, he
is a material change to the only input their business monetises. **That is the argument for being paid
from the revenue line rather than thanked for the mission.**

**So "the Dream Group" is a brand architecture, not a corporate structure.** That is not a scandal, plenty

Two consequences follow immediately:

  obligation, and it sits directly on top of whatever line we would be sharing.
- **The DreamFund is a promise, not a balance sheet.** If Bear's name goes near it, we should know who
  holds the money and who is liable if a promised payout does not arrive.

## The name question, flagged once and then dropped

"Bank" is a sensitive word in UK company naming and normally needs approval before it can be used in a
registered name. That is almost certainly why they write **HelpBnk** without the vowel. Yet the invite
Bear enthusiastically endorsed says **DreamBank**, spelled out in full. If Bear's name ends up on a public
thing the audience reads as holding or promising money, that is a question for Helen and Raemy before it
is a marketing question.

Ask it once, lightly, and move on: *"Quick one so our lawyers do not slow us down later. Is DreamBank the
public name, and have you had it cleared? I noticed you drop the vowel on HelpBnk, so I assume you have
already been through this."* If the answer is confident and specific, it is fine. If it is vague, that is
Raemy's item, not a reason to cool.

## The three shapes. Bring ours, do not receive theirs

**1. Content partnership, paid on the revenue it creates. Recommended.**
A defined format or series. Their production, Bear's channels and involvement, revenue share on the
advertising and sponsorship attributable to it. It needs no entity that does not yet exist, it needs no
valuation argument, and it pays Bear out of the line he moves. It is also the only one of the three that
can realistically be drafted in two weeks.

**2. Name and likeness licence.**
Bear lends his name to a defined property for a defined term. Fee plus a **creditable annual minimum**
rather than a flat fee, so the floor costs them nothing if the thing performs. Needs a counterparty that
can contract and pay, which today means HelpBnk UK Ltd.

**3. Equity.**
Park it. Not refuse it, park it. The honest line is that there is nothing to take equity in yet, and
saying so is a favour to them.

If they push to start creating first and paper it later, that is the moment that costs the most money.

## Read-aloud lines for the four moments that will actually happen

**When they say "let's just start making content and figure the commercials out as we go":**
"Happy to move fast, and I would rather we did. But Bear's involvement is the asset here, so let's put one
page around it first. A short term sheet, not a contract. I can have it to you Monday."

**When equity comes up:**
"Genuinely open to it further down the road. Right now there is one company and Simon owns most of it, so
there is not really a thing for us to hold yet. Let's build the revenue first and revisit when there is a
group to take a piece of."

**When the mission is doing the work of the money:**
"We are aligned on the mission, that is why Bear called for this. It also has to work commercially,
because Bear's name is the thing that makes it work for you."

**When they ask what Bear will commit to:**
Do not answer with a number of posts or appearances. "Tell me what the format is and I will tell you what
Bear can do around it." Bear's time is the scarcest thing in this conversation and should be the last
thing priced, not the first thing offered.

**Do not**, on this call: give a view on Ted's, Voy, Modern Savage or any other BGV numbers; commit Bear to
a date; agree a public announcement.
