He is right that there is nothing procedurally to sign and no vote to attend. **He is answering a
different question from the one the letter poses**, and the gap between them is Bear's stake.

**The letter can be read two ways and they lead to opposite outcomes.**

- **Reading A, Raemy's.** The 3,001 shares are offered to existing shareholders first under the SHA's
  pre-emption. Take them up and nobody is diluted. But the funding offer is *conditional on the offeror
  receiving 3,001 ordinary shares* - so if the shareholders take them up in full, **the £7m does not
  happen.** On this reading the company is offering something that, if accepted, kills the money it is
  raising. That is not a normal thing for a letter to do, and it is worth asking why it does.
- **Reading B, the one the conditionality points at.** The offeror gets 3,001 regardless, and existing
  shareholders are offered *further* shares alongside. On this reading Bear pays £1.95m and still ends
  up below where he started.

Under Reading B the letter's own promise fails arithmetically. It says subscribing pro rata enables a
shareholder "to maintain their current percentage ownership". **It cannot.** If every existing holder
subscribes their full pro-rata 3,001, the count goes to 8,974 and Bear's 466 shares are **5.2%**, not
7.8%. You cannot issue 3,001 shares to an outsider and leave everyone else's percentage intact - the
two statements in the letter cannot both be true.

**So the question that has to be answered before the 21 days run is simply: which is it.** One line to
Andrew settles it: *if existing shareholders take up their full pro-rata entitlement, does the offeror
still receive 3,001 shares?* Everything else - the £1.95m, the 3.9%, the 50.24% - hangs off that answer,
and right now Bear has been told "nothing to do" without it.

The other half stands whichever reading is right: **no reply is a decline.** Raemy's own account of the
mechanism confirms it. The default lands on **Wednesday 26 August** and it is chosen by silence.

## The arithmetic

Companies House shows DIRTEA's last filed statement of capital at **GBP 2,972** - 2,972 ordinary £1 shares, allotted 31 January 2025, filed 10 February 2026. At the letter's £8,330 per share that values the company at **£24.76m**, which is the same £25m the October 2024 round went in at. The price has not moved in twenty-two months.

Issue 3,001 new shares into 2,972 existing and the company has 5,973. The offeror holds 3,001 of them, which is **50.24%**. Not a blocking stake, not influence - control.

Bear's 7.8% is roughly 232 shares. After the issue those 232 shares are **3.88%** of the company. If he wants to stay at 7.8% he subscribes pro rata for about 234 more shares at £8,330, which is **£1,950,000 in cash**.

There is a second layer in the same letter. The proposed employee option pool of up to 1,000 options, if granted in full, takes the count to 6,973 and Bear to **3.33%**.

And the pro-rata offer only preserves anyone's percentage if **every single shareholder writes their cheque**. If they all did, the count would be 8,974 and the offeror would sit at 33.4%. On a cap table that has already been through one rescue, they will not all pay. So Bear's final percentage is decided by other people's willingness to fund, not by his own.

One more thing worth saying out loud: at the letter's own £8,330, Bear's 232 shares are worth £1.93m against the £2.45m he put in. **The price they are asking him to pay more at is a price at which he is already down.**

## The lever nobody has named

A holder of more than 25% can block a special resolution. Khazaei has that on his own. Bear's 7.8% sits alongside it.

Whether that lever bites depends on one technical point, and it is worth getting a straight answer on before anything else. Under section 561 of the Companies Act 2006, existing shareholders have statutory pre-emption rights over new shares **issued for cash**, and disapplying those rights needs a 75% special resolution. But section 565 takes allotments for **non-cash** consideration outside that protection. This deal is drafted as shares issued *as a condition of a loan facility* rather than as shares subscribed for cash - and if that is deliberate, the drafting is what removes the shareholder vote.

So: if the 3,001 shares are being issued for cash, the disapplication needs 75% and Khazaei alone can refuse. If they are being issued as non-cash consideration for the facility, statutory pre-emption does not apply and the protection has to come from somewhere else - the articles, any investment agreement, the directors' authority to allot under section 551, and whatever change-of-control consents Bear's own subscription documents contain.

Raemy should be reading the articles and Bear's share documents this week, not in the third week of August.
