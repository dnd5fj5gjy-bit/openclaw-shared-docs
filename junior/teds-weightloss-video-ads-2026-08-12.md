# Ted's Health, weight management: 10 video ads

Built 12 Aug 2026. Google Veo 3.1 via Higgsfield, 9:16, 8 seconds, native voiceover, no captions burned in.
Files: `workspace/docs/teds-ads-2026-08-12/`. 220 credits of Jesse's Higgsfield ultra plan.

## The constraint I applied, and why

Jesse asked for "weight loss medication ads". **None of these name, show or imply the medicine**, because
in the UK you cannot advertise a prescription-only medicine to the public. That is the Human Medicines
Regulations, enforced jointly by the MHRA, GPhC and ASA, and it is not a Meta policy you can appeal.

It is also the live reason: **Meta rejected asset "34" at 08:34 on 12 Aug for "looks like your ad sells
prescription drugs"**. So this set is deliberately built on the clinic, the process and the outcome.

Second rule, the one that actually gets weight-loss ads pulled: Meta's personal-attributes policy. An ad
may not assert or imply it knows something personal about the viewer. **Every line below keeps the
sentence off the reader** - it is about "our patients", "most people we see", or the clinic itself.

## Compliance check, run on every asset

- No medicine named, shown or implied. No pen, needle, syringe or packaging in any frame.
- No before-and-after, no body comparison, no weighing scales.
- No line asserts anything about the viewer.
- No burned-in text, so headline and primary text stay editable per placement.
- Screens checked at full resolution: 05's phone shows plain placeholder fields and a tick, no invented
  branding or readable claims.

## Two were rebuilt before delivery

- **03** first rendered as a rack of identical shirts. The two-different-sizes idea never appeared, so the
  visual carried none of the line. Rebuilt as a worn leather belt with several stretched notch holes.
- **08** rendered with a visibly malformed face on the walking shots, and the opening pose read as dejected
  rather than calm. Rebuilt shooting the laces tight and the walk from behind, so no face is ever visible.

## Before any of these run

1. **A clinician appears on camera in 01, 02, 06 and 09.** These are AI-generated people, not Ted's staff.
   That is normal for stock-style advertising, but it must not be presented as "our team" in the copy, and
   if the ASA asks, the answer needs to be that they are actors. Worth Jonathan's view.
2. Headline, primary text and description still come from the six written concepts in
   `teds-weightloss-ad-copy-2026-08-11.md`. These are the visual half only.
3. **The pixel still has no patient event.** Spend is live and cannot currently be attributed to a
   consultation or a prescription. That is the thing worth fixing before scaling any of these.
