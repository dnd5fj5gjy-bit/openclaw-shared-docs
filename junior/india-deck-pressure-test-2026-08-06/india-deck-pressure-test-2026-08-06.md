# The India deck: four numbers to fix, one that is under-pricing you

**6 August 2026. Source: the three decks you sent Del at 21:44 last night, read in full. Every figure below checked at its published source, not from memory. Nothing sent to anyone.**

---

## The verdict

The argument is sound and most of the numbers hold. I checked twenty-odd hard claims across the three decks and the majority came back verified at source, several of them stronger than you have written them. The strategy slide is the best thing you have written for a counterparty this year.

Four claims will not survive an Indian media agency checking them, and one of those is on the closing slide of two of the three decks. Separately, the money slide is anchored to a rate card that is too low and points the wrong way round, which means the deck argues for a smaller number than you could actually defend. And the finale, the 6,000m Ladakh summit, currently names no peak, and the peak everyone would assume has been closed since 2020.

Fix those before it goes to a sponsor. Del will not catch them tonight. A Mumbai agency's planner will catch them in an afternoon.

---

## The four to fix

**"579m not online yet" does not reconcile with any published figure.** This is on the closing slide of both the PITCH and the 6-slide, sat directly beside 958m, which invites the reader to add them. They come to 1.537bn, which is larger than India's population. The 958m is right: IAMAI and Kantar's Internet in India Report 2025, 958m active internet users, roughly 8% growth, rural running at about four times urban. All three of your other stats on that slide come from that one report and are correct. But the offline number does not come from it. The widely published figure is around 440m, from DataReportal's Digital 2026 India. Use 440m, or say "roughly half a billion" and lose the false precision. The point is unchanged and the slide stops being checkable against you.

**"India's connected-TV base doubled in a year" is overstated.** FICCI-EY 2026 puts CTV at about 68m households in 2025, of which roughly 40m weekly active, up from 30m the year before. That is a third, not a doubling. What did roughly double is Shorts viewing on connected TV, which is a different claim. The true version is better anyway: CTV advertising revenue grew 42% to ₹99bn in 2025 while linear television posted its fourth consecutive year of revenue decline. That is money moving, which is what a sponsor cares about, rather than screens appearing.

**"179 countries" cannot be sourced, and the superlative attached to it is worse.** I cannot find 179 anywhere. What is on the record, from Discovery's own release, is that the Modi episode was shown in more than 180 countries. Say "broadcast in more than 180 countries" and either drop "the most widely travelled adventure format in television history" or attribute it. An unattributed superlative on a slide is the cheapest thing in the deck to attack and the most expensive to defend.

**The 42.7m needs one more word on it.** The figure is real and I found it verbatim: cumulative reach across the Discovery network, originals and repeats included, **and DD National**. You already say "cumulatively", which is honest. But DD National is the free-to-air state broadcaster, and an Indian media buyer knows it. If you do not name it, they will discount the whole number on the spot and quietly mark you as someone who rounds up. Name it and the number survives, because 42.7m across Discovery and DD National is still an enormous statement about appetite. Related: the No. 3 channel ranking is real, but sources disagree on the underlying impressions, so cite the rank and never the impressions.

Two smaller ones while you are in there. The ₹21,000 crore was paid to "creators, artists and media companies", which includes the labels and broadcasters, so say it as published rather than as "creators". And the top-of-market comparison works better as "dedicated adventure and expedition channels stop around four million", because Sourav Joshi at thirty-odd million covers travel-adjacent ground and someone will name him at you.

## The number that is costing you money

Slide 14 says top Indian creators get $5,000 to $26,000 for an Instagram collaboration, and that YouTube integrations run $1,200 to $12,000. That is the wrong way round and both ends are low.

The Indian market data for 2026 has YouTube integrations at three to five times the equivalent Instagram Reel, and integrated sponsorships with the largest YouTube creators running ₹15 to ₹80 lakh, which is roughly $18,000 to $95,000. Mega-tier Instagram Reels run ₹1.4 lakh to ₹59 lakh.

So the deck currently sets its own floor at a tenth of the real ceiling, and then argues that a cinematic multi-language series "prices above those rate cards". You are asking a sponsor to price you above $12,000. On the corrected card you should be asking them to price you above $95,000.

This also fills the hole in that slide. You say $250k a season, paid up front, but never say by how many sponsors at what price, which is the first question any brand asks because it tells them what they are buying and who they are sharing it with. On the corrected numbers, $250k is roughly three to six category sponsors at the top of the Indian rate card, or a single title sponsor priced above it. Put that sentence on the slide.

One honesty note on the same slide: "no production risk on our side" is only true once the money is in. Until a lead sponsor signs, every pound of risk is ours, and the sizzle shoot you list as step two of the next-steps slide is unfunded. It is also the item that unlocks all the others. Give it a number, because that is the only cheque you are actually asking anyone for right now.

## The finale has a peak problem

The season ends on a 6,000m Ladakhi summit you fly off the top of. It is the best idea in the deck and it currently names no mountain.

The obvious one, Stok Kangri at 6,153m, has been closed since 2020 by the Ladakh administration and the wildlife department over glacier depletion, with no confirmed reopening. Anyone in the Indian adventure trade knows this. If the finale is discussed in a room and you do not have a peak, the conversation stalls there.

The two open alternatives are Kang Yatse II at 6,250m and Dzo Jongo at 6,128m. But Kang Yatse sits inside Hemis National Park, which runs straight into your own red line on slide 15, where you rule out filming in tiger reserves and core forest zones. Decide which rule you actually mean, protected areas or tiger reserves specifically, because as written the deck rules out its own ending. That is exactly the sort of internal contradiction a sponsor's legal team finds.

The IMF number is fine, incidentally, and you have been conservative. It is 123 peaks open to foreigners, not 100-plus: 51 in Uttarakhand, 47 in Himachal, 15 in the old J&K and 10 in Sikkim.

Name the peak, confirm it is on the open list, and note that flying off a summit in Ladakh needs aero-sports clearance in airspace that is close to a sensitive border, on top of the inner-line permits you already flag. A named and permitted peak turns the finale from an ambition into something a production can be booked against.

## The quote on slide 3 belongs to a competitor

"Survival-led factual in India remains largely untapped" is genuine Warner Bros. Discovery wording. It is also from their announcement of *Naked and Afraid: Aadimanav*, the first Indian adaptation of the format, which premiered on Discovery Channel India and discovery+ on 15 June this year.

So the sentence you are using to prove the seat is empty is a competitor's press release announcing that they are sitting down in it.

I would still use it, because it is the strongest third-party validation of the category you will ever get, and it is on the record. But name Aadimanav in the deck yourself and answer it in the same breath: it is a cast-based broadcast format on someone else's channel, with no continuing figure, no owned IP and no audience that transfers. That is precisely the gap you are describing. If you leave it out, a sponsor's agency finds it in one search and it looks like you either missed it or hid it. If you put it in, you look like the person who has actually read the market.

## What is solid, so you know what to lean on

The platform case is fully verified and you can say all of it without a caveat: 650m monthly logged-in Shorts viewers in India as of June 2025 and 75m on connected TV, both from Google India's own Brandcast; over 100m channels uploading in the past year with more than 15,000 past a million subscribers; ₹21,000 crore paid out over three years. The roughly tenfold RPM gap between an Indian audience and a UK or US one is real, though the exact band varies by source, so say "roughly ten times" rather than quoting $0.60 to $39, where the top end is not supported.

Bir Billing is stronger than you have written it. It has hosted the Paragliding World Cup twice, most recently with 105 pilots from 38 countries, launching at 2,430m and landing at 1,400m. That is a World Cup site with a genuine cross-country record, not just a flying site.

And on slide 16, items 3 and 6 are the same item. The voice-clone pilot is listed twice. Six moves in order is a strong closing slide and it currently has five.
