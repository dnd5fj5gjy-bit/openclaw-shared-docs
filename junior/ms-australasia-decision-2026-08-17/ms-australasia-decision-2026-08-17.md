# Modern Savage in Australasia: licence or export

**The question you left open on 13 August, answered with numbers**

Junior, 17 August 2026

---

## The answer

**Licence it. Do not export.** On 13 August I recommended this on strategy. I have now costed it,
and the numbers are more one-sided than the argument was.

Three figures decide it, per adult pouch:

| | We keep |
|---|---|
| Export to a partner who sells direct only | **A$3.77** |
| Export to a partner who also supplies retail | **minus A$6.14** |
| Licence at a normal 8% royalty | **A$7.93** |

A licence matches the best export outcome at a royalty of **3.8% of net sales**. Published food and
beverage trademark licensing runs **2.5% to 6%**. So an ordinary licence, struck at the low end of
the ordinary market, already beats exporting, before a single risk is priced in.

At 8%, which is defensible for a named founder rather than a bare trademark, we earn **more than
twice** what exporting earns, with **no production cash, no freight, no import permit and no
12-week lead time**.

---

## The finding that actually settles it

**Export cannot reach a shop shelf. At all.**

Our landed cost into Australia is **A$37.84** a pouch. A partner who has to fund a retailer's margin
as well as their own can pay us about **A$31.71**. That is a loss of **A$6.14** on every pouch that
goes through a store.

This matters because retail is the whole game in this category in Australia. Ancestral Nutrition,
the incumbent, claims **550+ retail doors**, and that distribution is the reason they win, not their
formula. Exporting locks us out of the only channel that beats them, permanently, by arithmetic.
Local manufacture opens it.

---

## And it is close to neutral for the partner

A deal that only works for us does not get signed. It does not have to be one:

| Per pouch, to them | Licence | Export |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution | A$22.16 | A$24.47 |
| Import permit, border inspection, 12-week lead | no | **yes** |

**A$2.31 a pouch apart**, nominally in export's favour, and the licence removes three operating
burdens that are worth more than A$2.31 to anyone who has actually run an import line. We are not
asking them to take a worse deal. We are asking them to take the one that also works for us.

## The pricing correction, and it changes the brief

I checked the Australian shelf tonight rather than assuming it. Pulled from the retailers' own
product feeds, not from a review site:

| | Price |
|---|---|
| Ancestral Nutrition, Primal Multi beef organs, 150g powder | **A$49.95** |
| Cell Squared, certified organic beef organs powder | **A$65.00** |
| Cell Squared, certified organic beef liver powder | **A$63.00** |
| Modern Savage adult pouch, at UK parity | **A$109** |

Read that way, we are **twice the category** and the deal dies on margin.

Read per serving, it inverts:

| | Per serving |
|---|---|
| **Modern Savage** (A$109 / 28) | **A$3.89** |
| **AG1 in Australia** (A$120 / 30) | **A$4.01** |

**Modern Savage is not an organ supplement. It is an AG1 competitor with organs in it**, and priced
against AG1 it is 3% cheaper, not 100% dearer. The adult pouch carries 5g of collagen, 5g of beef
protein isolate, prebiotic fibre, probiotics, greens, nine berries and the vitamin package. The
organ content is 2.5g of a 23.5g serving.

**This is the single most important thing to get right in the licensee brief.** If a licensee
benchmarks us against Ancestral Nutrition they will tell us the price is impossible and ask for a
cheaper formula. If they benchmark against AG1 they will tell us it is competitive. Same product,
same price, opposite answer. Brief the shelf, not just the product.

Worth noting the same lens applies at home. The overnight run on 16 August flagged GBP 55 as above
our own July recommendation of a $2.10 to $2.50 per serving band. Against AG1 rather than against
organ capsules, GBP 55 is at market. I am not re-opening UK pricing here, only flagging that the
verdict depends entirely on which shelf you measure from, and we have been measuring from one shelf.

## The blocker, which is the same blocker as everywhere else

**No licensee can make this product, because the specification does not describe it.**

The signed Lightning Nutra sheets do not define the "21 Nutrient Multi Blend", do not define the
"8 Berry Blend", and carry **no weights at all on the excipients**, which is **22.9% of the adult
serving** and effectively the entire sugar declaration.

A licensee cannot manufacture it, cannot label it and cannot lodge it. This blocks Australia, it
blocks the UK, and it is one email to Andrew Kouvel. **He is warm right now** and traded packaging
images with you on WhatsApp on 16 August. Ask for the supplier and trade name of the two blends
rather than a component breakdown. That is easier for him to give and better for us, because a
licensee then buys the identical material instead of building a lookalike.

I have not sent it. It needs your word.

## Confidence

The export and licence arithmetic is solid on the inputs shown. The inputs are not all equal.

competitor prices, the FX rates, and the state of the Drae thread and your diary. **Estimated by me
