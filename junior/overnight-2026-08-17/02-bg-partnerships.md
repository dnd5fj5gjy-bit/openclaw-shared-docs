## Bear's assets, stated plainly, because the pitch depends on which one you are selling

1. **9M Instagram followers** plus global TV distribution. Raw reach.
2. **Trust in the specific domain of danger.** He is arguably the most credible living person on the
   subject of "things going wrong a long way from help." That is a narrow authority and a valuable one.
3. **Chief Scout.** Family and youth credibility that money cannot buy and most talent cannot touch.
4. **A faith audience**, growing, engaged, and almost entirely uncommercialised.
5. **The family unit itself.** Bear, Shara and the boys as a credible household, not just one man.

Most brands who approach him buy asset 1 and pay reach rates. **Assets 2, 3 and 4 are worth
considerably more per pound and are barely being sold.**

## The shortlist

### 1. Emergency connectivity, satellite messaging — the single best open fit

**Who:** Garmin (inReach), Iridium, ZOLEO, and now Starlink / T-Mobile T-Satellite.

**Why this one leads the list.** Bear's entire public identity is getting home alive from somewhere
remote. A satellite SOS device is the most literal possible expression of that. There is no stretch
in the story, which is rare.

**The commercial gap is the interesting part.** I looked at how this category markets itself. It sells
almost entirely on technical review coverage: GearJunkie, OutdoorGearLab, Backpacker, Outdoor Life.
It is a spec war fought in the enthusiast press. **The category has no consumer face at all.** As
satellite messaging goes mainstream, and it is going mainstream fast now that Starlink and T-Mobile
have pushed it into ordinary phones, the winner will be whoever gets out of the spec war first and
sells the feeling instead of the feature. That is precisely what Bear does.

**Conflict screen:** clean. This is hardware, not survival education, so BGSA's licence should not
bite. Worth one check with whoever holds the BGSA relationship because the licence scope is broad and
our own map flags it as poorly drawn.

**Shape of the deal:** multi-year ambassador plus product integration into Running Wild or Adventure
Garage. Note that routes through EBG if it touches the shows.

---

### 2. Life insurance and family protection — the biggest cheque on this list

**Who:** Vitality first, then Aviva, Legal & General, Zurich.

**Why.** Insurance brands spend enormous sums on trust and almost always buy it badly, usually with a
comedian or a talking animal. Bear sells risk and preparedness sincerely, which is what the product
actually is. "I have spent my life managing risk. Here is the one everyone ignores" is a campaign that
writes itself and is entirely true.

**Vitality specifically**, ahead of the rest, because their whole model rewards members for healthy
behaviour. That maps directly onto the Modern Savage handbook: four pillars, ninety days, measurable.
There is a genuine product integration available, not just a face on a poster. That is the difference
between a fee and a partnership.

**Conflict screen:** clean. Nothing in the map touches financial services.

**One caution.** Jesse killed the Revolut approach on 16 Aug, but that was a judgment about a
commission-paid salesman with no offer, not about the category. Financial services remains open. It
just needs to arrive as a real proposition rather than a cold pitch.

---

### 3. Portable power and home energy resilience

**Who:** EcoFlow, Jackery, Bluetti, Anker SOLIX.

**Why.** Same structural gap as satellite comms and I checked it the same way. Every one of these
brands runs an ambassador or affiliate programme, and every one of them is built on volume: EcoFlow
pays roughly 5% commission to a long tail of creators, Jackery runs the Explorer Club for enthusiasts.
**They are all buying many small voices and none of them has bought one big one.** In a category
where the products are close to indistinguishable on spec, a single credible anchor face is a
disproportionate advantage, and it is currently available.

**Conflict screen:** mild adjacency to Water2 on "home sustainability." Different category and
different shelf, but worth naming to Craig rather than discovering later.

**Honest note:** these are mostly Chinese-owned hardware brands running performance marketing. They
may not have the budget or the instinct for a talent deal at Bear's level. Worth one conversation to
find out, not worth a campaign.

---

### 4. Travel insurance, medical evacuation and assistance

**Who:** Global Rescue, Redpoint Resolutions, battleface, World Nomads.

**Why.** Narrower money than life insurance but a much tighter fit, and these firms already sell to
exactly the expedition audience Bear leads. Global Rescue in particular does real evacuations from
real mountains, so the association is earned rather than borrowed.

**Conflict screen:** clean.

---

### 5. Family fintech

**Who:** GoHenry (now Acorns), NatWest Rooster Money, Greenlight in the US.

**Why.** This is where the Chief Scout asset gets used. Teaching children competence and independence
with money is the same story Bear already tells about competence and independence outdoors. The
brands in this space are all fighting for parental trust and none has a credible parent figure at the
front.

**Conflict screen:** clean.

---

### 6. The faith lane — the one nobody else can copy

**Who:** Alpha, Hallow, YouVersion, plus Christian publishing.

**Why this is different from everything above.** It is not a reach play. Bear is one of very few
globally famous people who talks about faith openly and without embarrassment, and that audience is
large, loyal and almost entirely uncommercialised. **Praise and Pump is the live proof** that there is
appetite here: Bear built a class format, Alastair is on board, and the tracks exist.

**Note we are already in this space.** There is a live Hallow relationship, active as recently as
13 August. So the question is not whether to enter the lane, it is whether what exists is being
treated as a partnership or as a favour.

**Conflict screen:** genuine adjacency risk to Mettle and BecomingX. Mettle is men's mental fitness
and Bear is a co-founder, so anything that looks like guided spiritual practice needs checking against
it before it goes anywhere. **This is the one on the list I would not move on without legal input.**

## Deeper research on the top two lanes

### Vitality: their entire roster is stadium sport, and that is the opening

I went through Vitality's ambassador list and sponsorship portfolio properly.

**Ambassadors:** Jessica Ennis-Hill, Jonny Wilkinson, Maro Itoje, Joe Root, Jos Buttler, Ellie
Simmonds, Heather Knight, Lord Sebastian Coe, Keely Hodgkinson, Nat Sciver-Brunt, Luke Donald, Sophia
Dunkley, Isa Guha, Tracey Neville, Iona Stephen, Dr Punam.

**Sponsorships:** Arsenal, Liverpool, Manchester City, AFC Bournemouth (front-of-shirt from 2026-27),
Chelsea, England Rugby, Scottish Rugby, Welsh Rugby Union, England Netball, the ECB, The Hundred, and
Parkrun.

**Every single one is stadium or track sport.** Athletics, rugby, cricket, football, netball. There is
not one adventurer, explorer, mountaineer or outdoors figure anywhere in it.

That is a much better pitch than "add us to the list". Vitality's model rewards members for moving,
and their entire communication of that idea currently runs through elite competitive sport, which is
aspirational but not relatable. **Bear is the opposite: not competing against anyone, outdoors rather
than in a stadium, family rather than individual, and about resilience rather than podiums.** It
reaches the member who is never going to identify with an Olympic 800m champion.

Parkrun in the portfolio is the tell. It shows they already understand that mass participation
matters more than elite performance to their actual book. Bear is the mass-participation version of
what the rest of that roster represents.

**Named route:** Nick Read runs sponsorship at Vitality and has spoken publicly about their strategy.
Their published contact routes are a media page and a partnerships form, which is not where this
belongs. This needs a warm introduction, which is exactly why it should go to CJ and EBG rather than
out from junior@.

### Garmin: no anchor face, and a warning about researching them

Garmin runs ambassador and affiliate programmes, including "League of Garmin", built on commission
and a long tail of creators. Same shape as the portable power category: **a lot of small voices, no
single anchor.** The structural argument holds.

**Two things I chased and killed, which are worth recording so nobody else wastes the time:**

- A search result titled *"What's your mission? Garmin begins the search for the next face of their
  advertising campaign"* looks exactly like a live opportunity. **It is from 2011**, it was aimed at
  European aviation pilots, and entries closed on 30 September 2011. The page now 404s. It still
  ranks.
- Four of the top results for Garmin sponsorship contacts are **typosquatted domains**
  (`garnimus.it.com`, `garnim.it.com`, `garminus.it.com`, `garmirn.it.com`) pushing a contact address
  of `OfficialGarmin@outlook.com`.

Search results for Garmin partnerships are unusually polluted. Anything found that way needs the date
and the domain checked before it is acted on.

## The biggest BG partnership is already in motion, and it changes the advice above

I found this looking for something else, and it matters more than anything on my shortlist.

**7 August, Benjamin Simon at Everest Brand Group to Bear**, copying Del Shoopman, CJ and Will
Gardenswartz. Bear forwarded it to Jesse the same afternoon. Nine days ago, and I can see no follow-up
in the thread since.

**The situation with Running Wild:**

- FOX has "extremely limited schedule availability" for 2027 and their negotiating baseline was
  described as **"starting from free"**.
- Drew's read is FOX wants a **50% or more cut from the $800,000 per-episode licence fee, to roughly
  $400,000**.
- RW9 currently runs on $400k FOX plus $200k international per episode, against a production cost of
  about $800k. **The Fund will not underwrite the deficit** without a return path.
- FOX also say the show needs creative rejuvenation, and cited Dancing with the Stars as a mature
  format revived through casting digital creators. Their words, per Ben: **social reach, cultural
  relevance and younger audience engagement need to become casting criteria alongside celebrity
  recognition.**

**The proposed answer is Meta.** Meta Glasses as season-long title sponsor contributing roughly $400k
an episode to bridge the gap, tied into **Project Kodiak**, the geo-spatial game built around Meta
Glasses and Bear's adventure IP. Ben's framing is that this gives Meta a connected platform across
product, television, gaming, social and real-world experience rather than a sponsorship, and that it
is *"the only adapt-or-die path for the show."*

### Three things this means for the partnership work Jesse asked for

**1. It confirms the routing recommendation above, emphatically.** EBG is not idle. They are
structuring a nine-figure-adjacent strategic partnership with Meta right now. Anything we do on brand
partnerships has to go through them or explicitly around them, and my shortlist should reach CJ and
EBG as a brief rather than going out as cold emails. That was the right call and this is why.

**2. Meta is spoken for.** Any partnership thinking that touches Meta, Instagram, or AI/AR hardware
now sits on top of a live strategic negotiation. That belongs on the conflict map, which currently
does not mention it.

**3. FOX's criticism is the same problem I found on Modern Savage, from the other end.** FOX is saying
the show needs social reach and younger engagement. Modern Savage has 9M followers upstream converting
at 0.06%. **Both are symptoms of one thing: Bear's audience is enormous and the machinery for
converting it is weak.** Fixing it serves the supplement launch and the show's renewal at the same
time, and that is a considerably stronger internal argument for investing in it than either case makes
alone.

I have taken no action on this. It is Bear's, Del's and EBG's thread and Jesse was copied for
awareness. Flagging it because Jesse asked me where the partnership opportunities are, and the honest
answer is that the largest one is already on the table and nobody mentioned it.
