# The UK competitive picture, which we did not have

**Researched 16-17 Aug 2026. Every price below was pulled from the competitor's own live product feed
tonight, not from a review site.**

Our competitive teardown of 15 July is titled *"US Competitive Landscape"* and it is exactly that. But
we sell in pounds, on a UK-facing site, with "Supporting British farmers" on the homepage, and the UK
company was incorporated on 12 August. **Nobody had looked at who we are actually up against here.**

### Three things that fall out of that table

**1. The entire UK field is capsules. Every single one.** The whitespace our July work identified in
the US is, if anything, wider here. Nobody in the UK is selling a flavoured organ powder. The
positioning holds.

**2. We are the most expensive thing in the UK market by a distance.** At £1.96 a serving subscribed
we sit **31% above Carnicopia's dearest SKU** and **more than double their liver product**. That is a
much bigger gap than the US, where our $69 lands neatly at the top of the premium band alongside
Noble Origins.

That is not necessarily wrong. It is the premium-taste-led position, deliberately chosen, and Bear
justifies a premium in a way Carnicopia's founder cannot. But it does mean **the UK proposition has to
work much harder than the US one.** A UK customer comparing us to Carnicopia is looking at £55 against
£27.94 and needs to understand what the extra buys. Right now the product page does not tell them.

**3. Carnicopia is not much of a brand, and that is the interesting part.** I could find no meaningful
Instagram presence for them. Fat Cow, by contrast, has 56,000 followers. Carnicopia is not winning on
brand or reach.

**They are winning on search.** Of the six results for "UK beef organ supplement brands 2026", **four
were Carnicopia's own blog posts**: "Best beef liver supplements UK (2026) guide", "Best beef organ
supplements UK (2026) guide", and so on. They have built a content operation that captures the exact
query a UK customer types, and then answers it with themselves.

**Modern Savage has no equivalent and no UK search presence to speak of.** We have a Recipes page and
a Handbook, neither of which targets a purchase-intent query. This is the cheapest competitive move
available to us and nobody is doing it. Four or five properly written UK-focused guides would compete
directly for the traffic Carnicopia currently owns uncontested, and unlike paid, it compounds.

## The thing I did not expect to find

**Fat Cow, our own co-branded partner, is already in a live collaboration with Carnicopia, our closest
UK competitor.**

From Carnicopia's own product feed:

- **Product:** "CARNICOPIA X Fat Cow Skincare - Inside & Out Glow Stack"
- **Vendor field, verbatim:** `Carnicopia x Fat Cow`
- **Price:** £89.99, in stock and purchasable
- **Published:** 5 July 2026
- **Contents:** Carnicopia GODDESS organ capsules plus Fat Cow's Citrus Tallow Face Cream
- **Their description:** *"a limited-edition collaboration between Carnicopia, Fat Cow Skincare and
  wellness coach Charlie Louise Evans"*

Fat Cow have promoted it themselves. Their own Facebook page carries a post reading
*"Carnicopia UK X FAT COW | Tallow Skin Care🐮🧴 Stay tuned👀"*.

### Why this matters for the agreement we are about to sign

Clause 16.1(a) of the execution version says that during the term, and for twelve months after, Fat
Cow will not:

> produce or supply a **co-branded skincare product** with any brand or individual **whose primary
> positioning is ancestral, primal or organ-based health and nutrition**

Carnicopia's primary positioning is organ-based nutrition. That is the whole company.

**But I do not think this is a straightforward breach, and it is worth being precise about why.** The
Glow Stack is a *bundle* of two existing own-brand products, not a newly formulated co-branded item.
Clause 16.2 expressly says nothing restricts Fat Cow's own-brand range. So a reasonable lawyer could
argue a bundle containing Fat Cow's existing face cream is not "producing a co-branded skincare
product", even though it is marketed under a "Carnicopia x Fat Cow" vendor name.

**That ambiguity is the finding.** The clause as drafted probably does not catch the exact thing that
is already happening with a direct competitor. So before signature there is a decision to make:

- **If we intend to prohibit this**, clause 16.1(a) needs to cover co-branded *bundles, promotions and
  collaborations*, not just co-branded products. As written it almost certainly does not.
- **If we do not mind**, that is a legitimate position, but it should be a decision rather than a
  discovery, and it means the exclusivity is worth less than it reads.
- **Either way, ask Ryan about it directly.** It was published on 5 July, two weeks before he sent the
  proposal on 20 July, and it has not been mentioned. That is probably nothing. It is worth one
  question.

## Three other things in that agreement that are now out of date

Reading the execution version against Jesse's own email to Ryan of 7 August, the document on file
does not reflect three things Jesse has already agreed:

| Point | Execution version says | Jesse agreed on 7 Aug |
|---|---|---|
| **Liability cap** | £250,000 (clause 19.6) | *"Just set it at your insurance rather than £250,000, since you are already covered to £5 million"* |
| **Exclusivity tail** | 12 months after the term (clause 16.1) | *"Exclusivity, agreed. Three months after we finish rather than twelve"* |
| **Contracting party** | **BEAR GRYLLS VENTURES LLP** | *"We are setting up Modern Savage Limited this week as the UK company, so we will sign in that name"* |

The party point is the one I would fix first. **Modern Savage Limited now exists**, incorporated
12 August, company number 17395103. Signing in the name of Bear Grylls Ventures LLP would put a third
entity on a Modern Savage contract, which is the same pattern as the ACF order sitting against BGV
Global Ltd and still unfixed.

**This matters right now, not later.** Ryan said on 7 August that his solicitor "will want the final
version". If the version on file goes over as final, it is wrong on all three points, and two of them
are concessions in Fat Cow's favour that Jesse has already made and would be re-making.

---

## What I would do

1. **Fix the three stale terms before anything goes to Ryan's solicitor.** Half an hour of work.
2. **Ask Ryan about the Carnicopia collaboration**, plainly and without accusation. It may be
   finished, it may be irrelevant, and either answer is useful.
3. **Decide whether 16.1(a) should cover bundles and collaborations**, then draft it to say so.
4. **Start the UK content play.** Four or five UK-focused buying guides. It is the one competitive
   move available with no budget, no approvals and no dependency on anyone's holiday.
5. **Put the price justification on the product page.** A UK customer is comparing £55 to £27.94 and
   currently has nothing to read.
