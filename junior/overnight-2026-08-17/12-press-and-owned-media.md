# The press is already writing this story. We are not in the conversation.

---

## What is in market right now

Coverage of the family supplement venture is running without any apparent involvement from us:

- **Hello Magazine** — Bear's "surprising confession" about Jesse, framed around the family
- **AOL** — *"Bear Grylls: I used to be vegan, now I tell my sons to eat offal"*
- **Plant Based News** — *"Bear Grylls Says Organ Meats Helped His Family 'Thrive' After Ditching
  Vegan Diet"*
- **Spectrum FM** — *"Bear Grylls son Jesse's diet turnaround sparks family supplement venture"*
- **Forbes**, January 2026 — *"Bear Grylls Is Taking On His Most Daring Adventure Yet:
  Entrepreneurship"*
- **The Health Optimisation Podcast with Tim Gray**, recorded 22 July

**The narrative that has formed is the vegan-to-offal story, told about the family rather than by
them.** It is a good story and broadly favourable. It is also being shaped by outlets choosing their
own angle, and Plant Based News covering your supplement launch is a reminder that not every outlet
picking this up is friendly to it.

None of this coverage is on the Modern Savage website. None of it has been amplified on the brand
account. It is free, credible, third-party proof, and it is being allowed to evaporate.

## The biggest owned-media miss: Bear's Substack exists and is empty

`beargrylls.substack.com` is live. It has a description, it is collecting subscribers, and **it has no
posts.**

Its stated subject, in Bear's own words:

> building strength from the inside out. Sharing practical life lessons on resilience, leadership,
> faith, and business, shaped by real experience in the wild and everyday life, to help you live with
> greater courage, clarity, and purpose

Read that against what is already happening elsewhere:

- **The Modern Savage handbook** is live, free, and built on four pillars: Start Strong, Train,
  Nutrition & Lifestyle, Attitude. It captures email.
- **Praise and Pump** is faith plus training, with a format Bear wrote himself and a session with
  Alastair tomorrow.
- **The Substack** promises resilience, leadership, faith and business, and delivers nothing.

**These are the same project, split across three places, and only one of them has any content in it.**

The handbook is essentially the first six Substack posts already written. The faith material is being
built right now. There is a channel sitting empty whose entire premise is the thing Bear is actively
doing.

**Why this matters commercially rather than just tidily.** An email list you own beats a follower
count you rent, and it is the only audience asset that survives a platform changing its mind. Bear
currently has two separate email captures running, on modernsavage.co and on Substack, with no content
going to either and no connection between them. That is the most valuable and least attended thing in
the whole picture.

**What I would do:** one owner, one publishing rhythm, and the handbook as the opening sequence.
Whether that lives on Substack or in the Modern Savage list is a real decision, and the answer is
probably Substack for Bear's own voice and the Modern Savage list for the product, with each pointing
at the other. What is not defensible is two empty pipes.

## The October launch needs one press moment, not a campaign

We have no budget for paid, so the launch has to earn attention. One well-placed moment beats a
scattergun.

**The story that will actually get picked up** is not a supplement launch. Nobody covers those. It is
the family story that outlets are already choosing to run on their own: a globally famous survivalist
took his family vegan, it did not work, his son was struggling, they went the other way, and now they
have built a business on it. That has already proven it travels.

**The angle we should own, and it is genuinely differentiated:** *"I stopped endorsing and started
owning."*

Bear has lent his name to other people's products for years, including a co-branded pack with
Ancestral Supplements in 2023. Modern Savage is the first one the family owns outright. That is a real
shift, it is true, it explains the standard, and it pre-empts the obvious question rather than waiting
for a journalist to ask it.

**The Ancestral point needs handling deliberately.** Ancestral is the Liver King brand that later took
a $25m misrepresentation suit. Any journalist writing a considered piece will find the 2023 Bear
Grylls Survival Pack in a single search. Getting in front of it costs nothing and looks like candour.
Being asked about it looks like something else.

**Practical sequence:**

1. Bear's own "why I made this" piece, on his feed. Everything else hangs off it.
2. One exclusive to a single outlet that has already shown interest, timed to the shipping date rather
   than the pre-order date, so the story has a product in it.
3. Press logos on the product page the same week, so traffic arriving from coverage lands on proof.
4. The podcast circuit, which is where this category is actually built. Tim Gray is already done and
   the clip is still unposted.

**One condition on all of it.** Do not schedule a press moment against an October date until Andrew's
manufacturing start date is confirmed. Our own reconciliation put break-even at 26 July for October to
hold, and inviting coverage to a launch that then slips is worse than no coverage.
