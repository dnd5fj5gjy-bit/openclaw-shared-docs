# Things I found on the live Modern Savage site tonight

**All verified against the page itself on 16-17 Aug 2026, not from memory or a summary.**

These came out of writing the marketing plan. They are commercial rather than creative, and two of
them sit on a page that is currently taking customers' money, which is why they are in their own file.

---

## 1. All three products are priced identically. Verified.

| | Subscribed | One-off |
|---|---|---|
| Modern Savage (adult daily blend) | £55/month | £65 |
| Mini Savage (kids) | £55/month | £65 |
| BG Summit Stack (performance) | £55/month | £65 |

Bundle: take any three and each pouch drops to £46.66 a month, £8.34 less than subscribing separately.

**Is this deliberate?** It might be, and if so it is defensible. One price across the range is simple,
easy to communicate, and the bundle maths is clean. I am not assuming it is a mistake.

**But two things follow from it that are worth a decision rather than a default:**

**The kids' pouch is the one to look at.** Mini Savage at £65 one-off is a high price for a children's
supplement, and it is almost certainly cheaper to make than the Summit Stack, which carries creatine,
NMN, PQQ and trans-resveratrol. Charging the same for both is either leaving margin on the table on
one or suppressing volume on the other. Mini Savage is also our single clearest category
differentiator, because no incumbent has a kids' SKU at all. It is the SKU I would least want priced
out of reach.

### CORRECTION, made later the same night: the pricing is on target and I had this wrong

My first pass said we were priced above the top of our own recommended band. **That was wrong, and it
is worth explaining how, because the mistake is an easy one to repeat.**

I converted the VAT-inclusive UK price into dollars and compared it against a benchmark that was set
in VAT-exclusive US dollars. That is not a like-for-like comparison. UK consumer prices must be
displayed inclusive of VAT, US prices are shown before sales tax, so converting one into the other
overstates the UK price by 20% before you start.

**Then I checked the US prices directly rather than deriving them.** The site prices each market
independently rather than converting:

| | Subscribe | One-off | Bundle of three |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK | £55 | £65 | £46.66 each |
| US | **$69** | **$79** | **$60 each** |

Against the July recommendation of $2.10 to $2.50 a serving, roughly $59 to $69 a pouch:

- **US subscription $69 = $2.46 a serving. That is exactly at the top of the recommended band, and
  exactly at the top of the recommended pouch range. On target.**
- US one-off $79 = $2.82 a serving, above the band, which is normal and intentional: pricing the
  one-off above the subscription is what makes the subscription the obvious choice.
- UK £55 inclusive of VAT is £45.83 ex VAT, about $2.22 a serving, **below** the US and below the
  middle of the band.

So somebody did the work properly. The pricing follows our own recommendation, and the UK and US
prices are coherent with each other once VAT is accounted for. In cash terms a UK customer pays about
8% more than a US customer on subscription, and that gap is VAT, which they would pay on anything.

*FX for the above: GBP/USD 1.3537, frankfurter.dev, quote date 14 Aug 2026.*

**What still stands is the parity point above**, which is about the three products being priced the
same as each other within each currency, not about the level. That question is unaffected by any of
this.

## 2. "Supporting British farmers" is on the homepage. The product is made in Florida.

The homepage hero carries three lines: *"Modern by Design"*, *"Savage by Nature"*, *"Created and owned
by Bear Grylls and his family"*, and **"Supporting British farmers."**

Our own analysis says the adult blend is manufactured by ACF in Florida, and that a US-made
meat-derived product cannot legally enter the UK unless the organ powder producer is GB-listed, which
it is not.

I am not asserting a contradiction, because "supporting British farmers" could refer to a specific
sourced ingredient, or to a commitment for the UK production run, or to something else I do not know
about. **But it is the kind of line a journalist or a customer will test**, and the family name is on
it. Someone who knows the sourcing needs to confirm what it refers to and that it is defensible.

This matters more than usual because the press is already writing about the brand. The family
supplement venture story is running in Hello, AOL, Plant Based News and Spectrum FM right now.

## 3. Instagram is the only social channel linked from the site

No TikTok, no YouTube, no Facebook. For a brand launching in October in a category where discovery has
largely moved to TikTok, one channel is a real constraint, and it is one algorithm change away from
zero reach.

Worth noting the brand account is `@modernsavage.co`. The cleaner handle `@modernsavage` belongs to
someone else entirely, an unrelated individual. Nothing to do about that now, but it is worth knowing
before anyone prints a handle on packaging.

minutes available tomorrow.
