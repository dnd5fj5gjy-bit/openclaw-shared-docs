# The pre-order email sequence

**Six emails, written and ready. Not a brief.**

Our July competitive work identified the warm list as the single highest-return lever available at
launch, and it is the one asset we own outright rather than rent from an algorithm. The handbook page
is capturing addresses right now and nothing is being sent to them.

Two audiences, and they should not get the same thing:

- **The warm waitlist** (roughly 1,800 as of the July note, worth re-counting before send). These
  people asked to hear about the product.
- **Handbook downloaders**, arriving continuously now the page is live. These asked for Bear's
  ninety-day handbook, not for a supplement. Selling to them immediately wastes them.

The sequence below is written for the waitlist. Notes at the end on how to fork it for handbook
downloaders.

**Send cadence:** one a week, six weeks, landing the last one at pre-order close.

**Compliance:** structure and function language only, no disease claims, inside the 12 July guardrail.
Nothing below promises a delivery date beyond what the site already says publicly, which is October.

>
> It was not working for Jesse. That is the honest version, and he has said as much publicly since.

## Email 4 — The taste problem

**Subject:** The bit that took two years
**Preview:** Anyone can put organs in a tub.

> Anyone can put organ powder in a tub. It is not difficult and it is not expensive.
>
> Making it taste like something you would choose to drink is a different problem entirely, and it is the one that took us the longest.
>
> Organ meat has a strong, specific, metallic flavour. The industry's answer was to encapsulate it so nobody has to deal with it. Ours had to be a genuine flavour system that worked without loading it with sweeteners or burying it in cocoa.
>
> Eight freeze-dried berries. That is the answer, and it took a long time to get right.
>
> The reason this matters is not indulgence. It is that a supplement you dread is a supplement you stop taking. Every capsule brand in this category is quietly fighting a compliance problem, and the tub in the back of the cupboard is the evidence.
>
> Something you look forward to gets taken. That is the entire product thesis.

## Forking it for handbook downloaders

These people asked for the handbook. Give them the handbook.

Send emails 3, 1, 4 in that order, one a week, then stop. No pre-order push at all in the first month.
Move them onto the main sequence only if they open two or more.

The logic: they raised their hand for content, not for a product. Converting them takes patience and
costs nothing, and burning them costs an address we cannot get back. The list is the asset, not the
individual sale.
