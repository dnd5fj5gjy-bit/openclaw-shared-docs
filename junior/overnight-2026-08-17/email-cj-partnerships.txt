Jesse asked me to look at where the open partnership opportunities are for Bear. I have done that, and the useful part turned out to be the screening rather than the ideas.

I ran everything against the equity and exclusivity map we built in April. Once you knock out supplements, water and home sustainability, off-grid homes, men's mental fitness, personal development, survival education, group fitness, production, the show-linked outdoor and automotive categories, and AI likeness, most of what an agency would normally pitch is already closed. That is a good problem, but it means the remaining lanes are worth being deliberate about.

Six came through clean. In order of how strongly I would back them:

Emergency connectivity and satellite messaging, meaning Garmin inReach, Iridium, ZOLEO and now Starlink. This is the best fit on the list by some distance. Bear's whole identity is getting home alive from somewhere remote, and a satellite SOS device is the most literal possible version of that. What makes it commercially interesting is that the category currently markets itself entirely through technical review coverage and has no consumer face at all. As satellite messaging goes mainstream, and it is going mainstream quickly now it is reaching ordinary phones, whoever stops fighting the spec war first will win it. One check needed on whether the BGSA licence bites, since the scope is broad, but this is hardware rather than survival education so I do not think it does.

Life insurance and family protection, with Vitality ahead of Aviva, Legal and General and Zurich. Insurers spend heavily on trust and usually buy it badly. Bear sells risk and preparedness sincerely, which is what the product actually is. Vitality leads because their model rewards members for healthy behaviour, which maps onto the Modern Savage handbook almost exactly, so there is a real product integration available rather than a face on a poster.

Travel insurance, medical evacuation and assistance. Global Rescue, Redpoint, battleface. Smaller money, tighter fit, and they already sell to the expedition audience.

Portable power and home energy resilience. EcoFlow, Jackery, Bluetti, Anker. Same structural gap as satellite comms: every one of them runs an ambassador programme buying a long tail of small creators at around five percent commission, and not one has bought a single anchor face. Mild adjacency to Water2 on home sustainability, worth naming to Craig rather than discovering later. Honest caveat, these are performance marketing businesses and may not have the instinct for a talent deal at this level.

Family fintech. GoHenry, now Acorns, plus NatWest Rooster Money. This is where the Chief Scout position earns money. Teaching children competence with money is the same story Bear already tells about competence outdoors, and none of these brands has a credible parent figure at the front.

Faith, meaning Alpha, Hallow, YouVersion and Christian publishing. Not a reach play. Bear is one of very few globally famous people who talks about faith openly, that audience is large and almost entirely uncommercialised, and Praise and Pump shows the appetite is real. We already have something live with Hallow, so the question is whether it is being treated as a partnership or a favour. This is also the one I would not move on without legal input, because of the adjacency to Mettle.

Two things I want to flag rather than assume.

The first is routing. EBG are paid twenty five thousand dollars a month for Running Wild and Adventure Garage brand partnerships. If we start approaching brands directly we are either duplicating what they are already paid to do or cutting across their mandate. My view is that anything touching the shows goes to them, and only the categories outside that go direct. I would rather you tell me where that line sits than guess at it.

The second is instrument. A cold approach from an assistant is the wrong tool for someone with nine million followers. Bear should be approached, not approaching. Where there is no warm route, I would rather wait for one than spend the asset cheaply. I checked the public contact routes for Garmin and Vitality and both are sponsorship request forms, which is not where this belongs.

Full write up with the conflict screening for each is on file and I can send it over or put it in front of Jesse, whichever is more useful.
