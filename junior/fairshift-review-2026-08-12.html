Fairshift

For Will Usher. Prepared by Junior, Jesse Grylls' AI agent.
This goes at the commercial model, not the site. The site is the strong part.

I looked at the prototype at usher-autonomous-systems.github.io/fairshift, rendered properly rather than the empty shell you get from a plain fetch.

Everything below is checked at source and dated. Where a number is yours I have taken it from your estimator; where it is the market's, the source is named at the end.

1. Your take is about 2.8x your closest competitor's

Your estimator quotes £129.00 ex VAT for a 6 hour bar shift, which is £21.50 an hour, against an illustrative worker rate of £13.00 an hour.

What that £13.00 actually costs you as employer of record:

ComponentPer hour
Worker pay£13.00
Holiday pay accrual, 12.07%£1.57
Employer NI, 15% above the secondary threshold£2.19
Fully loaded employment cost£16.75
Your charge£21.50
Your gross margin£4.75

That is 28% on top of employment cost, or 22% of the charge rate. Pension auto-enrolment, employer's liability insurance and right-to-work checking all come out of the £4.75, so the real figure is thinner.

Limber charges 10% on top of employment costs, on the same structure. They are also employer of record: "We employ the staff, not you." Their own site quotes fee examples of £1.38 and £1.47 an hour, and a Plus plan at £25 per site per month that drops to 0% when a venue rehires from its own pool.

On take rate alone that is 2.8x what the incumbent asks. But take rate is not what a venue buys, and benchmarking you against Limber alone turned out to be the wrong comparison. Limber is the cheapest operator in this market, not the typical one.

2. What the market actually charges, and why you are not expensive

Published rate cards, read on 12 August 2026:

WhoCharge to venue, per hour
Limber, digital, employer of record, 10% take~£18.43
Fairshift, your estimator£21.50
The Hospitality Company, London waiting staff, min 4 hours£21.50 + VAT
Poptop, London professional waiting staff, aggregated£22.00 – £32.00

You have priced to the penny at what a named London incumbent charges, at the bottom of the published London range, and about 17% above the one digital player undercutting everybody. You are not expensive. The 2.8x is real on the take, but the take is a small part of the price, so it makes the gap sound far larger than a venue would ever experience it.

So do not cut to 10%. That would remove about 64% of your gross margin to close a 17% price gap, and would drop you below the traditional agencies that are most venues' actual alternative. It would also start a price war with the operator furthest down the cost curve, on your first day.

The move that survives both comparisons

The National Living Wage is £12.71 from 1 April 2026. Your illustrative £13.00 is 29p above the legal floor, 2.3%. A site whose entire promise is "done honestly" cannot have "fair pay" mean 29p, and someone will eventually say so in public.

So hold the price and move the pay instead. Pay £14.00 an hour:

  • Employment cost: £14.00 → £15.69 with holiday → £18.04 with NI
  • Hold your charge at £21.50, and your take falls from 28% to about 19%
  • Margin per hour: £4.75 → £3.43. A 28% haircut, not a 64% one.

The venue pays what it would pay a traditional agency. The worker gets a pound an hour more than anyone else is paying. The front page becomes literally true, and you are not competing with Limber on the one axis where they will beat you.

3. Coople went offline on 1 January 2026

Their own homepage carries it: "Coople is now offline from 1 Jan 2026." They were the best funded player in exactly this space in the UK.

Do not read that as the market being empty and waiting. Read it as the market having already killed the company with the most money in it. Before anything else, work out what Coople could not make work, because whatever it was is still sitting there and your prototype does not yet say how you avoid it. My guess, worth testing rather than trusting: the margin above does not cover the cost of the human vetting and support that makes the service worth choosing, and it does not scale with software, because it is people.

Which leads to the thing an investor will say to you. At 22% of revenue, with support and vetting linear in headcount, this is a staffing business with a good website, not a marketplace. Staffing businesses get priced on 0.5x to 1.5x revenue. Marketplaces get 5x to 10x. Decide which one you are building on purpose, because the pricing above decides it for you by default.

4. The 2027 change that goes at your core promise

Under the Employment Rights Act 2025, qualifying workers on zero and low hours contracts get a right to be offered guaranteed hours reflecting the hours actually worked in a reference period. Agency workers are explicitly in scope, and the obligation sits with the hirer, that is the venue, not with you. Expected in 2027.

Your pitch to a venue is flexibility without commitment. The law is about to make repeated use of the same worker create a commitment, on the venue, by operation of law. The venues that use you most are the ones that get caught. Nobody in hospitality has priced this yet.

That is a threat, and it is also the single best wedge on this page. Whoever turns up in 2027 with a product that tracks reference periods, warns a venue before a worker qualifies, and handles the offer properly, is selling compliance rather than labour. Compliance is bought by finance directors, is sticky, and is not priced at £1.47 an hour.

The consultation on the detail closes 25 August 2026. That is thirteen days from today. Read it and respond. It is free, it is public, and being on the record as the operator who engaged is worth more to a young company than it costs.

5. What is genuinely good

Do not let anyone talk you out of these.

  • "What you won't find on this site." No fake reviews, no fabricated numbers, no false badges. Most people at this stage invent all three. Keep it on the live site, not just the prototype.
  • Showing ex-VAT and the cash total together. Small venues get burned by £129 becoming £154.80. A real, ownable difference that costs you nothing.
  • "A simple chain, not a promise of guaranteed fulfilment." Refusing to promise fill rate is the right call and it will save you your first three relationships.
  • Naming five roles rather than all of hospitality. Bar, waiting, runners, kitchen porters, front of house. Narrow enough to actually be good at.
  • The postcode checker returning an honest no. Better than a map of places you would like to be.

6. What I would do next, in order

  1. Answer the PAYE question, the one I asked you on WhatsApp. If workers are self-employed, the control and direction test in hospitality will not hold, and that is an HMRC and tribunal problem rather than a pricing one. If they are PAYE through you, the model above is right and margin is your whole business.
  2. Hold £21.50 and raise worker pay to £14.00, taking your cut from 28% to 19%. Do not chase Limber down to 10%.
  3. Put the comparison into the estimator. You are at the same price as a named London incumbent and below the published London range, and you are showing your number with nothing beside it. That is the one presentation that makes a fair price look expensive. Show the venue what the shift costs all-in through you against a traditional agency, and show what the worker takes home in both.
  4. Work out what killed Coople before you write another line of code.
  5. Read the consultation and respond before 25 August.
