# Amrita service agreement - review

**Ted's Health x Functional Nutrition Ltd (trading as Amrita Nutrition). 3 August 2026. Junior.**

**Verdict: do not sign as drafted.** Two things change the economics and one is time-critical
this week. The rest is a normal supplier markup, but the first two are not points of drafting
taste, they change what the deal is worth and whether we get the brand we actually want.

---

## 1. The 20% is not 20%, and below a £50 basket it is close to nothing

The discount is 20% off RRP **excluding VAT**, and shipping is **£4.95 per order, free over £40
inc VAT**. Both halves matter.

Take RRP ex VAT as R. The patient pays us 1.2R, we hand 0.2R to HMRC, so we keep R. We pay
Amrita 0.8R. Gross margin is 0.2R **minus the shipping**, against a patient payment of 1.2R.

- Before any shipping, 20% off ex-VAT RRP is **16.7% of what the patient actually pays.**
- The £4.95 wipes out the margin entirely below about **£29.70 retail**.
- Free shipping cuts in at £40 inc VAT on the trade order, which is roughly **£50 retail**.

So between £30 and £50 retail we earn between nothing and about £3.40, then at £50 it steps up
to the full 16.7%. There is a cliff, not a curve. Pure Encapsulations lines mostly sit in the
£20 to £45 band, so a **single-SKU order is the normal case and the normal case earns us almost
nothing.** Take off card fees of roughly 1.5% plus 20p and some of it is negative.

Three asks, in order of how much they are worth:

1. **Free carriage on all orders.** We are a reseller sending them continuous volume, not a
   practitioner buying a box a month. This is the single highest-value change and the easiest
   for them to give.
2. **A reseller rate, not a practitioner rate.** 20% off RRP is what Amrita gives an individual
   practitioner who is doing none of the work. We carry the platform, the payment, the customer
   service, the returns conversation and the clinical wrapper. 30-35% is the rate that matches
   what we are actually doing.
3. **Pin down what the £40 is measured on** - our trade invoice or the retail value. As drafted
   it is ambiguous, and the answer moves the cliff by £10.

There is also a build answer that costs them nothing: **sell protocols, not single products.**
A three-product protocol clears £50 on its own and lands us in the 16.7% band by design. Worth
Felix knowing before he builds the basket.

## 2. We have told them we are an open storefront. That is the answer that loses Pure Encapsulations

Tom asked on 31 July: *"Is access restricted by login? (this impacts product availability)"*. He
flagged the impact himself, because his brands gate on it.

- Amrita's own site says: "The Amrita Nutrition site is for registered Practitioners and their clients." Everyone else is sent to their consumer site, Supplement Hub.
- Pure Encapsulations' own channel policy is explicit that selling online does not convert their healthcare-professional channel into a direct-to-consumer one.
- Jesse answered this correctly on 20 July: the supplements sit "inside the patient's own portal behind login, rather than on an open public storefront."
- **Felix's reply today at 12:52 says browsing is open to anyone and only buying needs an account.**

That is the version that goes to Tom's brand managers. Pure Encapsulations is the brand Jesse
named as the one we most want, and open browsing is the fact most likely to get it refused, or
to get the range cut back to whatever the non-gated brands allow. Felix did offer to build it
behind a login, but it puts the burden on Tom to come back and it will be read as our position.

**Fix it today while the thread is warm.** One line: the supplement range sits behind patient
login, not on the open storefront, consistent with what Jesse set out on 20 July. It costs
nothing, nothing is live yet, and it is the difference between the full Pure range and a
shortlist.

## 3. The clause markup

**Counterparty.** The contract is with **Functional Nutrition Limited**, company 06418561,
incorporated 2007, active, Sheffield. Amrita Nutrition is the trading name. That checks out at
Companies House. Two small things: the registered office there is *The Old Chapel*, Mortimer
Street, and the agreement says *8* Mortimer Street, so use the Companies House version; and the
company's filed SIC code is 96040, physical well-being activities, not wholesale.

**Cl. 3.2 - we verify everything, including allergy data.** We are made responsible for verifying
descriptions, dosage and **allergy information** before publishing, and they carry no liability
for inaccuracies. We cannot verify the allergen content of someone else's product; only the
manufacturer can. On a platform dispensing against a clinician's protocol this is the wrong
allocation. Ask for a warranty that the feed is accurate as supplied by the brand, and carve
allergen and dosage data out of 3.2.

**Cl. 6.2 and 6.4 - refunds come to us, not the patient, unopened only, 30 days, shipping
deducted.** We are the seller of record. Our obligations to the patient are set by consumer law,
not by Amrita's returns policy, so every gap between the two is a cost we absorb with no recovery.
Patients on a protocol open things. Ask for refunds to follow our consumer obligations, or accept
it and price the leakage in.

**Cl. 13.2 with 19.2 - liability capped at the price of the product, and we indemnify them.**
They cap at the amount paid for the item in question, we give a broad indemnity, and no indemnity
comes back the other way. Ask for a product liability indemnity from them and evidence of their
product liability cover. Note the cap cannot exclude death or personal injury caused by negligence
in any event.

**Cl. 21.1 with 12.2 - they terminate instantly, we give 30 days.** 21.1 lets them terminate
immediately for breach of **any** term, with no cure period, and clause 1 makes listing an
unapproved product an immediate-termination event. We must give 30 days. Ask for a 10 business
day cure period for anything remediable, and a run-off so orders already placed still ship.
Patients mid-protocol are the exposure.

**No pricing lock.** Our whole margin is a percentage of an RRP they set and can change at will,
and the discount itself has no floor. Ask for 30 days' written notice on any change to RRP or
to the discount.

**Cl. 9 - set-up costs all ours, and open-ended.** 9.2 includes "any third-party fees related to
the setup process", which on its face includes their own tech agency. Tom has already said his
team cannot deliver until September. Ask for anything chargeable to be agreed in writing with a
number before it is incurred, the same wording we put into the SCEND contract.

**Cl. 10 - no service levels at all.** No dispatch time, no response time, no stock accuracy
figure. Ask for the same three we asked SCEND for: same-day dispatch on orders before a stated
cut-off, a stock accuracy number, and a support response time.

**Cl. 4 and 22 - the data protection position is not papered.** Clause 4 promises UK GDPR
compliance but there is no processor schedule: no security measures, no sub-processor control,
no breach notification, no deletion on termination. We are sending patient names, addresses and
emails from a health platform. Clause 22 is worse than absent, it is a paste from Amrita's own
customer-facing privacy notice, written as though we were the patient. It should come out and be
replaced with a proper data processing addendum. This one is Raemy's and the lawyers', not ours.

**No minimum term.** We build the integration at our own cost under clause 9 into a partner who
can walk on 30 days' notice. Ask for a 12 month initial term, or for them to carry the set-up
cost if they terminate inside 12 months.

**Cl. 1 - UK only unless agreed in writing.** Fine today. Worth knowing now, because the
white-label pitch Jesse made on 20 July was one platform across many clinics, and if any of them
are not UK this needs the written agreement first.

---

**Recommended sequence.** Correct the login answer to Tom today. Put the commercial asks
(carriage, rate, the £40 basis) to Tom as one note, since they are his to give and do not need a
lawyer. Send clauses 3.2, 6, 13, 19, 21 and the data protection gap to Raemy on his return
Tuesday. Do not sign until the carriage and the rate are settled, because those two decide
whether the line makes money at all.
