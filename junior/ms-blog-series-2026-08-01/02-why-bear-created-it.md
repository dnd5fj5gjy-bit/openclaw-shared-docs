# Why Bear Grylls and His Family Built Modern Savage

Most celebrity supplements start with a phone call from a brand. This one started in a kitchen.

## The problem the family was trying to solve

Over the past decade the Grylls family shifted the way they eat. Grass-fed red meat, eggs, bone
broth, raw milk, honey, fruit. Ancestral, in the plain sense of the word: the foods that fed human
beings for thousands of years before the food system got clever.

The change was noticeable enough that it stopped being a phase. More energy, better focus, stronger
immunity. Bear has been public about it, including about having been vegan for a period before it,
which is not a thing people usually volunteer.

But there was one part that never got easier. Organs.

Organ meat is the most nutrient-dense food on the planet, and almost nobody eats it any more. Liver,
kidney, heart, spleen. Our great-grandparents ate the whole animal because wasting it was
unthinkable. We eat the muscle and throw the rest away, and we have quietly lost most of the
nutrition in the process.

Getting a family to eat it is another matter. Children, and adults, do not want heart, lung and
bull testicles for breakfast. Neither did the Gryllses.

## The question that became the product

What if you could take the whole nutrient density of organs, freeze-dry it, and put it into
something that tastes good enough to have every day, without anyone having to look at it?

And then, since you are already making a daily blend: what if you stopped there? Or put the
collagen in it too. And the protein. And the fibre and the probiotics. And the vitamins and
minerals, from actual food rather than a laboratory. And the berries.

That is the whole origin. One pouch that replaces a shelf.

## Why they own it rather than endorse it

This is the part that keeps getting reported wrong, so it is worth being blunt.

**Modern Savage is not an endorsement. It is not a licensing deal. It is not a collaboration and
nobody paid for the Bear Grylls name.** The family owns the company. Bear, Shara, Jesse, Marmaduke
and Huckleberry.

The reason is simple. If you endorse a supplement, you do not control the formula. Somebody else
decides what goes in, what the margin is, when to cut a corner on an ingredient because the price
moved. You find out what changed at the same time your customers do.

The family were not willing to put their name on something they could not control to the
milligram, and give to their own children.

So they built it instead. Formula, sourcing, standards, packaging, pricing. All of it in-house, all
of it theirs, all of it their responsibility when it goes wrong.

## "Made for our own kitchen first"

That line is on the bottom of every page of the website and it is not a slogan, it is the test the
formula had to pass.

Every version was made, tasted and rejected in a family kitchen before it went anywhere near a
manufacturer. The artificial sweetener was in the formula and got thrown out for organic maple. The
berry blend changed. The flavour was reworked until the children would drink it without being asked
twice, because a supplement nobody finishes is a supplement that does not work.

The commercial version of this is unglamorous: it took two years and cost more than buying an
off-the-shelf blend and putting a label on it. That was the point.
