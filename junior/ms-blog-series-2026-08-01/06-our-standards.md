# Our Standards: What We Refuse to Put In, and Why

Anyone can write a list of things they do not put in a product. The list only means something if it
cost you something, so here are ours with the price attached.

## We show you the doses

Every blend in Modern Savage is declared at its milligram weight. 2,500mg of organs. 5,000mg of
collagen. 5,000mg of protein. 3,501mg of fibre and probiotics. 1,121mg of vitamins and minerals.
1,000mg of dates and berries.

The industry standard alternative is the proprietary blend: a list of impressive ingredients under
a single total, so you can put a pinch of the expensive one at the bottom and let the cheap filler
carry the weight. It is legal, it is common, and it exists for exactly one reason.

Declaring the doses means a competitor can read our formula. That is the trade and we took it.

## Grass-fed, and freeze-dried rather than heat-dried

The organs are grass-fed. Freeze-drying removes the water at low temperature instead of cooking the
material dry, which is slower and considerably more expensive per kilo. It is the difference between
concentrating food and processing it.

## Vitamins from food, not from a synthesis line

Every vitamin and mineral in the blend arrives inside something that grew: marine algae for calcium,
acerola cherry for vitamin C, fermented MK-7 for vitamin K2, unrefined sea salt for trace minerals,
plus leaves, roots and mushrooms.

A synthetic premix would have been a fraction of the cost and would have hit the same numbers on the
panel. The reason we did not use one is that a nutrient in food arrives with the other compounds it
naturally occurs alongside, and we would rather work with that than against it.

## No artificial sweeteners

There was an artificial sweetener in the formula. It worked, it was cheap, and it went in the bin.
It was replaced with organic maple sugar, which costs more and is harder to work with.

The reason is the only reason that matters in this company: the people testing it were feeding it to
their own children. A rule you enforce when it is expensive is a standard. A rule you enforce when
it is free is a preference.

## No fillers, no bulking agents, no dressing

Nothing is in the pouch to add weight, improve flow or make the scoop look fuller. If you are
paying for 23.5 grams, you are paying for 23.5 grams of ingredients that earned their place.

No artificial colours either. The colour in the powder is the eight organic berries and the date.

## Named, trademarked ingredients where the form matters

Where the specific form of an ingredient changes whether it works, we name it and pay for it:

- **LactoSpore®** Bacillus coagulans, a spore-former specifically because it survives stomach acid
  and shelf life, which most probiotic strains do not
- **Aquamin-F™** marine-algae calcium rather than rock-mined calcium carbonate
- **Fibriss®** organic baobab
- **CreaVitalis®** creatine monohydrate in the Summit Stack

Naming them means we cannot quietly substitute a cheaper equivalent later. That is the point of
naming them.

## Dosed for children means reformulated for children

Mini Savage is not the adult blend in a smaller scoop. The organ blend is rebuilt: no lung, no
testes, roughly half the dose. Vitamin D and vitamin K are lowered to sit within sensible levels
for a child. Grass-fed bovine colostrum is added, which brings milk into the product as a declared
allergen.

Running two formulas instead of one costs more at every stage, from development to the print run.
It is the whole reason the kids' product exists rather than a shrunken adult one.

## What we will not claim

We will not tell you Modern Savage cures, treats or prevents anything, because it is food and food
does not do that. We will not put a number on how you will feel in thirty days. We will not pretend
that one scoop makes an otherwise poor diet fine.

It is a food supplement. It is not a substitute for a varied and balanced diet or a healthy
lifestyle. What it does is make it realistically possible to get nutrient-dense, nose-to-tail,
whole-food nutrition into a normal week, which is difficult to do any other way in 2026.
