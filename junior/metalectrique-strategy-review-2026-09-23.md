# Metalectrique: the plan, reviewed critically

**For Jesse. Wednesday 23 September 2026. Junior.**

## The recommendation

**Yes, sign it, but do not introduce anyone until the technology has been independently tested.** The heads of terms cost you no money and lock up defence for at least a year. The real risk is not the paper. It is putting your defence contacts in front of a battery nobody independent has measured.

## What we are doing

Taking the defence rights to Trevor's aluminium-air battery. We sell into defence, choose who manufactures and pay him a royalty on what we sell. He keeps everything outside defence. No investment, no shares, no upfront fee.

## Why a licence, and not the alternatives

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| Invest (his August ask: GBP 1.5m) | Cash at risk in a company filing micro-entity accounts, whose own numbers pack disagreed with itself by about GBP 850k |
| NDA only | Protects secrets, not the opportunity. He could take your contacts' interest straight to BlueBear or Swift |
| Introduction fee | Hands him your network for a percentage and secures nothing back |
| **Licence (chosen)** | You risk time and contacts, not capital, and you own the defence channel |

## What the heads of terms give you

- **Exclusivity** in defence worldwide, from signing, for at least a year, including BlueBear, Swift and QinetiQ
- **Non-circumvention** for three years on anyone you introduce
- **Binding** on signature, not just an intention
- **Manufacturing choice** stays yours
- **A year** to agree the full contract, with the heads of terms in force until then

## The five things that could go wrong

**1. The technology may not perform outside a lab. This is the big one.**
Gilo Cardozo reviewed the spec sheet on 15 Sept and raised five problems: very low power delivery (each cell falling to about 0.24 V at 1.6 A), electrolyte freezing in sub-zero air, not enough real-world airflow, membranes drying and clogging, and whether a lithium hybrid is needed for bursts of power. The energy figures come from Metalectrique's own sheet. **Nobody independent has measured a cell.**
*What to do:* have Gilo or a lab test a pack before the first introduction. The drone pilot you agreed with Trevor on 13 Sept is the right test.

**2. His track record.**
In October 2019 he announced a multi-million-pound deal with Austin Electric for thousands of packs "starting in 2020". Nothing shipped. His consultant also described Sellafield as "commercial supply" in August when Trevor's own source called it a proof of concept. He now says he has won a GBP 130k Sellafield contract; that is unverified.
*What to do:* treat his claims as claims until you see paper.

**3. There is no patent.**
His only patent was UK-only and has lapsed. What he owns is know-how and two trade secrets (the electrolyte and a trigger mechanism). So your exclusivity is a promise from him, not a legal monopoly. Anyone who works it out independently can compete.
*What to do:* accept it. It is why the deal costs you no cash.

**4. He may not own all of it.**
His own 2019 IP audit said contractor and employment agreements were not in place, naming a contractor, Ian Jackson. If contractors never assigned their work, MAL may not own everything it is licensing.
*What to do:* put an ownership warranty in the full contract. Deliberately left out of the heads of terms so they get signed.

**5. "You choose the manufacturer" only works if he shares the know-how.**
The heads of terms give you the choice, but do not yet oblige him to hand over the recipe or supply electrolyte to another manufacturer. Until the full contract says so, in practice only he can make it.
*What to do:* this is the first thing to settle in the full contract. If you want it now, it is one added sentence.

## Two smaller points

- **Bear signs personally.** The obligations on our side are light (pay royalties, confidentiality), so the exposure is small, and the agreement can move to a company later. Raemy or CJ may still prefer a company from day one.
- **It binds.** Worth a solicitor's read before you sign, even a quick one.

## The order I would do it in

1. Send the email and heads of terms today.
2. Sign this week.
3. Build the drone pilot, and have Gilo test the pack. Gilo signs confidentiality and non-circumvention first.
4. Only then introduce defence contacts.
5. Full contract within the year: know-how sharing, electrolyte supply, ownership warranty.
