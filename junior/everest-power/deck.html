The app
Every box carries a unique 2D code printed on the filling line. The rep scans it, the shop pays by UPI against that scan, and the box is bound to a shop, a GPS point, a distributor and a timestamp that nobody can unpick later. Sell-in and sell-through on one screen, live, nationally.
Our compliance surface is about a thousand distributors, not thirteen million shops. GST is charged upstream and sits inside the printed price, so the last mile carries no paperwork at all. A street vendor stocks us because we ask nothing of him he is not already doing for Glucon-D.

What has to be true

Three numbers, and everything
rests on the first one.

Ten a day

Three boxes per outlet per month carries every scale figure in this deck, and nothing supports it yet except judgement. At five a day the year one contribution halves and the distributor economics stop working. The pilot exists for this and nothing else.

Rs 2.10 of goods

Built from wholesale trade prices, not a quotation. Sachet laminate is the volatile line and it is priced in dollars. Every 50 paise of error is 13% of our contribution. First thing to replace with a real number.

A single-brand van

Rs 58 a box is thin. Indian distributors survive on 8% because they carry twenty brands in one van. Ours will probably have to already distribute something else, and the territory map should be drawn on that basis.

Everything else in this deck is either sourced or arithmetic. These three are judgement, and they are the ones to attack first.

Appendix · decisions already taken

Four things in the original
brief that had to change

1

Settle in UPI, not stablecoins

Using crypto to pay for goods is prohibited in India, the RBI has an outright ban on the table for private stablecoins, and every transfer carries 1% TDS on top of 30% on gains. Keep the scan, the code and the audit trail. Change the rail. Build settlement swappable in case the Digital Rupee matures into a programmable state-backed alternative.

2

No caffeine

Caffeinated beverages moved into the 40% GST slab on 22 September 2025, and FSSAI has banned the words "energy drink" outright. Twenty-two points of tax, a mandatory warning label and the loss of every customer under eighteen, for an ingredient we do not need.

3

Indian Himalayan rock salt, not Himalayan pink salt

The pink salt on every shelf in the world is mined at Khewra in Pakistan, and India banned all imports from Pakistan, direct and indirect, on 2 May 2025. Real Indian Himalayan rock salt comes from Drang and Gumma in Mandi, Himachal Pradesh, the only rock salt deposit in India. Smaller story, and it is ours.

4

Bear Grylls Everest Power, never Everest Power

Everest is India's number one spice brand, Rs 2,601 crore of revenue, 400,000 outlets across 1,000 towns, which is our exact channel. Everest alone is unprotectable and picks a fight we do not need. The Bear Grylls name is the asset and Everest is a descriptor sitting under it.
