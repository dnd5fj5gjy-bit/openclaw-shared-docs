The product

Twelve grams. Ten rupees.
No fridge, no fizz, no caffeine.

What it is

Glucose and electrolyte powder in a single-serve sachet. Tear, drop into 300 to 500ml of water, drink. Citrus and nimbu-masala. The formulation is sixty years old and does not need to be clever.

What it survives

Shelf-stable for eighteen months at 45 degrees, because that is what a petrol pump in Rajasthan actually is. No cold chain, no glass, no returns logistics.

What it costs

MRP Rs 10. India's bridge-pack price point, the same as a shampoo sachet. Glucon-D already sells a sachet at exactly Rs 10, which is the proof the price point works in our category.

Where we sit on price. Electral is Rs 23 for 21.8g, the pharmacy premium. Prolyte trades around Rs 10 for 21g, the value end. At Rs 10 for 12g we are dearer per gram than Prolyte and far cheaper per gram than Electral. We are not competing on rupees per gram and should not try to. The premium is the name on the front, which is the entire thesis.

The model

Wholesale only. One distributor
per region, town and city.

We never sell to a consumer and we never run a warehouse of our own. We make powder, we sell boxes of 100, and we appoint the person in each territory who already drives a van full of somebody else's brands.

Us to distributorRs 6.63
Distributor to shopRs 7.20
Shop to customerRs 10.00
Minimum order to a shop100

Territory is drawn at 300 outlets

At 150 outlets a distributor grosses Rs 17,000 a month, which is not a business and they quit by month four. At 300 they gross Rs 52,000, which is a real job. At 500 it is Rs 115,000, which is a career. We draw the map around the number that keeps people.

Exclusive, and revocable

One territory, one distributor, exclusivity in writing, and a monthly volume floor. The app tells us who is hitting it. Exclusivity is what they are buying and it is what we take back when they under-deliver.

08
The obvious objection

How does any of this work
with a man selling off a cart?

The tax is already paid

GST is collected upstream, not on the street. We charge it when we invoice the distributor. The distributor charges it when he invoices the shop. By the time the box reaches a cart, the tax has been paid twice over and is sitting inside the printed price.

MRP is legally tax-inclusive

Under the Legal Metrology Act the printed maximum retail price includes all taxes. The vendor sells at Rs 10, keeps his margin, and calculates, collects and remits nothing. There is no till, no return, no paperwork.

He does not need to register

GST registration bites at Rs 40 lakh of turnover, roughly Rs 3.3 lakh a month. A street cart is nowhere near it. He simply cannot reclaim input credit, which costs him nothing because he bought at a price that already included the tax.

The compliance surface is about a thousand distributors, not thirteen million shops - every registered entity in the chain is one we appoint and contract. That is the point of selling wholesale only: we carry the paperwork and the last mile carries none of it. A street vendor stocks us because we ask nothing of him he is not already doing for Glucon-D.

The part worth building

Scan the box. Take the payment.
See every sale as it happens.

1. Scan

Every box of 100 carries a unique 2D code printed on the filling line. The rep scans it in the shop.

2. Collect

The shop pays by UPI against that scan. It confirms in two seconds, free, on a phone they already use.

3. Bind

The box is now permanently tied to a shop, a GPS point, a distributor and a timestamp. Nobody can unpick it later.

4. See

Sell-in and sell-through on the same screen, live, nationally. A Pune box scanned in Surat lights up the moment it happens.

On the crypto question. The instinct is right and the rail is wrong. Using crypto to pay for goods is prohibited in India, the RBI has kept an outright ban on the table over private stablecoins, and every transfer carries 1% TDS on top of a 30% tax on gains. A shopkeeper in Nashik is not opening a wallet. UPI gives us better traceability than a stablecoin would, for free, today. We build the settlement layer swappable, and if the RBI's Digital Rupee matures into a programmable state-backed rail, that is where this idea comes back legally.

Four decisions that change the numbers

What I would change
about the brief

1

Settle in UPI, not stablecoins

Crypto payment for goods is prohibited in India and the RBI has an outright ban on the table for private stablecoins. Keep the scan, the app and the audit trail. Change the rail. UPI does the job better, free, on a phone the shop already owns.

2

No caffeine

Caffeinated beverages moved into the 40% GST slab on 22 September 2025. Uncaffeinated electrolyte powder sits at 18% or lower. Twenty-two points of tax, plus a mandatory warning label, plus the loss of every customer under eighteen, for an ingredient we do not need.

3

Indian Himalayan rock salt, not Himalayan pink salt

The pink salt on every shelf in the world is mined at Khewra in Pakistan, and India banned all imports from Pakistan, direct and indirect, on 2 May 2025. We cannot legally use it. Real Indian Himalayan rock salt is mined at Drang and Gumma in Mandi, Himachal Pradesh, the only rock salt deposit in India. Smaller story, and it is ours.

4

Bear Grylls Everest Power, never Everest Power

Everest is India's number one spice brand, Rs 2,601 crore of revenue, 400,000 outlets across 1,000 towns. That is our exact channel. Everest on its own is unprotectable and picks a fight we do not need. The Bear Grylls name is the asset; Everest is a descriptor sitting under it.

Manufacturing

Made in India, on somebody
else's line

Third-party contract manufacture, not our own plant. India has deep, cheap, licensed capacity in ORS and form-fill-seal sachets sitting idle. We bring the formula, the artwork and the volume. We bring no capital expenditure at all.

Site it in the west or centre of the country, near a port and near the freight corridors, so we are not paying to move powder across India before the business starts.

What we ask for before anything is real

· Quoted price per sachet at 1m, 10m and 100m units

· FSSAI licence number, verified at source

· Stability trial at 45 degrees and 75% humidity

· Confirmation they can print a unique 2D code per box on the line

That last one is not standard, and the entire app depends on it. Ask it first, not last.

Cost of goods in this model is Rs 2.10 a sachet, built from trade prices rather than a quote. A 21.8g ORS sachet trades at Rs 4.65 to Rs 6.00 all-in on the Indian wholesale exchanges, which already carries the manufacturer's margin and the trader's, so Rs 2.10 for 12g at volume is defensible. It is still an estimate and it moves the whole model, so it is the first thing to replace with a real number.

What I am least sure about

The three numbers most
likely to be wrong

Ten a day

Three boxes per outlet per month carries every scale figure in this deck and nothing supports it yet except judgement. If it is five a day rather than ten, the year-one contribution halves and the distributor economics stop working. The pilot exists for this.

Rs 2.10 of COGS

Built from wholesale trade prices, not a quotation. Sachet laminate is the volatile line and it is priced in dollars. Every 50 paise of error is 13% of our contribution.

A single-brand distributor

Rs 58 a box is thin, and the reason Indian FMCG distributors survive on 8% is that they carry twenty brands in one van. A distributor carrying only us may not be viable, in which case we either widen the margin or accept that our distributor already distributes something else. I lean towards the second.

Everything else in this deck is either sourced or arithmetic. These three are judgement, and they are the ones to attack first.
