# Bear Grylls Everest Power
## Financial model, downside and set-up cost

**For Raemy Singh, CFO. Version 2, 11 August 2026.**
*Supersedes the 10 August version. Three things in it were wrong and are corrected below.*

Everything here is an estimate. **No manufacturer has quoted and no distributor has signed.**
That is not a caveat on the model, it is the model's main finding: the two numbers that decide this
business are the two we do not yet own.

---

### What changed since the 10 August version, and why

**1. The exchange rate was wrong, and every pound figure with it.** The model carried 106 rupees to
the pound. The real rate on 10 August was **128.70** (ECB reference series, verified at source
11 August). Everything denominated in rupees was therefore reported about 21 per cent too high in
pounds. Year one contribution at the base case falls from GBP 2.08m to **GBP 1.71m**. Nothing about
the business changed; the reporting was wrong. All three model files now import one rate from one
file so they cannot drift apart again.

**2. The rate of sale was not applied consistently.** The volume correction of 10 August, from 10
sachets per outlet per day to 2.5, had been applied to the downside but not to the unit economics or
to the pilot sizing. Both now run on 2.5, with 10 shown as a labelled bull case.

**3. The app is no longer a cost.** Jesse has confirmed Felix builds the unique-code system and
retailer app in house. The GBP 20,000 line is out, so there is now **one set-up number, not two**.

Cost of goods build, Rs 2.10: powder 1.15, laminate and fill 0.55, outer box and carton 0.22, inward
freight and wastage 0.18. **This is the number nobody has quoted.** A full formulation specification

**GST classification is worth up to 13 points.** Proprietary food (HSN 2106) at 18 per cent is the

than Rs 6.63, but FSSAI has banned the word ORS on the label of a product that is not one, so we do
not plan on it. A caffeinated classification would cost us, and there is no caffeine in the product.

### 2. Volume: the correction, restated

The pitch assumed **10 sachets a day in each of 60,000 outlets**. The 60,000 outlets are conservative,
being 0.5 per cent of India's 13m kirana shops. **The rate of sale is roughly four times too high as a
twelve-month average.**

At 10 a day, year one revenue is Rs 143 crore. That is **25 per cent of Electral**, India's number one
ORS brand, selling since the 1970s, and 22 to 27 per cent of the entire Rs 800 to 1,000 crore ORS
category in our first year. It passes the shopkeeper test in a good outlet in May. It does not survive
as the average of 60,000 shops over twelve months.

**Planning case: 2.5 sachets per outlet per day = 4.5m sachets a month in year one.**

| At 2.5/outlet/day | Outlets | Sachets/month | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot, one state | 5,000 | 0.4m | GBP 0.14m/yr |
| Year 1, five states | 60,000 | 4.5m | **GBP 1.71m/yr** |
| Year 3, national | 400,000 | 30.0m | GBP 11.40m/yr |

One thing the lower forecast does not change: **we plan conservatively and buy capacity
optimistically.** The factories have been asked for **peak** monthly capacity rather than average,
because this product sells into the Indian summer. On our own numbers a normal seasonal curve is
roughly 108 tonnes in the peak month against 22 in the trough, and a line sized to the average cannot
serve that. Specifying the headroom costs nothing today.

### 4. What would actually move these numbers, in order

1. **The manufacturer's price.** Rs 2.10 is our estimate. Every 50 paise of error is 12 per cent of
   contribution at the plan margin. Five factories have been approached, one has replied and none has
   quoted. This is the critical path and nothing else in the model matters as much.
2. **What the distributor takes.** 8 per cent is assumed, not agreed. At 8 per cent a distributor
   earns Rs 0.58 a sachet and cannot live on us alone in year one, which is why we want to be one line
   in an existing distributor's book rather than have a dedicated one.
3. **GST classification.** Covered above. The prudent read is already taken.

| Subtotal | 51,985 |
| Contingency at 15% | 7,798 |
| **Total** | **59,783** |

**Seven of these fourteen lines are requests for quotation rather than quotes**, so treat the total as
an order of magnitude and not a commitment. The only committed spend to date is the GBP 759 trade mark
instruction.

**One line deserves a flag, and it is a working capital point rather than a cost.** The first
production run is 500,000 sachets, GBP 12,606, and it is sized by the factory's minimum order and by
the minimum on a printed laminate, not by the pilot. At the corrected rate of sale the pilot of 300
outlets over 90 days consumes about **67,500 sachets**. So roughly **432,500 sachets, about GBP
10,900 of stock, sits in a warehouse when the pilot ends.** Shelf life is 18 months so it is not a
write-off, and it is the stock the first real distributor would sell. But it should be understood as
cash tied up rather than cost incurred, and the true figure will not be known until a factory states
its minimum order quantity. None has.

Two costs the in-house app does not remove. The per-box unique code is a **printing** requirement on
the manufacturer and has to be written into the manufacturing specification. And collecting by UPI
needs the Indian entity and a payment provider before a single rupee moves.

### 6. Where the trade mark stands

Intepat is instructed at **GBP 759** for the search and filing across classes 5, 30 and 32, and their
search opinion arrived on 11 August. Classes 5 and 32 both come back low to medium risk and both say
proceed. Two points for you.

**The class 30 report has not been delivered.** Their covering email names the file and it was not
attached. Class 30 is the food class the well-known EVEREST business sits in, and is the reason we
commissioned the search. Chased.

**The opinion is conditioned on an assumption they did not verify.** Both reports state that they
proceed on the understanding that the subsisting BEAR GRYLLS registrations in India in classes 5, 30
and 32 are owned by the applicant or an associated proprietor, and that written consent under section
14 of the Indian Trade Marks Act can be obtained for the personal name. We have asked them to identify
those registrations and the proprietor of record rather than assume it.

Two smaller points that carry cost. Both reports recommend filing a stylised logo alongside the word
mark, which is not in the budget above and cannot be done until the pack design is settled. And the
Madrid route is unavailable because there is no UK application for this mark, so we either file in the
Indian company on incorporation or file in the UK entity now and assign later. **That last point is
useful: it means the trade mark no longer waits on the entity decision.**
