## Product and manufacturing specification, draft 1

**Date:** 11 August 2026
**Status:** Draft for manufacturer comment. Two lines are not yet locked and are marked OPEN.
**Confidential.** Issue only under executed non-disclosure agreement.

### 1. What this document is

Every factory we have approached has our product description. None of them has a formulation, which is
why none of them has priced it. This is the sheet a contract manufacturer needs in order to quote a
number rather than a range.

Everything here is a target with a tolerance, not an instruction. We expect the manufacturer's R&D to
come back on it. What we do not expect to move is section 2, the regulatory position, and the 12 gram
fill weight the entire commercial model is built on.

---

### 2. Regulatory position, and the two things that constrain the formula

**This is a proprietary food, not an oral rehydration salt.** The pack will not carry the letters ORS,
will not carry the WHO formula, and will not make a rehydration therapy claim. FSSAI does not permit
"ORS" on the label of a product that is not one, and our own best pack mockup currently prints it, which
has to be corrected before artwork goes anywhere.

**The only function claim we can make runs off the vitamin, not the electrolytes.** Under FSSAI a
nutrient function claim requires a significant amount of the nutrient per serving, conventionally 15
per cent of the Indian RDA. So the sachet carries vitamin C at a level that clears that bar, and any
immunity or wellbeing wording is attached to the vitamin C and to nothing else. It is never attached to
the salts, and never phrased as resistance to disease.

**The manufacturer or our Indian regulatory adviser must confirm the exact RDA figure and the permitted
claim wording before artwork.** We have specified the vitamin at a level intended to clear 15 per cent
with room for degradation over shelf life, but the number on the label is a regulatory question, not a
formulation one.

### 4. Target formulation, per 12 g sachet

| Ingredient | Target | Tolerance | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dextrose monohydrate | 10.05 g | balance to fill | Carbohydrate base, carrier, sodium co-transport |
| Sodium chloride | 700 mg | ± 5% | Sodium |
| Trisodium citrate dihydrate | 350 mg | ± 5% | Sodium, buffer, mouthfeel |
| Potassium chloride | 300 mg | ± 5% | Potassium |
| Citric acid, anhydrous | 400 mg | ± 10% | Acidity, flavour lift |
| Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) | 18 mg | to deliver ≥ 12 mg at end of shelf life | The claim |
| Flavour, powder | 90 mg | to sensory | See section 6 |
| Sucralose | 25 mg | to sensory | Sweetness, see section 8 |
| Anticaking agent (silicon dioxide or tricalcium phosphate) | 60 mg | ± 20% | Flow, moisture |

**Resulting finished drink at 400 ml**

| | Target | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium | approx. 355 mg per sachet, about 39 mmol/l | Above a sports drink, below an ORS |
| Potassium | approx. 157 mg per sachet, about 10 mmol/l | |
| Glucose | approx. 9.1 g per sachet, about 2.3% | |
| Osmolality | **target 200 to 240 mOsm/kg** | Deliberately hypotonic |
| Energy | approx. 37 kcal per sachet | |
| pH | 3.4 to 4.0 | |

**The design decision behind those numbers, so you can argue with it.** The drink is deliberately
hypotonic. Plasma sits near 285 mOsm/kg, so a drink below that empties from the stomach and crosses the
gut wall faster, which is the honest version of "hydrates quickly" and is defensible without a
therapeutic claim. The cost is sweetness and body, since there is less sugar in it than a consumer
expects at this price. That is what the sucralose is doing.

Raising the dextrose would improve taste and the energy story and push osmolality up towards isotonic.
We would rather stay hypotonic, but we want the manufacturer's view, particularly on how the two
options taste after four hours in a plastic bottle in 40 degree heat, which is how this product will
actually be drunk.

### 5. Raw material requirements

- All ingredients food grade, FSSAI compliant, with certificates of analysis per batch.
- No animal derived ingredients. The product should be suitable for vegetarians and carry the green dot.
- No caffeine, no added preservative, no artificial colour.
- Sodium and potassium salts to pharmacopoeial grade where the price difference is immaterial.
- Declare the origin of the flavour and the sweetener. We will want to second source both.

---

### 6. Flavour — OPEN

Two profiles are in contention and this is a decision for us, not for the factory, but we want your
view before we take it.

**A. Lime and salt.** Culturally native, sits alongside nimbu pani, and carries the salt level in
section 4 without the salt reading as a fault. Our default.

**B. Orange.** Broader appeal, familiar from the existing electrolyte category in India, and masks
salt less well.

Please quote against A, note any price difference for B, and tell us which you think travels better at
this price point and in Indian heat and humidity. Please supply samples of both at the target
formulation for a blind sip test.

---

### 7. Quality, stability and shelf life

- **Target shelf life: 18 months** from manufacture, unopened.
- **Stability trial required at 45°C and 75% relative humidity.** We need vitamin C retention, moisture
  pickup, caking, colour and sensory over the trial. If you hold existing data on a comparable
  electrolyte powder in a similar laminate, send it, it may save us the wait.
- Moisture content at fill: state your target and your in process limit.
- Microbiological and heavy metal limits to FSSAI standard, stated in the quotation.
- Fill weight control: state your tolerance and how it is verified in line. At Rs 10 retail, systematic
  overfill is a direct margin loss and we will audit it.

---

### 8. Three levers on cost, which we would like priced separately

The Rs 2.10 target is ours, not yours, and no one has yet told us whether it is real. These are the
three places we think it moves, and we would rather see them itemised than buried in one number.

1. **Sucralose.** At the inclusion above it is one of the larger ingredient lines in a product that is
   mostly cheap sugar. Please price the formulation as specified, and also a variant where part of the
   dextrose is replaced with sucrose and the sucralose is reduced or removed. Sucrose is sweeter per
   gram and much cheaper, but it costs us osmolality against a fixed 12 gram fill.
2. **The laminate.** Please break out the packaging cost per sachet separately from the powder, with
   your recommended structure, and tell us what a lower cost structure would cost us in shelf life.
3. **Fill weight.** Price at 12 g. Also state the delta per gram, so we can see what section 9 costs us.

---

### 9. The two open decisions

**Fill weight.** The model, the margin and the price ladder are all built on **12 g**. Several of the
pack concepts in circulation print NET WT 21 g. At a fixed Rs 10 retail that difference is most of the
margin. It has to be closed before artwork, and 12 g is the recommendation.

**Flavour.** Section 6. Gates the formula, the lab work and the artwork.

Neither is a manufacturing question. Both sit with Jesse.

---

### 10. The coding requirement, restated because it is not standard

Every outer box carries a **unique machine readable code, applied in line at the point of packing, no
two boxes the same**. A retailer scans it to claim an incentive, so the codes must be reconciled to a
batch record we can receive as a file.

This is not decoration and it is not negotiable. It is the anti fraud instrument the distribution model
rests on. Please state plainly whether your line can do this today, what it costs per box, and if it
cannot, exactly what you would need. Three of the five manufacturers we approached have not addressed
this question at all.

---

### 11. What we need back from you

1. Your FSSAI licence number and the name it is held in.
2. Capacity, stated as tonnes a month **and** as sachets a month at a 12 g fill, and your **peak**
   capacity rather than your comfortable one. Year one at plan is 18 million sachets a month, which is
   216 tonnes a month at 12 g.
3. Minimum order quantity and lead time from artwork approval to dispatch.
4. **A price ladder**: per sachet at 1 million, 10 million and 100 million sachets a year, stating
   clearly what is in and what is out, with powder and packaging separated.
5. Your answer on the unique code, section 10.
6. Whether you will run the stability trial in section 7 and what it costs.
7. Samples of both flavour profiles.

We would start with a paid pilot batch before committing to volume.
