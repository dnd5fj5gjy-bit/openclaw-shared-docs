# Commenda call, Wednesday 12 August, 14:00 UK
## What to say, in order

**Who is on it:** Manya, with Chad and Spencer copied. Commenda is one of three firms quoted for
incorporating the Indian subsidiary. It is the only one that engaged on substance, FSSAI licensing
tiers, rather than just asking for a slot.

**What you now have that you did not have on Monday:** KNM's written quote, arrived 11 August. That is
the number to hold Commenda against. Do not open with it.

---

### The one thing to get out of this call

**A written fixed fee for a pre-revenue Indian subsidiary, split into one-off and annual.** Not a rate
card, not a range, not "it depends". If you leave the call without a number in writing you have had a
sales call.

---

### Open with this

> "We are incorporating a wholly owned subsidiary in India for a consumer product business. Before we
> talk about anything else, I want to be clear about what we are: a company with no revenue, no
> employees and no premises, that will place one manufacturing order and sell through one distributor.
> Price it for that, not for a trading company. What does that cost to set up, and what does it cost
> to run for twelve months?"

That framing is the whole call. Every number that comes back should be tested against it.

---

### The three things to strip, if they price them

These are in KNM's quote and we should not be buying them in year one.

1. **Payroll services.** KNM priced a minimum of five employees. We have none.
2. **Transfer pricing documentation.** The full study is threshold-driven and we will be far below it.
   A Form 3CEB certificate may still be needed if there is any related-party transaction. Buy the
   certificate, not the study.
3. **Import Export Code.** IEC is for importing or exporting. Everest Power is made in India and sold
   in India. Not needed until we export.

Also question **Shop and Establishment registration**, which registers a place of business, when we
will not have an office.

### The one thing not to strip

**Dematerialisation and ISIN.** A wholly owned subsidiary of a foreign body corporate is not a small
company, so shares probably have to be held in dematerialised form. KNM priced it at USD 1,500 across
the demat account and the ISIN. Ask Commenda to confirm whether it applies to us and what they charge.
If they say it does not apply, get that in writing, because KNM says it does.

---

### The four questions, in this order

1. **"What is the fixed fee to incorporate, all in, including name approval, digital signatures,
   directors' identification, the SPICe filing, PAN and TAN, and the FDI and FC-GPR reporting to the
   Reserve Bank?"** One number.
2. **"What is the annual fee to keep it compliant with no trading activity?"** Then, separately, what
   changes when we start trading. Two numbers, not one blended one.
3. **"What is your resident director service, per year?"** KNM is USD 3,600. That is the benchmark and
   it is fair. If Commenda is much higher, that is the tell on the rest of their pricing.
4. **"Do you price the FSSAI central licence, and if so what is it?"** Nobody has quoted this. KNM
   advised us on the licence structure and then left it out of their fee proposal entirely.

### Where the numbers should land

| | KNM quoted | What we should be paying |
|---|---|---|
| One-off incorporation | GBP 6,627 | around GBP 3,500 |
| Resident director, per year | GBP 2,666 | GBP 2,666, this one is fair |
| Annual accounting, tax, audit | GBP 13,481 | around GBP 5,000 |
| **Year one total** | **GBP 22,774** | **around GBP 11,000** |

The original budget of GBP 11,000 was not the thing that was wrong. KNM's scope was. An Indian private
limited must be audited whatever its size, must file with the Registrar of Companies, must file a
corporate tax return and an FLA return to the Reserve Bank. That is real and it is not free. Bookkeeping
at GBP 3,730 a year for a company with almost no transactions is not.

### If they will not put a number in writing

Say this, and mean it:

> "We have a written fixed fee from another firm. I am not going to run a process where one firm quotes
> in writing and another quotes on a call. Send me the fee in an email after this and we will decide
> between the two."

Three firms have now tried to convert a written quote into a call. Two of them we have already put on
the back burner.

---

### One thing to be careful about

Do not describe the product, the formulation or the launch timing in detail. The formula specification
is confidential and there is no non-disclosure agreement with Commenda. "A consumer product business"
and "a powder sold in sachets" is as far as it needs to go for a pricing conversation.
