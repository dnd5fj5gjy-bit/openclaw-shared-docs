Bear, CJ, Raemy,

An update on BGEP, the India electrolyte sachet. Jesse's idea, four days old, and it has moved further than that sounds. Short version: the price point is genuine white space, the volume assumption in the original plan was too high and has been corrected, and the first factory has come to the table this morning.

The product. A 12g electrolyte and energy sachet at Rs 10, made in India, sold wholesale into kirana shops, street vendors and petrol pumps. Bear's face on the front.

What is good, and it is better than we assumed. Rs 10 for 12g is empty space. The category prices at Rs 1.06 per gram without exception: Electral 21.8g at Rs 23.05, Cipla Prolyte 21g at Rs 22.26. At Rs 10 for 12g we are Rs 0.83 per gram, 22% cheaper than every incumbent, and we hit an impulse price their pack sizes cannot reach.

What we corrected in our own plan. The plan assumed ten sachets a day in every one of 60,000 outlets. The 60,000 outlets are fine, that is 0.5% of India's kirana. Ten a day as a twelve month average is around four times too high. At that rate year one is Rs 143 crore of revenue, which is a quarter of Electral, India's number one ORS brand since the 1970s at Rs 562 crore, and 22 to 27% of the entire Indian ORS category. Nothing in Indian FMCG has done that from a standing start. We are planning on 2.5 a day: 4.5m sachets a month and about GBP 2.1m of contribution a year. A quarter of what the deck promised, still a good business, and it implies a smaller factory.

Manufacturing is the critical path and it moved today. Five factories have our specification. Hindustan Foods, via The Vanity Case Group, came back yesterday willing to quote. Sanjiv Chatterji, who heads their food and drink division, has handed it to Robin, his head of business development. Our confidentiality agreement went to them this morning and we have offered calls Tuesday, Wednesday or Thursday. No price yet from anyone. Our Rs 2.10 cost of goods is an estimate and every 50 paise of error is 12% of contribution, so the first real quote is the number that decides this.

Raemy, that agreement is your paper adapted for a factory, out in your name as CFO on BGV Global Limited, unsigned on our side. It comes back to you to counter-sign, so you are not surprised by it.

The company. It has to be an Indian company, because a foreign company cannot hold an FSSAI licence and whoever is named on the pack needs one. The real constraint is not cost, it is the resident director: Indian law needs one director physically in India for 182 days of the Indian financial year, so neither Bear nor Jesse can be it. That service is buyable, so it is a cost rather than a blocker. We have a call with Commenda on Wednesday at 14:00 UK to price incorporation properly. We are assuming a central FSSAI licence from day one because we sell in several states.

Trade mark. Intepat have quoted about USD 1,025 to clear and file all three classes in India. That felt like a gate and it costs eight hundred pounds. Two other firms are still to quote.

Getting the money home. The Indian company earns rupees, pays Indian tax, and distributes to us as dividends. The rupee is freely convertible for that, so the cost is tax rather than currency: Indian corporation tax and then dividend withholding capped by the India and UK treaty. Raemy, we do not want to quote that rate from memory, so it needs an Indian adviser to confirm. The stablecoin idea is out. In June the Enforcement Directorate raided five Bengaluru crypto payment firms over roughly Rs 2,500 crore of unauthorised cross border stablecoin transfers, and on 2 July the RBI told a Parliamentary committee that digital assets should not be legalised. UPI does the job on the sales side for nothing.

Bear, your contact came back with three questions on 7 August and here are the answers. On partner category, it is an existing food and FMCG distributor's van and order book rather than a drinks specialist or pharmacy, because 90% of Indian FMCG moves through kirana. At our margin no distributor can live on us alone, he would gross around Rs 43,000 a month against Rs 1.5 to 2 lakh of van, driver, godown and salesmen, so we need to be one line in an established distributor's book, not a dedicated one. On pricing, compare us to sachet rehydration and glucose powders, Electral, Prolyte and Glucon-D, not to energy drinks. That is Rs 10 against Rs 22 to 23 for the incumbent pack. He said his own group is not in the right zip code but offered to think about who is, so those answers are worth sending back to him.
