Bear, CJ, Raemy,

An update on BGEP, our India electrolyte sachet. Four days old and further along than that sounds.

The product: 12g electrolyte sachet at Rs 10, made in India, sold wholesale into kirana shops, street vendors and petrol pumps.

How we intend to own it. We build this ourselves and we keep it. The factory is contract manufacturing on our formula, distribution is contracted region by region, and the brand and the company stay with us. We are not licensing the name out for somebody else to run. We would also rather fund it ourselves than take money in, so outside investment is a maybe at best and only if it arrives with distribution attached.

So the one thing worth asking Indian contacts for is distribution. Introductions to established food and FMCG distributors with vans, godowns and an existing order book. Not an operator to hand the brand to.

The good news. Rs 10 for 12g is empty space. The category prices at Rs 1.06 a gram without exception (Electral 21.8g at Rs 23.05, Prolyte 21g at Rs 22.26). We would be at Rs 0.83 a gram, 22% cheaper than every incumbent, at a price their pack sizes cannot reach.

The correction. Our own plan assumed ten sachets a day in each of 60,000 shops. The 60,000 shops are realistic. Ten a day as a year-long average is about four times too high, because it would put us at a quarter of Electral, India's number one since the 1970s, inside twelve months. We are now planning on 2.5 a day, which is 4.5m sachets a month and roughly GBP 2.1m of contribution a year. A quarter of the original number, still a good business.

What the lower forecast does not change. We are planning conservatively and buying capacity optimistically. The factories have been asked to price at 1.5m, 6m and 18m sachets a month, so the top of that ladder is still four times our planning case, and we are asking for peak monthly capacity rather than an average because this product sells into the Indian summer. A normal seasonal curve on our own numbers is around 108 tonnes in the peak month against 22 in the trough, and a line sized to the average cannot serve that. We would rather hold a supply chain that can fill shelves if this runs hot than explain empty ones. It costs nothing to specify the headroom now, while we are not paying for a dedicated line.

Manufacturing, which is the critical path. Hindustan Foods, via The Vanity Case Group, came back yesterday willing to quote, and their head of food and drink has put his business development lead on it. Our confidentiality agreement went over this morning with three call slots this week. Nobody has given us a price yet, and that price decides everything: our Rs 2.10 cost of goods is an estimate and every 50 paise of error is 12% of contribution.

Raemy, that agreement is your paper adapted for a factory, out in your name on BGV Global Limited, unsigned on our side. It comes back to you to counter-sign.

Everyone we have approached so far, so you have the full picture.

Manufacturers, all sent our specification. Sonfus, GDM Nutra and SevenQ Nutrition on 7 August, chased on 9 August, none has replied. Halewood Laboratories and The Vanity Case Group, which owns Hindustan Foods, on 9 August. Vanity Case is the one that came back.

Incorporation and FSSAI, three firms on 7 August. Commenda have replied and we have a call on Wednesday at 14:00 UK. KNM India have replied and want a call before a number. Dezan Shira have replied and are engaged. We have held all three to a written fixed fee before any sales call, because three calls before three quotes loses the comparison.

Trade mark, three firms on 7 August. Intepat have quoted, about USD 1,025 to clear and file all three classes. S. S. Rana sent credentials and promised fees today. Anand and Anand have not replied.

The company. It has to be Indian, because a foreign company cannot hold an FSSAI licence and whoever is on the pack needs one. The real constraint is the resident director: one director must be in India 182 days a year, so it cannot be Bear or Jesse. That is buyable, so it is a cost not a blocker.

Getting money out. The Indian company pays Indian tax and sends profit up as dividends, so the cost is tax, not currency. Raemy, we need an Indian adviser on the treaty rate rather than a number from memory. The stablecoin idea is dead: the Enforcement Directorate raided five crypto payment firms in June over Rs 2,500 crore of cross-border stablecoin transfers, and the RBI told Parliament on 2 July that digital assets should not be legalised. UPI does the job for nothing.

On distribution, so anyone asking gets the same answer from all of us: we want to be one line in an established distributor's book, not a dedicated distributor of our own. At our margin nobody can live on us alone in the first year, and 90% of Indian FMCG moves through kirana shops that these vans already serve every week.

What we need now.

The dividend position on bringing Indian profit back, confirmed by an Indian adviser rather than quoted from memory.

Nothing needs money yet. The field test in the original plan was costed at GBP 50k and most of it has been answered from the desk. Full workings available if you want them.
