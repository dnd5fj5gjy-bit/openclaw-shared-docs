## The regulatory position

### 1. The pouches are food, not therapeutic goods

The instrument that decides this is the *Therapeutic Goods (Declared Goods) Amendment (Sports
Supplements) Order 2020*, in force since 30 November 2020. It pulls a sports supplement into
medicine regulation only if the product is:

- presented as a **tablet, pill or capsule**, or
- represented as improving **sport, exercise or recreational performance** *and* containing a
  scheduled poison, a WADA Prohibited List substance, or a named "relevant substance".

Modern Savage is a **powder scooped into liquid**, which is a traditional food form, and it is sold
as a daily wholefood blend rather than a sports performance product. It fails both triggers. TGA's
own position is that supplements containing only food ingredients, presented as foods, continue to
be sold as food at retail.

The market confirms it. Beef organ supplements sell openly through Australian health food retail as
foods under the Food Standards Code, with no ARTG listing.

**So: no Australian sponsor, no ARTG listing, no TGA GMP clearance of the Florida factory.** The
whole gate I previously flagged does not apply to the two pouches.

The live compositional question is the added vitamins. Vitamin C, D3 and K2 at our doses are
constrained by the Food Standards Code, and the likely home is Standard 2.9.4, formulated
supplementary sports foods, which permits additions that ordinary foods cannot carry. That is a
label and dose question for an Australian food consultant, not a barrier.

### 2. The border is open, which is the opposite of the UK

This is the real surprise. The UK route is blocked because our Florida factory is not GB-listed for
meat products. Australia does not work that way.

**FSANZ assessed the United States as BSE Category 1 in May 2015**, the top category. New Zealand
is also Category 1, assessed November 2011. Category 1 and 2 countries are eligible to export beef
and beef products to Australia subject to certification. Countries outside those categories cannot
export at all.

So US-made Modern Savage **can** legally enter Australia. It needs a biosecurity import permit
through BICON, US government health certification, and it will be subject to border inspection as a
risk food. That is process and cost, not a wall.

The NZ-origin testes and lung already in our formula are helpful here, not a complication.

### 3. Summit Stack is the exception, and it cannot go as a food

Summit Stack contains **NMN**, and Australia treats NMN as a therapeutic ingredient, not a food
ingredient.

TGA approved NMN as a permitted ingredient for listed complementary medicines on **10 December
2025**, with conditions: oral only, maximum 500mg a day, twelve weeks or less, adults only, not in
pregnancy.

The sting is commercial. The approval was granted to **SyncoZymes with two years of market
exclusivity, to 10 December 2027**. Until then, an NMN product in Australia has to use their
material or wait.

**Recommendation: leave Summit Stack out of the Australasian launch.** Take the two pouches in as
food, and revisit Summit Stack after December 2027 or reformulate it without NMN for that market.
Dragging it along converts a straightforward food launch into a listed-medicine project.

## Who is already winning, and why it argues for local manufacture

**Ancestral Nutrition** is the incumbent. Byron Bay operation, freeze-dries Tasmanian grass-fed
organs in small batches, sells single organs and blends in capsules and powders, and claims over
**550 Australian retail doors**. NXGEN Wholefoods runs a similar play on certified organic South
Australian beef.

They are not beating imports on price. They are beating them on provenance. In a country that farms
some of the best grass-fed beef on earth, "American organ powder" is a hard sell to exactly the
customer who buys this category.

Local manufacture is available off the shelf. **Australian Capsuling Co** does contract and private
label beef organ capsules and bulk powder. **Gembra Health** wholesales bulk organ powders sourced
from Australian and New Zealand pasture-raised cattle. The supply chain we would need already exists
and already serves this category.

**The market is worth the effort:** Australian dietary supplements ran about **USD 3.9bn in 2025**,
growing around 7% a year.

## What to offer a partner

Not a distribution agreement. A licence.

- They take the brand for Australia and New Zealand, exclusive, with a term and minimum volumes.
- They own the entity, the manufacturing, the regulatory compliance, the warehousing and the sales.
- They make Modern Savage locally to our specification, from Australian and New Zealand organs.
- We provide the brand, Bear, the formula, marketing, and funding support where it accelerates them.
- We take a royalty on net sales rather than a manufacturing margin.

That is a business a good operator will want. Per-parcel logistics is not.

**One thing to resolve first.** Our formula specification has gaps that block anyone from making it
anywhere. The signed sheets do not define the "21 Nutrient Multi Blend", do not define the "8 Berry
Blend", and carry **no weights at all on the excipients**, which is 22.9% of the adult serving and
essentially the entire sugar declaration. A licensee cannot make or label the product without that.
It is the same blocker as the UK manufacturing route, and it is one email to Andrew Kouvel.

## Confidence, and the one thing to pay for

The classification conclusion is well-evidenced. It rests on the text of the 2020 Order, TGA's own
guidance, and the plain fact that competing products sell as foods in Australian retail today. I am
confident in it.

I am not an Australian regulatory consultant, and this is a document to act on, not to file. Before

That is a few thousand dollars and a couple of weeks, and it should run in parallel with the partner
conversations, not before them.
