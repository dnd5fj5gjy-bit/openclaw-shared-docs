# Modern Savage: what the pre-order money is actually worth

**2 August 2026. Written the night after pre-orders opened.**

---

## The five lines

1. **The money is good money.** A US founding order carries roughly $35 to $41 of contribution on a single pouch, and $75 to $102 on the three-product bundle, across every cost per pouch we could plausibly be paying. Nothing about the pricing is broken.
2. **The UK half of it is not ours yet.** We are taking UK payments today against a full-refund promise, and there is currently no lawful route to put a UK pouch in a UK customer's hands in October. Until there is, that money is a liability sitting in the bank, not revenue.
3. **SCEND asked us a blocking question three days ago and nobody has answered it.** They cannot ship without our UK VAT number and we cannot import without an EORI number. It is the cheapest item on this page to fix and it is the one holding up the rest.
4. **The founding page has to sell the bundle, not the pouch.** Eight hundred bundles pays for the whole production run with about thirty three thousand dollars spare. Eight hundred single pouches leaves us about forty six thousand short. Same customers, same stock, same scarce adult pouches.
5. **The model everyone is working from is fifteen percent light on US revenue.** It still prices the US subscription at $59.99. It is $69.

**One line to say out loud:** "We are collecting UK money we cannot yet ship against. Get the VAT and EORI numbers moving this week, and put the bundle in front of people on the founding page."

---

## The problem worth leading with

The campaign that went out on Friday takes payment immediately, promises delivery around October, and offers a full refund at any point before the product ships. It also says, in plain terms, that we deliver to the UK and the US.

For the US that is a normal pre-order. For the UK it is a promise resting on four things, and I cannot confirm that we hold any of them.

We need a contracted UK manufacturer. My last record has three suppliers at NDA stage on 13 July, and nothing since. The site says made in the UK on the pouch artwork, so someone may well have signed something I have not seen, but the claim is currently unsupported in anything I can read. We need a UK VAT number, because as an overseas seller holding stock in the UK we are a non established taxable person and the registration threshold is zero. We need an EORI number to import the goods at all. And we need food business operator registration plus a UK address on the back of the pack, which is a separate print run from the US artwork.

Jack Crumpton at SCEND put the first two of those to us directly on 30 July. He was clear that they can onboard us without the VAT number but cannot ship a single order until it exists. That message has sat unanswered while the campaign went live and started collecting UK cash.

This is not a compliance footnote. It is the difference between the UK pre orders being revenue and being a refund queue. A refunded order does not come back clean either, because the card processing fee is not returned. Every UK pound we take before this is resolved is a pound we might have to give back, having paid to receive it.

Worth being fair about the counter case. Pre selling ahead of a supply chain being fully papered is completely normal, the ship date has a deliberate buffer built into it, and we have until roughly the end of September to close all four gaps. Nothing here is a crisis today. It becomes one only if the gaps are still open in four weeks, or if UK order volume gets big enough that unwinding it would be embarrassing rather than merely annoying. The reason to move now is that a VAT registration is a form, and a manufacturer contract is not. They have very different lead times and only one of them can be fixed in an afternoon.

## What an order is actually worth

The good news is that the pricing works comfortably. On a single subscribed pouch we keep a little over half of the net price after processing, pick and pack, courier and cost of goods. On the three product bundle we keep a similar proportion of a much larger number. Even at the top of the cost range, where a pouch costs us $27 to make, a US bundle still contributes more than seventy five dollars.

Two things are worth pulling out of that.

The US earns more than the UK, but by less than we have been assuming. Net of VAT, a UK subscription at £55 is worth about eleven percent less than a US subscription at $69, not the sixteen percent in my own ledger. Sterling has moved and the gap has narrowed. It is still a real gap and it still argues for the US being the priority market, which is where Bear landed independently, but it is not large enough on its own to justify abandoning a market.

All three products sell at one price and they do not cost one price to make. Our only held quotes have the kids blend costing about two dollars less than the adult blend, and we have no quote at all for the Summit Stack, which carries the most expensive actives in the range. Flat pricing across three different cost bases means one of these products is quietly our best and one is quietly our worst, and right now we do not know which is which.

## Why the mix decides whether the run pays for itself

This is the finding I would act on first, because it costs nothing.

Treat the launch as a treasury question rather than a margin one. Against the last stated commitment of roughly one hundred and six thousand dollars, a US bundle keeps about a hundred and seventy four dollars of cash after processing, so a little over six hundred bundles covers the entire run. A single pouch keeps about seventy four dollars, so it takes more than fourteen hundred of those to do the same job.

We have capped the founding cohort at eight hundred. That cap was set on supply grounds and it is the right number. But eight hundred bundles funds the launch outright and eight hundred singles does not come close, and the only difference is what the page puts in front of people first. It draws exactly the same eight hundred units of the adult blend either way, because the extra products in the bundle are the two we have plenty of.

That was the recommendation in the July sizing work and the arithmetic on the real prices makes it stronger, not weaker.

## What I do not know, and would want before this is treated as settled

Four figures turn all of this from a range into an answer. The real cost per pouch from whoever is actually manufacturing. The Ships A Lot rate card, which we have still never received despite the contract being executed on 10 July. A restated commitment figure for the UK route, because the hundred and six thousand is from 26 July and was built on the US manufacturing plan that has since been superseded. And the UK and US split of what has sold so far, which Felix's dashboard should give us today.

The working model is saved alongside this note with every input tagged as verified, quoted, estimated or unknown. Re-run it with the real numbers and it will answer the question properly.
