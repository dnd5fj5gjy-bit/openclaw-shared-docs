# Pre-order page: what has to be on it before you take a penny

**25 July 2026. Built because pre-orders may open next week and this is a day-one problem, not a later one.**

This is the practical version: the decision, the copy, and the two settings. Anything load-bearing should get a look from Ignition, but none of this is exotic and it is all standard practice.

---

## The one decision

**Charge on despatch, or charge now with a stated window?**

**Recommend: charge on despatch.** It removes almost the entire problem. You hold the order, you take the money when the pouches actually ship, and the rules about refunds and delay notices largely stop biting because you are not holding customer cash against undelivered goods.

The trade is real: you do not get the cash early, which for a bootstrapped launch is the whole appeal of a pre-order. If you need the cash to fund the run, charge now and accept the obligations below. That is a legitimate choice, made deliberately.

If you charge now, three things become mandatory rather than nice:

1. A clear expected despatch window stated **before** payment, not in a confirmation email afterwards.
2. A delay notice if you miss it, with a genuine option to cancel and be refunded.
3. Refunds processed promptly rather than converted to credit.

## Why it matters on both sides

**US.** The FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule requires a reasonable basis to expect shipment within the time you state, or within 30 days if you state nothing. Miss it and you owe the customer a delay notice and the option to cancel. "We stated 30 days and hoped" is exactly the position it exists to catch.

**UK.** Under the Consumer Contracts Regulations the default is delivery within 30 days unless the customer agrees otherwise, and distance selling carries a 14-day cancellation right. Agreeing otherwise is easy, but it has to be agreed up front and visibly, which is what the copy below does.

A Bear-fronted launch is not a good place to find out how these work. The downside is not the fine, it is the story.

---

## Copy for the page, above the buy button

> **This is a pre-order.** We are making the first run now. Your order is reserved and will ship by **[DATE]**. We will email you the moment it leaves the warehouse. If anything slips, we will tell you straight away and you can cancel for a full refund, no questions.

Pick a real date for `[DATE]` with buffer in it. Whatever you honestly expect, add two weeks. The cost of shipping early is a delighted customer; the cost of shipping late is a delay notice and a refund queue during your launch week.

## Copy for the confirmation email

> Thanks, your Modern Savage pre-order is reserved.
>
> **What happens next:** we are finishing the first production run. Your order ships by **[DATE]** and you will get a tracking email the moment it goes.
>
> **If it slips:** we will email you before that date with a new one, and you can cancel for a full refund with one reply.
>
> **Charged today / charged when it ships:** [pick one and say it plainly]

## The two settings

1. **Payment capture.** Authorise now, capture on despatch, if the backend supports it. Felix will know. If it does not, charge now and use the copy above.
2. **A cancel path that works.** One email address that a human reads, and a refund that goes back to the original card. Do not make someone chase a refund during your launch week and then post about it.

---

## Also worth deciding now

**Cap the pre-order.** You told Phoebe you cannot serve more than roughly 800 subscriptions a territory against an 8-week lead time. If the launch goes well you can sell past your own supply chain in a weekend, and then every one of those customers gets a delay notice instead of a product. A stated number of founding places turns that risk into a scarcity story, which is the better version of the same fact.

**Say when the second run lands.** If you sell out, the page should say when the next run ships rather than going dead. That is a free email capture and it keeps the momentum you paid marketing for.
