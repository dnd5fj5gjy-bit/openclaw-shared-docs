## 1. The scope is inverted

Felix asked Raemy on 2 Aug for a read of "the privacy policy, terms and conditions, refund policy,
and shipping info". Raemy forwarded it to Paul on 4 Aug as "our policy note for our retail page on
Stripe... nothing too detailed". So Paul is reviewing four policy pages.

For a supplement brand the regulatory exposure is almost never in the policy pages. It is in what you
say about the product. Right now `/ingredients` says:

- collagen is "for skin, hair, nails, joints and gut lining"
- LactoSpore is "a hardy live **probiotic**"
- inulin is "a **prebiotic** fibre. Food for the good bacteria already in your gut"
- bovine heart is "naturally **rich in** CoQ10, which the heart and muscles run on"
- beef liver is "the single most nutrient-dense food there is"
- marine calcium is "**absorbed better** than the rock-mined kind"

In Great Britain those are health and nutrition claims under retained Regulation 1924/2006, where only
claims on the GB register may be made. "Probiotic" is itself long treated as an unauthorised health
claim. There is no authorised health claim for collagen. "Rich in" is a defined nutrition claim with a
numeric threshold, and CoQ10 has no nutrient reference value, so it cannot meet one.

In the US the same sentences are structure/function claims, which carry a disclaimer requirement under
21 CFR 101.93. **I searched every page I pulled from modernsavage.co tonight. The FDA disclaimer
appears zero times, on any page.**

Getting `/ingredients` and the three product pages added to Paul's scope costs one sentence on the
call. It is the highest-value sentence available.

## 2. Three facts Paul cannot see, and needs

The terms and the shipping page both tell customers we are "aiming to deliver around October 2026",
and the shipping page states "UK orders ship from our UK warehouse". Both read as settled fact. As of
tonight:

- **No UK manufacturer is contracted.** Calita is a call on Thursday morning, and its appeal is a MOQ
  of 500 pouches against Parkacre's 10,000 per SKU. Aviform is stopped waiting on the composition of
  the "21 Nutrient Multi Blend".
- **The UK warehouse cannot ship.** SCEND stated on 30 Jul it cannot ship a single UK order without a
  UK VAT number, and that number does not exist. Their contract is not signed - Jack Crumpton was
  still asking for company details on 3 Aug.
- **UK money has been taken since 1 Aug.** On the 2 Aug count, 49% of orders were UK: roughly $2,214
  of the $4,518 taken by then, plus about half of every day since.

The FTC rule Paul will reach for requires a reasonable basis for the stated shipping date *at the
moment the order is taken*. The US side probably has one: $80k paid against a $160k, 7,500-pouch run.
The UK side is the one to put in front of him.

This is the most useful thing Jesse can say on the call, and it is the thing most likely to go unsaid
because it is uncomfortable. A document review cannot find it. Only we can hand it over.

## 3. Four concrete gaps in the live pages

**a) No UK GDPR Article 27 representative.** The terms and the privacy policy both name the seller and
controller as Modern Savage Inc., incorporated in Delaware. No UK or EU establishment is named
anywhere. We sell to UK consumers, in sterling, with a UK delivery option. That is the exact trigger
for Article 27: designate a representative in the UK, in writing, and publish their details in the
privacy notice under Article 13. The policy names none. Same point for the EU if EU shipping opens.

**b) The privacy policy stands behind a contract that does not exist.** It names ShipHero as the
fulfilment platform and states that every named processor "is bound by a data processing agreement
with appropriate safeguards". SCEND is the UK 3PL, is not named, and has no signed contract, so no DPA
can exist. That is a statement made to consumers that we cannot currently support. Cheap fix: name the
UK provider and sign the DPA alongside the contract.

**c) Subscriptions cannot be cancelled online before the second delivery.** The terms, the returns
page and the shipping page all say cancellation from the account is available "any time after your
second delivery", and that cancelling earlier requires emailing us. California's Automatic Renewal Law
requires an online cancellation route where the signup was online. Ask Paul directly whether this
passes in California and the other ARL states. It is a small build change if it does not.

**d) Trademarked ingredients are named publicly.** Aquamin-F, Fibriss and LactoSpore all appear on
`/ingredients` with their marks. Ingredient licensing agreements have been an open item since July.
Ask whether we hold licences to use those marks in marketing.

## 4. What to push back on

Governing law is currently England and Wales, with an express carve-out preserving mandatory consumer
protections for US, EU and other buyers. For a business where about half the customers are UK, that is
the right shape.

A US firm's default is to flip to US state law with binding arbitration and a class action waiver. Do
not accept a wholesale flip. Against UK consumers a foreign governing law cannot strip mandatory UK
protections, and an arbitration clause of that kind is very likely unfair under section 62 of the
Consumer Rights Act 2015. So the flip buys nothing on the UK half of the customer base and costs a
document that currently reads well.

The ask is: keep England and Wales, harden the US side inside it.

## 5. What to actually say

Five things, in this order.

1. "Before we start on the policies, two of our pages worry me more than these four do. Can you take
   `/ingredients` and the three product pages as well? I want to know which of our ingredient
   descriptions are structure/function claims, and whether we need the FDA disclaimer on the site as
   well as the label."

2. "You need a fact the documents do not show. The pages say we ship around October and that UK orders
   ship from a UK warehouse. Our UK manufacturer is not contracted yet and our UK warehouse cannot
   ship until we have a VAT number we do not have. We are taking UK money today. Tell me what the
   pre-order pages need to say for that to be safe, and tell me at what point it stops being safe."

3. "We are a Delaware company selling to UK consumers. Do we need a UK GDPR Article 27 representative,
   and does the privacy policy need to name them?"

4. "Our subscription can only be cancelled from the account after the second delivery. Before that the
   customer has to email us. Does that pass the California Automatic Renewal Law and the other state
   auto-renewal rules?"

5. "Governing law is England and Wales because about half our customers are in the UK. I want to keep
   that. Please harden the US position inside it rather than flipping it."

And one to close on: **"What is the fastest thing on this list, and what would you fix before we take
another order?"** Paul offered the call specifically to get input before undertaking the work. That
question is what turns a scoping call into a work order.
