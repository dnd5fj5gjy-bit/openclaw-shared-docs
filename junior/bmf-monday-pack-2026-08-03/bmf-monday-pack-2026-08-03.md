## The thing that must not be missed

On 27 July, the same day you spoke to Farren about building a BMF hub, Christina Weguelin at Battersea Power Station Development Company wrote to say they want possession of the units next to the gym at Grosvenor Arch for a heritage use. The licence only allows them to recover part of it by ending all of it, so they would serve formal notice on the mutual one-month break, and then, in her words, look to grant a new licence for the gym area only.

You replied the same evening that this was contrary to your motivation because we are trying to expand, not shrink. She chased on 28 July, and again on 30 July with a line worth reading twice: they have begun work on the adjacent unit and are still keen to discuss.

Three things about that. The break is one month, which is not a lease and gives almost no protection. The replacement licence is described as something they would look to grant, which is an intention rather than a commitment, and their own footer says nothing binds them until a written agreement is executed. And they are building next door already, which means the placemaking decision has been taken and the only open question is what we get out of it.

The exposure is straightforward: the hub you described to Farren, and the gym you recruited Alastair for in April, could be served with thirty days' notice while the replacement is still a conversation. That does not make it a crisis. BPSDC clearly want the gym to stay, they have offered to come back with options on where your wider proposal could work across the estate, and a landlord who is mid-build next door has every reason to keep a Bear Grylls name at the entrance to their site. It is leverage, not a threat. But leverage decays, and it is strongest before the notice is served rather than after.

So the ask is small and it is this week: get the new licence agreed in principle before anything formal lands, push for longer than one month's notice on the replacement, and put the expansion request and the heritage request on the same table so they are traded against each other rather than settled separately. Christina gave you her mobile twice.

## Does the GoRuck story stand up

Yes, with one caveat worth holding.

GoRuck was founded in 2008 in Jacksonville Beach, Florida by Jason McCarthy, a former Green Beret, exactly as Farren described. Public reporting puts gross revenue at forty six million dollars for 2023, so his fifty million was fair. They have run over ten thousand of their own Challenge events since 2010, which means the events capability is real and not a marketing line.

The caveat: one third party estimate of their direct web sales puts 2025 materially below 2024. That is a single channel and an outside estimate rather than a filing, so I would not lean on it. But it is enough that I would not assume the UK budget is unlimited. Ask what is actually committed rather than what is possible.

The part that matters more is what is missing. Their published events calendar is entirely American. For a company whose whole brand is built on events, arriving in Britain with no British events operation is a real gap, and it is precisely the gap BMF fills. We hold relationships across roughly a hundred and forty parks, most of them dormant but not lost, two live sites in Hyde Park and Battersea, an email list built over twenty five years, and a name that a British audience already associates with military fitness. They have gear, money and a format. We have permission, place and audience. That is a genuine trade and we should stop thinking of it as them doing us a favour with free rucksacks.

## The trap in the structure

Farren is going to be GoRuck's UK Director. He is also the person we would be handing the BMF brand to. Both of those can be true and it can still work, but only if it is priced honestly, because the default outcome is that BMF becomes GoRuck's UK marketing channel and gets paid in backpacks.

Kit at zero cost is a real saving and it is worth having. It is not payment for brand access, distribution and event permissions. If we build a Military Games with them, the thing that has value in five years is the event itself, not the sponsorship of it. HYROX is the proof: it started with a few dozen competitors and is reported to be heading for around one and a half million participants across a hundred and twenty one events in thirty four countries this year, at revenues in the hundreds of millions. Farren worked with them early and used them as his own example, so he understands exactly what he is describing.

That means three things go in writing before anything is announced. BMF owns the event brand and its intellectual property, whatever we call it. BMF owns the entrant data and the email list that comes out of it, not the sponsor. And GoRuck's involvement is a sponsorship with a term, a fee or a revenue share, and a category exclusivity that we grant deliberately rather than give away. Kit in kind sits on top of that, not instead of it.

None of this needs to be adversarial. Say it as a compliment: this is worth doing properly because it could be big.

## Three people, two different businesses

Jay is settled. You closed it on 31 July and Josh agreed. It does not need re-opening on the call and I would not spend a minute of the thirty on it.

What is genuinely open is that you have two live candidates pointing at two different companies. Farren is mass participation, rucking, events, a national audience and a commercial sponsor. Alastair Nicolas is the faith-forward community gym at Battersea, your father's introduction, and he told you on 22 July that he is a hundred percent in and wants to remodel the movement to be Christ-centred. Both are good. They are not the same business, and the same physical unit cannot be the flagship for both without one of them feeling like a tenant.

The clean answer is that they are not competing for the same job. Alastair runs the room, which is community, membership, weekly rhythm, the thing that has to be true every Tuesday. Farren runs the reach, which is events, audience and the national return. If you can hold both under one brand with a clear line between the hub and the tour, you have a much better business than either alone. If you cannot, choose the one your father will get behind, because his motivation is the reason this brand is still worth anything.

Worth saying plainly: you have not been back to Alastair since 22 July either. He is waiting on an invitation to the gym.

## What I would close in the thirty minutes

Say to Josh, roughly in this order:

"Battersea first. There is a one-month break on the licence and they have started building next door. I want us to agree the replacement licence before any notice gets served, and I want longer notice on the new one."

"Second, Farren. I want to move on him. He is the best candidate we have seen, he brings GoRuck with him, and the risk is that we end up as GoRuck's UK marketing arm paid in rucksacks. So if we build the Military Games, BMF owns the event and the entrant data, and GoRuck are a sponsor on a term with a number attached."

"Third, what have we actually got. I need the email list size, the park permissions that are still live, and what the Hyde Park franchise agreement lets us do. I cannot plan an event without knowing how many people we can reach for free."

"Fourth, separately from Farren, my dad introduced Alastair for the Battersea room and he is keen. I want to run the hub and the events as two jobs, not one."

Then agree who writes to Farren and by when, because he has been waiting a week.

## What I could not verify

The size of the BMF email list, which is the single number that decides whether a relaunch event can be filled for nothing. What the Hyde Park franchise agreement permits. Whether the GoRuck UK entity exists yet or whether Farren's directorship is still verbal. And GoRuck's committed UK budget, which is the difference between a sponsor and an enthusiast.

One filing detail, since it will come up if structure does: Companies House shows Be Military Fit Holdings Ltd was renamed BGV Holdings Ltd on 2 July 2026, company number 12859246, still active, accounts filed to 30 September 2025. Worth knowing which entity holds the brand before anyone discusses giving someone a share of it.
