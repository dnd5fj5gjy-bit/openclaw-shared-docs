# BGEP: does a shop sell ten a day?

**10 August 2026. Junior.** The deck asks for GBP 50k to answer this question in the field.
Most of it can be answered from the desk for nothing, and the answer changes an email we sent
on Friday. Model: `demand_model.py`, re-runnable. Sources: `RESEARCH-NOTES.md`.

---

## The answer in three lines

**The outlet plan is conservative. The rate-of-sale assumption is doing all the work, and it is
roughly four times too high.** 60,000 outlets is 0.5% of India's 13m kirana, which is easy.
Ten sachets a day as the *average* across all 60,000, every month, is not.

**Pricing is the good news and it is better than we thought.** Rs 10 for 12g is genuine white
space. Nobody credible is at the Rs 10 price point.

**One thing to change this week:** the RFQs to Halewood and Hindustan Foods ask for a single
price at 18m sachets a month. That is the wrong question in two ways.

---

## What the plan gets right

**The Rs 10 price point is empty, and we can own it.**

| | Pack | MRP | Rs per gram |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electral (No.1, FDC) | 21.8 g | Rs 23.05 | 1.06 |
| Electral unit dose | 4.4 g | Rs 4.65 | 1.06 |
| Cipla Prolyte | 21 g | Rs 22.26 | 1.06 |
| **BGEP planned** | **12 g** | **Rs 10** | **0.83** |

The category prices at Rs 1.06 a gram, everywhere, without exception. At 12g for Rs 10 we are
**22% cheaper per gram than every incumbent** and we hit an impulse price point their pack
sizes cannot reach. Jesse's Rs 10 instinct and the 12g fill weight are both right.

**Correction to our own file.** Our 9 Aug note said "Prolyte is Rs 10 for 21g" and the 12g
recommendation was argued off it. Prolyte's MRP is **Rs 22.26** (verified at PharmEasy, 10 Aug);
Rs 7.50-10 is what the *trade* pays, not the shelf price. The recommendation survives, the
reasoning behind it did not. Memory corrected.

**60,000 outlets is not the stretch.** It is 0.5% of the kirana universe, and 90% of Indian
FMCG still moves through that channel.

---

## What the plan gets wrong

### 1. Ten a day is roughly 4x too high as an average

Three tests. Two fail.

**Test A, against the brands that already won.** Year one at the plan rate is Rs 143 cr of
revenue. Electral, India's No.1 ORS brand with 50%+ share, selling since the 1970s, is Rs 562 cr.
**The plan reaches a quarter of Electral in twelve months**, from no distribution, no trade
relationships and no factory. Nothing in Indian FMCG has done that.

**Test B, against the category.** At the plan rate we take Rs 216 cr of retail value out of a
category worth **Rs 800-1,000 cr in total**. That is 22-27% share in year one, which would make
BGEP the No.2 ORS brand in India before its first anniversary. Even against the wider
ORS-plus-glucose-powder pool of ~Rs 3,100 cr, which is the fairer denominator for a "want"
product, it is 7% in year one.

**Test C, against the shopkeeper, which the plan passes.** Ten a day is Rs 3,000 a month of
retail value, 1.4-1.8% of a kirana's Rs 20-25 lakh annual turnover. High for one SKU, but not
impossible. **A good outlet in Delhi in May can absolutely sell ten a day.** The plan's problem
is not physics at one shop. It is calling that the average of 60,000 shops for twelve months.

| Rate of sale | Sachets/mo | Tonnes/mo | Contribution/yr | % of Electral |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plan, 10/day | 18.0m | 216 | GBP 8.3m | 25% |
| Strong, 5/day | 9.0m | 108 | GBP 4.2m | 13% |
| **Base, 2.5/day** | **4.5m** | **54** | **GBP 2.1m** | **6%** |
| Slow, 1/day | 1.8m | 22 | GBP 0.8m | 3% |

**Base is still a good business.** GBP 2.1m of annual contribution off a 12-month-old brand is
a real outcome. It is just a quarter of what the deck currently promises, and it implies a
completely different factory.

### 2. The run rate is flat and this category is not

Glucon-D, the closest channel analogue, reports pronounced seasonality with a Q2/Q3 peak. We are
selling heat relief in a country that had 554 heatwave days in 2024. A flat 18m a month is not
how this sells. On the Base case a normal summer curve gives **108 tonnes in peak month against
22 in trough** - a 5x swing. Peak is what a contract manufacturer reserves a line for, and
nobody has asked either of them for a peak number.

### 3. At 8%, no distributor can live on us

The distributor earns **Rs 0.58 a sachet**. Running a van, a driver, a godown and two or three
salesmen costs Rs 1.5-2 lakh a month before he earns anything. At the Base rate, a distributor
with 1,000 outlets grosses **Rs 43,000 a month** from BGEP. He cannot do it.

This is not fatal, but it kills a premise. It means we are one line in an existing distributor's
book, not a dedicated distributor we control end to end. **That is the assumption the scan app
was built on.** Holding my no on the app for the pilot, now for a structural reason rather than
a cost one.

## What I have not verified

- The Rs 562 cr Electral figure is a brand turnover number from FDC's reporting via broker
  research. Whether it is company net sales or secondary sales value moves the unit count by
  about 1.5x. It does not change any conclusion above, all of which hold on either reading.
- Rate of sale benchmarks per outlet are derived from category revenue, not observed. No
  published per-outlet throughput exists for this category. That is precisely why the pilot
  needs to measure it.
- The 2x summer index is a normal FMCG curve applied by me, not Glucon-D's actual published
  index, which they do not disclose.
