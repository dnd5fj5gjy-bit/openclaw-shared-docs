## Why the list is frozen

Marketing email to individuals is governed by regulation 22 of PECR. The default is consent. The only
practical way round it is the soft opt-in in regulation 22(3), and its first condition is the one that
bites here: the details must have been obtained **by the sender** in the course of a sale, or
negotiations for a sale, to that recipient.

The sender has to be the same legal person that collected the data. It is not.

Companies House, checked this morning: the company that traded BMF through the period this list was
built is **BE MILITARY FIT LIMITED, number 11228310**, incorporated 27 February 2018 under the name
**BG MILITARY FITNESS LIMITED** and renamed in January 2019. It was **dissolved on 22 February 2022** —
which lines up exactly with Mushtaq's note that the data stops in 2021.

Two more companies of the same name have been incorporated and dissolved since: 13922252 (Feb 2022 to
Dec 2023) and 15367124 (Dec 2023 to May 2025). Each was incorporated within days of the previous one
dying. I could not find a live Be Military Fit company on the register at all, and that is a question
for the room rather than a conclusion from me: **which entity is the relaunch actually going to trade
through, and does it lawfully hold this data?** HPO Technologies Limited, where Mushtaq sits, was
incorporated in September 2020 and is a different legal person again.

So whichever entity sends the relaunch email, it did not collect these addresses. Soft opt-in is not
available to it. That leaves consent, and there is no consent record.

## And you cannot email them to ask

This is the part people get caught by, so it is worth being blunt about it.

In March 2017 the ICO fined **Flybe £70,000** and **Honda £13,000** for exactly this move. Flybe mailed
three million people with "Are your details correct?", asking them to update their marketing
preferences. Honda mailed 289,790 people with "Would you like to hear from Honda?", covering the part
of its database where it held no opt-in or opt-out record because of a system flaw.

Honda's case is ours almost precisely: a legacy database of unknown consent status, and a
well-intentioned email asking people to confirm. The ICO's finding was that **the email asking for
consent is itself marketing**, and so it needs the consent it is asking for. Honda argued it was being
responsible. It was fined anyway.

A "we're relaunching BMF, are you still interested?" send to 42,000 people is that email.

## What the list is still worth

Quite a lot, just not as a broadcast channel.

It is a market map. Forty-two thousand records tell you which parks filled, which cities had density,
what the seasonality looked like and where the franchises actually worked. That is analysis on data
you already hold, for internal purposes, and it is the single best input into deciding where Farren's
pop-up tour should go. Use it to choose the cities.

It also matters for what it says about the brand's reach at its peak, which is a number worth having
in front of any partner or sponsor.

What it cannot be is the free launch channel, and Farren is currently building his phase one on the
assumption that it is. He needs telling before he plans around it, not after.
