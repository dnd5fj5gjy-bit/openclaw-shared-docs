# Ted's Health — weight management
## Ad copy, six concepts, ready to load

**11 August 2026.** Written against the rules that actually get these ads rejected.

---

### The two rules that shaped every line below

**1. No medicine, named or implied.** UK law, covered in the setup document. Nothing here says or
hints at a drug. Everything sells the programme and the clinic.

**2. No personal attributes.** This is the rule most weight loss ads die on and almost nobody
accounts for. Meta does not allow an ad to assert or imply that it knows something personal about the
viewer, and health is explicitly covered. So **"Struggling with your weight?" is a rejection**, because
it asserts a fact about the person reading it.

The fix is to move the sentence off the reader and onto someone else. First person, third person, or
the clinic's own voice:

| Rejected | Works |
|---|---|
| "Struggling to lose weight?" | "Most people we see have lost the same stone four times." |
| "You've tried everything." | "Our patients have usually tried everything." |
| "Is your BMI over 30?" | "Our programme starts with an eligibility check." |
| "Tired of failing diets?" | "Diets fail most people. That is a design problem, not a you problem." |

Every line below already follows this. **If you rewrite anything, keep the sentence off the reader.**

### Making 30 out of these six

Do not write thirty new arguments. Take these six and vary **one element at a time**, so the test
stays readable:

- **The opening line only.** It is what 80% of people read. Five openers per concept is five ads.
- **Length.** A three-line version and a one-line version of the same concept behave very differently.
- **Format.** The same words as static, as a talking-head video, as text-on-screen.
- **Voice.** Clinic voice versus a clinician speaking to camera. If Dr Andrews will appear on camera,
  do that. A named, real doctor is the single strongest asset you have in this category and no
  competitor can copy it.

Keep the concept name as the first token of the ad name, so the report tells you which *argument* won.

### Before any of it goes live

- Read every line against the drug-name list. Thirty ads is thirty chances.
- Read every line against the personal-attributes rule. The sentence must never be about the reader.
- No claim about how much or how fast. No numbers, no timelines, no "up to".
- No before-and-after imagery, whatever Meta's current position on it is.
- Landing page held to the same two rules as the ad.
