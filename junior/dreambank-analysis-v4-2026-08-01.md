# Dream Bank - Analysis

**1 August 2026.** Based on the financial model sent by Jack Whettingsteel, 31 July.

---

## The verdict

Dream Bank is an early-stage idea being presented as a fundable plan. The idea has real merit and the
distribution behind it is genuinely unusual. But they do not control any of the levers that decide whether
it makes money, their customer acquisition forecast only works if Bear and Simon promote it for free, and
the round is being marketed before the things their own model flags as serious have been resolved.

It could become big. On the facts as presented it is not yet investable.

---

## The plan depends on Bear marketing it for nothing

This is the largest assumption in the document and it is never stated as one.

The marketing budget is negligible. Set it against the number of accounts the plan promises to open and
the implied cost of winning a customer is a tiny fraction of what banks actually pay, by a factor closer
to fifty than to two. No bank acquires customers at that price. Nobody does.

What closes that gap is Bear and Simon posting about it. That is the customer acquisition strategy. It
does not appear anywhere as a line, a cost or a commitment, but it is what the forecast requires in order
to hold together.

Three things follow, and all of them are problems.

**It is unpriced.** There is no charge anywhere in the cost base for the single input the plan most
depends on. The promotion is assumed to arrive free and forever.

**It is unsecured.** Nobody has committed to it. There is no obligation on anyone to post, no term, no
minimum, no agreed campaign. If enthusiasm cools in eighteen months, the plan does not lose a marketing
channel, it loses its entire acquisition engine, and there is nothing to fall back on.

**It is unhedged.** There is no budget to buy growth if the posts underdeliver. The plan has no second
plan.

The conversion leap is also bigger than they are treating it as. Simon's community is entrepreneurs, so it
maps fairly directly onto business current accounts. Bear's audience is broad adventure and inspiration,
which is a much longer distance to travel to opening a business bank account. Assuming a large slice of an
existing following converts into bank customers in the launch month is the boldest thing in the model, and
it is presented as a given rather than a risk.

Underneath that, the growth curve itself is a spike in month one followed by a perfectly straight line for
a year. Straight lines are what a spreadsheet contains when nobody has built a funnel. There is no
conversion rate, no channel mix and no acquisition cost behind them.

---

## They are renting a bank, not building one

ClearBank holds the licence, the accounts and the money. Dream Bank owns the app and the brand. That is a
legitimate way to start and it avoids years of regulatory work, but it makes ClearBank a landlord rather
than a supplier. ClearBank takes a cut of the interest before Dream Bank sees any of it, then invoices
separately for the platform, for every account, for every payment and for every identity check. Across the
first three years, more than half the interest those customer deposits generate ends up with ClearBank
rather than Dream Bank. In the first year, ClearBank's payment charges alone exceed the entire interest
income. The model concedes this in a footnote and moves on.

The pricing behind all of it expired in March. Every cost in the plan comes from an agreement that is no
longer live, and Dream Bank has no leverage to renegotiate, because the implementation fee is already paid
and the commitment letter is already signed.

So the two things that decide whether this business is worth anything, the Bank of England base rate and
ClearBank's price list, both sit outside management's control.

---

## The design contradiction

Dream Bank earns money when balances sit still. It pays ClearBank when accounts get used.

The largest single cost in the plan is per-payment fees, and those scale with activity. So the economics
quietly prefer a dormant account holding a large balance over a busy business account doing what a business
account is for. The better this works as a working bank for entrepreneurs, the worse it performs
financially. That is not a number to be tuned, it is a contradiction between the product they are selling
and the way they get paid, and nothing in the plan addresses it.

---

## The things they should have fixed before asking

**The round is short of its own requirement.** Their model calculates what they need and it is more than
they are raising, flagged inside the sheet as a high risk against themselves. A second raise is already
scheduled, on their timing.

**A cash obligation is missing entirely.** ClearBank requires a minimum balance under an agreement signed
in May. No figure for it appears anywhere in the model.

**A third of the profit comes from products that do not exist.** Ten of them, insurance through lending to
accounting, none built, no partners signed.

**There is no company.** Companies House shows one entity, Simon's advertising agency, wholly controlled by
him. There is no Dream Bank, no DreamFund, no Dream Media. There is nothing to buy a share of.

**And there is no price.** No valuation, no percentage, no mention of equity anywhere in the model. An
amount has been asked for without saying what it is worth or what it buys.

---

## What is genuinely good

The distribution is real, and it is the hardest thing in the world to manufacture. Most fintechs die
because nobody hears about them, and that is the one problem this team does not have. The difficulty is
that they have built a plan which depends on it entirely, for free, without securing it.

The model itself is honest. It labels its own guesses, raises the hardest questions against itself, and
openly documents a serious error an earlier version made and corrected. Almost every criticism above is
already flagged somewhere inside their own spreadsheet, which says something good about the people doing
the work.

Deposit businesses also compound. If this reaches real scale the economics improve sharply, because the
supplier costs tier down while the interest keeps climbing. The version of this that works, works well.

---

## What would have to be true

Four things, all at once. Deposits reach several hundred million. Interest rates stay roughly where they
are for years, when the Bank has just split six to three and is leaning toward cuts. ClearBank re-prices
without taking more, having every reason to take more. And Bear and Simon keep promoting it for nothing,
for years, converting at a rate no bank has achieved by paying.

Each is individually plausible. All four together, over four years, is a demanding ask, and the plan is
priced only for the version where they all hold.

---

## Where this leaves it

The gap here is not honesty, it is stage. This is a warm, credible team with an attractive mission and
genuine reach, holding a plan that has not finished being built. No entity, no valuation, no current
supplier pricing, an underfunded round, and an acquisition forecast that rests on free celebrity promotion
nobody has committed to.

Those are all fixable, and none of them requires anything but time and work. They are being asked to be
overlooked rather than fixed, and that is the part worth being clear-eyed about.
