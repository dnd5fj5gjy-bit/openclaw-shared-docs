# Modern Savage — Business Plan v3

### 31 July 2026 · Prepared by Junior for Jesse Grylls

*Supersedes v2.2 (14 July). One thing has changed since that version and it changes the shape of the whole plan: we are no longer trying to have physical product in customers' hands for 12 September. We are pre-selling a capped founding run, announcing it from the Health Optimisation Summit stage, and shipping in October. Everything below follows from that.*

---

## 1. The plan in five lines

1. Modern Savage is the family's 100% owned primal-nutrition brand. Three organ-based SKUs, sold direct on subscription at modernsavage.co. Formulas locked, samples approved, US production paid for and running.
2. The 12 September launch with product in hand is gone. It was lost to one unsigned spec sheet and an unresponsive manufacturer, not to anything about the product.
3. So we sell the founding run before it exists: **800 founding places, $125 bundle, roughly $100,000 raised, shipping October.** The pre-order very nearly funds its own launch.
4. Bear on the Health Optimisation Summit stage on 12 September is the irreplaceable asset, and it is protected. "Run one sold out, run two opens tonight" is a stronger thing to say from that stage than "please visit our website."
5. The UK is a second manufacturing base, not a shipment. It is real but it is behind the US, and it has one unresolved product question (Summit is not sellable in Great Britain as formulated).

## 3. What went wrong, stated plainly

The launch date was never set by the powder. It was set by the pouch artwork, and the pouch artwork has been blocked since 10 July by an unsigned spec sheet at ACF.

The chain from print-ready artwork to sellable stock runs 46 days at its fast end: prepress and print, delivery to Miami, fill and pack, freight, receiving. Hold that chain still and slide only the artwork date, and every working day of delay comes straight off the launch.

The gate was 24 July. It passed with no artwork, no signed spec sheets, and no written reply from ACF since the 22 July call. Two separate requests from Belmark for something as small as a shipping address also went unanswered. That is not a supplier deliberating, that is a supplier not reading the thread.

**The lesson worth keeping:** three suppliers this month cost us time by not answering, and in each case we chased through the channel that had already failed. The rule now is that when a supplier misses two written chases, the third contact is a phone call.

**One number underneath all of this is still unconfirmed.** The 15-working-day print lead time at Belmark has never been put in writing. If the true figure is 20, the September date was already gone before any of the chasing started. Confirming it is a single line to a supplier who answers everything, and it remains the highest-value question on the board.

## 4. The plan that replaces it: the Founding 800

**Cap: 800 founding places.** This is a supply number, not a marketing device. A founding place is a subscription, so it draws a unit every month, not once. Run one is 2,500 adult units, the binding SKU. Against a reorder cycle of eight to twelve weeks, cover has to outlast the cycle, and every route to that number lands between 830 and 1,250. The conservative end is 800.

**Offer: a bundle, not a bag.** The scarcity is in the adult blend only. Mini and Summit have 2,500 units each and no demand engine pointed at them. So the founding offer is a two-product bundle at $125 (adult plus Summit as the default, adult plus Mini as the family option), a full set at $180, and the single adult bag at $79.99 available but deliberately not the hero.

| Founding offer | Price | Cash at 800 places | Adult units drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adult only, subscription | $59.99 | $47,992 | 800 |
| **Two-product bundle** | **$125** | **$100,000** | **800** |
| Three-product bundle | $180 | $144,000 | 800 |

Every row consumes the same 800 units of the scarce SKU. The bundle raises twice the cash for it. These are already our published prices, so this is not a new pricing decision.

**No discount.** The founding benefits cost us nothing: a twelve-month price lock, a hand-numbered insert card, and community access from the moment of order rather than from delivery. That last one matters, it is the only thing a customer receives immediately, and it turns an eight-week wait into a membership.

**It will sell.** The waiting list alone at 15-25% conversion delivers 270-450 orders. The full list at 1% delivers 576. Treat 800 as an operational limit that binds within days, not a scarcity mechanic that may or may not trigger. Which means the sold-out page and the run-two waitlist have to be built before the page opens, not on the morning it sells out. Both are built and previewable now.

**The counter counts real orders.** No inflated numbers, no fake timers. This audience trusts Bear specifically, and a fake scarcity mechanic is the most recognisable thing in direct-to-consumer. It reads as someone else's brand.

**The stated ship date is 12 October, and we ship in September.** Whatever we honestly expect, plus two weeks. Shipping early costs nothing. Shipping late costs a delay notice, a refund queue and a story, in launch week, on a Bear-fronted brand.

**Compliance is on the page, above the buy button.** The US mail-order rule and the UK 30-day default mean we either charge only on despatch or state a clear shipping window before payment. Both versions of the copy are written. Which one goes live depends on one treasury question in section 6.

## 7. The UK

The UK is a second manufacturing base, not a container from Miami. That removes freight, duty, VAT drag and nineteen days on the water, gives UK reorders in weeks rather than months, produces natively UK-compliant product, and ends single-vendor dependence on ACF.

**Manufacturers.** The Protein Partners in Cheshire are the front-runner: they have confirmed they can source every organ including grass-fed bison liver, they hold three in-house regulatory specialists, they sample within a week of quote, and they can produce in up to eight weeks. Parkacre in Lincoln is the live second quote and therefore the leverage. Both are waiting on the same thing the US track is waiting on: our formula specifications.

**One unresolved product question.** The Summit Stack is not sellable in Great Britain as formulated, because of NMN's novel-food status. The decision is to either produce a UK Summit without it or hold Summit back from the UK launch. That decision has to be made at quote stage, not after.

**Regulatory path.** Food business operator registration (free, immediate, do it as soon as UK stock has an address), a manufacturer-led safety plan, UK-format labels rather than US ones, product liability insurance extended to UK-made stock, and no medicinal claims anywhere.

**Structure.** UK manufacturing plus UK stock plus UK customers likely creates a UK taxable presence. Either Modern Savage Inc registers for UK VAT and trades directly, or a UK subsidiary sits under it. That is a question for Raemy and it must be answered before the first UK sale, not after.

**Fulfilment.** Scend, the UK partner of our US fulfilment provider, agreed terms on 23 July. Several service levels remain undefined and need closing before stock arrives.

**Recommended first UK run:** the adult SKU at 2,500 units plus a smaller Summit run if minimums allow, roughly £70,000-95,000 upfront with about half of that as the near-term deposit. UK demand at £65 is unproven at volume, and the eight-week reorder makes a fast follow cheap. Prove it, then mirror it.

## 10. The honest risk list

- **ACF silence continues.** The pre-order plan already absorbs it, which is the point of the pivot, but the mid-August artwork date is real and slipping past it moves the ship date into November.
- **The unconfirmed print lead time.** One line to a responsive supplier fixes it. Until then every date in this document inherits an assumption.
- **Overselling our own supply chain.** The 800 cap exists for exactly this reason and there will be pressure to raise it the moment the page sells out. Raising it is a capital decision about triggering run two early, not a marketing decision.
- **Pre-order compliance.** Getting the charge mechanic or the ship window wrong on a Bear-fronted launch is a consumer-protection story, not a paperwork problem. Both versions are drafted; one setting has to be chosen deliberately.
- **Two manufacturers, two versions of the truth.** One master specification document, version-controlled, owned by us, and both manufacturers work from it.
- **Label claims against formula amounts.** A review of the live site against the manufacturer's panel found several mismatches between stated and actual amounts, and US-format daily values that are not UK-safe. These need closing with the manufacturer before any label goes to print.
