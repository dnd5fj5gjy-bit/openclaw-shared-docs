# 2. THE ONE THING THAT EXPLAINS EVERYTHING ELSE

Almost every strange decision in this business traces back to a single regulatory fact, so it is worth
understanding properly before you touch anything.

**US-made organ powder cannot lawfully be imported into the UK.**

Not "is difficult to". Cannot, without a process that takes months.

The mechanism, verified at source and re-verified on 2 August:

- The UK treats our blend as a **composite product** — it contains both processed animal product and
  plant material.
- A composite escapes veterinary checks only if it contains **no** processed meat product. Non-meat
  animal products get a 50% threshold. **Meat gets no threshold at all.** Any amount, explicitly
  including meat powders and extracts.
- Our adult blend carries 2,500mg of bovine organ per serving. That is a meat powder.
- So it requires an export health certificate, a border control post veterinary check, IPAFFS
  pre-notification, an approved country, and a **listed establishment**.

The listing requirement is the killer. It is the establishment producing the **meat ingredient** that
must be approved for export to Great Britain — not necessarily the plant that blends it. Listing runs
competent-authority to competent-authority with no published service standard. It takes months.

There is a second, separate problem. **US federal regulation 9 CFR 310.16(a) states that livestock lungs
shall not be saved for use as human food.** Our adult formula contains bovine lung. It sells legally in
the US because supplements sit under the FDA rather than the USDA, but a GB export health certificate is
signed through the meat system. Whether this blocks the certificate outright is **not settled** and I
have deliberately not asserted it. It needs Drew Kouvel at ACF and it needs APHA.

The inversion is worth knowing because it points at the answer: **Great Britain permits lung for human
consumption and the United States does not.** It is why haggis cannot be exported to America. The
ingredient most likely to block a certificate is simply fine if the product is made and sold in Britain.

### What follows from this

1. **UK manufacture is the answer, and it is the strategy we are already executing.** Make it in the UK
   and the entire import regime disappears for us — no border, no certificate, no listing, no APHA
   licence, and the lung problem resolves itself.
2. **Where a residual question sits, and whose it is.** "Made in the UK" removes our import exposure
   entirely. Some blends will still have to be bought from ACF because they cannot be sourced in the UK.
   **When our UK manufacturer buys raw materials from ACF, that importation and its compliance are
   theirs, not ours.** They are the buyer and the importer of record: the export health certificate, the
   IPAFFS pre-notification, the border checks and the establishment-listing question all sit on their
   side of the line. We are buying a finished, UK-made product from a UK company.

   That is the whole commercial point of manufacturing here, and it is why the answer to "how do we
   handle UK import compliance" is that we do not have to.

   **What remains ours is not paperwork, it is supply risk.** If a raw material turns out not to be
   importable, our manufacturer cannot make the product to the current spec, and that changes our
   formula or our timeline. So we need to know the answer. We do not need to own the process. See 3.1.
3. **BG Summit Stack is exempt from all of it.** No animal organs, so it is not meat, so it imports on
   ordinary food supplement rules. It is the only SKU that could be sold in the room at the 12 September
   Summit if the organ products end up US-only.

# 14. GLOSSARY

- **APHA** — Animal and Plant Health Agency. The UK authority on import authorisation for animal products.
- **BCP** — Border Control Post. Where veterinary checks physically happen.
- **COA** — Certificate of Analysis. Third-party lab verification of contents.
- **Composite product** — a food containing both processed animal product and plant material. Our blend.
- **EHC** — Export Health Certificate. Required for meat products entering GB.
- **EORI** — Economic Operators Registration and Identification number. Needed to move goods across the UK border.
- **FBO** — Food Business Operator. Must be registered and named on the pack with a UK address.
- **FHDDS** — Fulfilment House Due Diligence Scheme. Obliges a 3PL to check its clients' VAT status.
- **IPAFFS** — the UK system for pre-notifying imports of animal products.
- **MOQ** — Minimum Order Quantity.
- **NETP** — Non-Established Taxable Person. An overseas seller with UK stock. VAT threshold is zero.
- **NRV** — Nutrient Reference Value. Required on UK labels.
- **POAO** — Products of Animal Origin.
- **3PL** — Third-Party Logistics. SCEND in the UK, Ships A Lot in the US.
