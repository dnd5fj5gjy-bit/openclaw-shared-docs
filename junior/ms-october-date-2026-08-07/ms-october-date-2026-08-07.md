# Modern Savage: what actually decides the October date

**Built overnight, Friday 7 August 2026. Every fact below was read at source tonight, in Jesse's mailbox and on modernsavage.co, not from memory.**

---

## The verdict

October is not gated on the formula, the manufacturer, or the warehouse. It is gated on a printed pouch that nobody has started designing.

Belmark sent the blank dielines on **5 August, at 13:16 and 13:18** (two templates, projects #296995 and #296996). That is the day the artwork clock started. Sarah Salscheider said plainly in the same thread that **Belmark do no in-house graphic design**, so the artwork is ours to produce, and no designer is appointed to it. Until artwork is approved and a print order is placed, ACF has nothing to fill, Ships A Lot has nothing to receive, and not a single US order can dispatch.

The UK side had already gone. On Sam Platt's own numbers the earliest UK dispatch is 26 October at best, 9 November realistically, and that clock has not started either, because he is still owed the quotes he promised on 4 August and nobody has replied to him in three days.

Every product page on the site publishes "aiming to deliver around October 2026". **US October is still winnable. UK October is not.** Winning the US one comes down to approving the back-of-bag artwork inside the next week to ten days. That is a day of somebody's time, not a negotiation, and it is the highest-leverage thing on the whole launch right now.

## Why the bag is the binding constraint

The chain, with what is actually evidenced against each step:

- **5 August, 13:16.** Belmark send the two blank templates. Jesse had asked that morning at 03:23 for exact dimensions "so we can do the designing". Sarah, 09:05: "We unfortunately do not do any in-house design for graphics, but I can recommend some designers if you need one."
- **Artwork.** Not started. The back-of-bag and supplement facts panel have been outstanding since mid-July; Jesse chased Drew for it on 27 July ("Do you know when back of packaging design will be available?"). Drew sent something labelled "Summit Bag" on 5 August at 17:44 and rang about it, so Summit may be part-solved. The other two are not.
- **Prepress.** $150 per SKU, one-off, per Belmark's quote. Trivial money, but it sits after artwork, not before.
- **Print run.** 5,000 units per SKU, approved by Jesse on 16 July. Freight is FOB De Pere and excluded, so a truck to Florida sits on the end of it.
- **Fill.** ACF at Pompano Beach cannot fill until the pouches land, and they need the raw materials in the same window.
- **Receive.** Ships A Lot then have to book it into the WMS before anything ships to a customer.

Six steps, three of them not started, and the first one is the only one we control directly.

## The number that decides it, and we do not have it

**Belmark publish no lead time anywhere on their site.** I checked their flexible packaging and digitally-printed-pouch pages tonight; both talk about "rapid turnaround" and "speed to market" and neither commits to a figure. So the honest position is that we do not know how long the print run takes, and it is the number the entire October date hangs on.

If it is three weeks from art approval, and artwork is approved by 15 August, print lands around 5 September, Florida by roughly the 12th, filled and out to Ships A Lot late September, first US dispatch in the first half of October. That works, with no slack anywhere. Every week artwork slips pushes first dispatch by a week, and by the end of August October is gone in both markets.

One email to Sarah answers it. It should have gone on Tuesday.

## The UK side, unchanged since Tuesday

Nothing has moved since 5 August. Sam Platt gave us the first unqualified yes from a UK manufacturer on 4 August and has had no reply. He owes quotes; we owe him three questions, one of which is whether a segregated non-halal run is possible for our SKUs, because his halal certification requirement probably kills bison in the UK product rather than delaying it.

Separately, the site says "UK orders ship from our UK warehouse". SCEND put it in writing on 30 July that they cannot ship a single UK order without our VAT number. That has now sat at our end for eight days. It is a form, not a negotiation, and it is the cheapest gate on the board.

## The escape hatch is closed for two SKUs out of three

The obvious fix is to ship UK orders out of the US run. That works for exactly one product.

- **Modern Savage (adult)** is beef organ powder. Legally meat. The producing US establishment has to be listed for export to Great Britain, and no supplement blender is on that list.
- **Mini Savage** contains bovine colostrum. That is a dairy product of animal origin, and Defra's rules put it behind the same class of gate: an export health certificate for colostrum-based products, from an establishment approved to export to GB. Verified against gov.uk tonight. Mini is not the easy one; it is the same problem in a different certificate.
- **BG Summit Stack** is creatine, electrolytes, NMN, PQQ, trans-resveratrol, plant-derived D3 and K2 fermented from natto. **No animal material at all.** It imports into the UK as ordinary goods.

So there is a real, narrow option: Summit can serve UK customers in October out of the US run. The two organ SKUs cannot, on any route, before November. Worth knowing before we decide what the UK page is allowed to promise.

## What the book is actually worth

The site is honest about pre-orders, and the terms were tightened on 5 August. Three separate pages now say the same thing: your card is charged today, the date is a target not a guarantee, and **you can cancel for a full refund, no reason needed, at any point before dispatch.**

That is the right policy, and it is also the whole commercial point. **Until something ships, the pre-order book is a refundable deposit, not revenue.** Roughly half of it is UK, where nothing dispatches this year on current evidence. So the founding-run arithmetic needs correcting: the second $80,000 due to ACF on delivery cannot lean on the pre-order book, because half of that book is against a market that cannot be fulfilled and all of it is repayable on request.

And there is a timing point that runs the wrong way from instinct. The site commits us to emailing everyone with a revised date and offering a refund when it slips. **That email gets more expensive every day we delay it.** Sent against a small book it is a re-date. Sent in October against a large one it is a cancellation event.

We have already made this exact call once, correctly, in public. The shipping page tells Canadian customers: "we would rather open Canada once we are dispatching from stock than take your money months ahead of it." That reasoning now applies to UK buyers of the two organ SKUs.
