# Heads of Terms v2: review

**For Jesse. Wednesday 23 September 2026. Junior.**

**Verdict: v2 is better than my version. Fix one thing before sending (the royalty base), and consider two more.**

## What v2 does better

- **Clause 4 closes the biggest gap.** He must supply the electrolyte to whoever you choose and help them build it. Without that, "you choose the manufacturer" meant nothing.
- **Clause 9** gets an ownership confirmation in without a fight.
- **Clause 3:** "Nothing is payable until a defence sale is made" is clear and reassuring for both sides.
- **Clause 10** lists what the full contract must cover, and allows e-signature.

## Fix before sending

**1. The royalty is charged on the whole product, not the battery.** Clause 3 charges 5%, 7.5% or 10% of net sales of "defence products". If Psalm 144 sells a GBP 50,000 drone with a GBP 2,000 battery pack in it, he is owed up to GBP 5,000 on the whole drone, not GBP 200 on the pack.
*Suggested wording:* "The royalty is calculated on the net sales value of the cells and power packs. Where they are sold as part of a larger product, it is calculated on Grylls's list price for the power pack on its own."

## Worth considering

**2. The royalty rates are high.** You pay 7.5% when you use another manufacturer and 10% on the three existing contacts, so choosing someone else costs you more. That is fair to Trevor, because he loses his manufacturing margin, and it will help him sign. Just check you are happy with 10% on BlueBear, Swift and QinetiQ.

**3. "Defence contractor" is wide.** Anything supplied to a defence contractor counts, and most large aerospace firms are defence contractors. This is where Trevor is most likely to push back, because it could catch his civil aviation sales. If he raises it, the easy concession is "for defence or military use".

**4. What if he ends it after a quiet year?** Clause 8 lets him end "the exclusivity", but does not say whether you can keep making products you already sell. One sentence fixes it: "Grylls may continue to make and sell products already developed or under contract, paying the royalty."

## Fine as it is

The 20% non-circumvention payment (more enforceable than the full deal value), five years' confidentiality, names and announcements, and the one-year minimum.
